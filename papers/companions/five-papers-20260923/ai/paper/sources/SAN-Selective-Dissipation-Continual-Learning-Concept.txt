Selective Dissipation for Continual Learning:
Receiver-Relative Residuals and Self-Regulated Plasticity in Self-Aware Networks

Working concept note for Micah Blumberg's SAN research program.
Dated September 14, 2026.
Status: Proposed AI mechanism and experimental specification. No new model training or biological-data analysis is reported in this note. The proposal is not a claim that continual learning has been solved, that SAN invented its component algorithms, or that the resulting system would be conscious.

Proposed abstract

A deployed learner should remain capable of acquiring new knowledge without treating every observation as a reason to rewrite established competence. We propose a SAN-inspired continual-learning mechanism in which history-conditioned receiving dynamics resolve information already explained by the model, while unresolved relations remain available for further inference and selectively gated learning. The architecture distinguishes transient activity, retained eligibility, reversible parameter changes, and slower consolidation. A learned update controller uses the trajectory of receiver-relative residuals, independently observed consequences, and measured interference to select the location and timescale of modification. The central hypothesis is that receiver-conditioned settling behavior supplies useful information for allocating computation and plasticity beyond instantaneous loss, surprise, and input novelty. Controlled streaming tasks would test retained competence, acquisition of new relationships, correction of familiar but inaccurate predictions, rejection of unlearnable noise, and preservation of the capacity to learn. The proposed work evaluates an engineering interpretation of SAN; it does not equate suppression of residual activity with deletion of knowledge or infer a biological mechanism from artificial performance.

The proposed contribution

The user's formulation is that a learned network increasingly regulates its own further modification. Its dissipative operation computes away what is already known so that new relevant differences can deepen the existing structure of knowledge. This note operationalizes that formulation rather than treating it as an already validated learning algorithm.

The important distinction is between a known capability and the transient discrepancy involved in using it. Resolving a discrepancy should not delete the capability. A familiar relation can produce a useful response while producing little additional teaching signal. A newly meaningful relationship between familiar components can require learning even when neither component is novel.

The proposed SAN contribution is a causal connection between history-shaped receiving dynamics and selective modification. Learned organization should influence which incoming differences settle through existing computation, which need further evidence, and which produce a justified change in the organization itself. The controller should then learn from the measured consequences of its decisions. A static novelty threshold is an initial comparator, not the complete proposed mechanism.

Existing SAN foundations

The first SAN book, Bridging Molecular Mechanisms and Neural Oscillatory Dynamics, already connects predictive comparison, suppression of redundant information, pattern stabilization, and preservation of established structure while learning. Particularly relevant locations in the uploaded review-copy text are Cycle 12, around extracted lines 521-539; Cycle 13, around lines 547-567; and Cycle 22, especially the signal-discrimination formulation at line 922. Cycles 34 and 35 supply the existing BTSP-to-PWD and behavioral-learning bridge. These are historical SAN formulations, not independent empirical validation of their complete biological conjunction.

The saved SAN-Cycle proposal already specifies a persistent tonic canvas, receiver-relative differentials, reversible adapters, episodic memory, multiple consolidation timescales, matched candidate evaluation, and a residue-and-reset test. Its governing questions already include whether receiver-relative information adds to loss, novelty, gradient magnitude, or surprise. This proposal should therefore be developed as a focused mechanism paper within that existing program, or as a tightly scoped extension of the draft, rather than presented as the first SAN proposal for continual learning.

The archived SAN-Cycle validation record reports an executing scaffold and rollback tests, alongside mixed outcomes from a single toy seed. That record is not evidence of general superiority. The new selective-dissipation mechanism has not been tested by those old scaffold checks. Earlier adverse PWD benchmarks also remain separate adverse results; compatible literature cannot turn them into successes.

What 'compute away' means

For an encounter indexed by t, let k index the iterations of computation during that encounter. At an identified receiving module R, let r_R(t,k) be the discrepancy between an available observation and the reconstruction or prediction produced by the current system. The observed side of this comparison must not be silently replaced by the model's own preferred answer.

One proposed implementation uses a constrained iterative state update z_(k+1) = F(z_k, x_t, C_t, W_t). Here x_t is currently available evidence, C_t is the carried receiving context, and W_t is the retained parameter state. During a diagnostic settling interval, W_t is held fixed. The model must first attempt to explain the encounter with the competence it already has.

Dissipation means that a declared transient discrepancy or computational potential decreases through these dynamics. It is not a synonym for multiplying every activation by a decay factor. A procedure that forces every hidden state to zero would eliminate signals, not demonstrate understanding. Likewise, a sufficiently unconstrained reconstruction module might copy its input and make the measured residual disappear without learning a reusable relation. Withheld observations, future consequences, and constrained capacity are needed to reject that trivial solution.

An oscillatory implementation is possible but not required for the initial AI study. It would need actual phase or propagation dynamics and an ablation against an equally informed nonoscillatory implementation. A digital recurrent relaxation rule should not be called physical electromagnetic dissipation. The biological and artificial claims remain distinguishable.

The mechanism must also distinguish smaller residuals from faster relaxation. A learned prediction can reduce the initial amplitude while leaving a normalized decay profile unchanged. Measuring only time above a fixed threshold would confuse these effects. Report initial discrepancy, normalized time course, residual reactivation, and the amount of actual computation performed separately.

Representation and outcome must not be confused

A representation residual asks whether existing representations account for the current observation. An outcome residual asks whether the current system predicts or produces the correct consequence. Either can be nonzero without the other.

A familiar command can acquire a new meaning after a software update. Its syntax may produce almost no input novelty while its expected consequence is wrong. Similarly, a new association between familiar objects can require a new relationship without requiring a new feature dimension. A learner that updates only outside the span of familiar features will fail these cases.

A large persistent residual can also be irreducible noise, corrupted evidence, an incorrect source, or a mismatch caused by the wrong active context. These conditions should lead to different actions: seek evidence, change a route, revise a model, retain a scoped exception, or decline a permanent update. They must not all produce indiscriminate parameter growth.

Repeated independent evidence is not necessarily redundant. It can improve calibration or reveal a changed frequency of events. The test should distinguish duplicated observations from additional informative observations, and familiarity from confidence in an uncertain estimate.

A candidate update rule

A practical first implementation can use ordinary gradients to form candidate updates while using SAN's proposed receiving dynamics to control their allocation. Let W_R be the parameters of a reversible receiving module. One candidate rule is Delta W_R = -eta * g_R * (M_R elementwise grad_W_R L_verified).

Here eta is a bounded step size. The scalar g_R is a learned proposal gate between zero and one. The mask M_R has the same shape as W_R and selects or weights eligible parameter locations. L_verified is an explicitly specified objective grounded in permitted observations, delayed task labels, subsequent measurements, or an independently assessed action consequence. It is not the model's unsupported assertion that its answer is correct.

The proposed inputs to g_R and M_R include the receiver-relative residual trajectory, independently measured uncertainty, the active route, historical usefulness of similar updates, and estimated interference with retained capabilities. The controller should also distinguish inference-state changes from durable parameter changes. A candidate that merely chooses an existing module is not the same event as one that changes that module's weights.

The novelty of this proposal cannot reside in multiplying a gradient by a mask. Masked, gated, selective, and learned updates already exist. The specific question is whether the measured history-conditioned settling trajectory predicts where additional learning will help, beyond the scalar information supplied by instantaneous loss, gradient magnitude, or prediction confidence.

A learned proposal policy and an acceptance test should remain separate. The proposal policy decides whether an expensive candidate update is worth trying. The acceptance test compares the bounded candidate with the unchanged system on matched new-evidence and retention conditions. Repeated use of one acceptance set can overfit that set, so later independent evaluation and rotating validation evidence are required.

An explicit controller objective could be future task loss plus a weighted penalty for regression on protected capabilities, update cost, and extra capacity. Controller training must use development streams; the final stream must not be used to tune the policy or thresholds. Negative proposal outcomes are useful training examples for the controller, but the controller's own modifications should also be versioned and bounded.

Retained changes should be tested after ordinary working state is cleared. Selective removal of the candidate adapter or other proposed memory should remove its predicted benefit under matched conditions. Resetting only temporary activations and observing that a benefit remains helps locate the residue. A complete reset must include relevant optimizer state when that state can affect later updates.

Extending weights versus extending knowledge

Weight updates, new feature allocation, changed routing, and an external memory entry are different operations. The first prototype should use a fixed budget so that improved performance cannot be attributed merely to accumulating more parameters. A later controlled-growth arm can compare reuse, small adapter allocation, merging, and retirement under the same total memory and compute limits as its comparators.

The knowledge-tree intuition is best tested through reusable dependencies: familiar components should support new combinations and new rules should transfer to previously unseen combinations. The stored structure can be graph-like rather than a literal tree because a new concept may depend on several older concepts. Matrix coefficients should not be asserted to be inspectable symbolic branches unless an implementation provides that structure.

Learned suppression should reduce unnecessary writes, not permanently freeze established representations. The system needs to correct old mistakes, respond to environmental change, and recover learning capacity. Finite storage cannot preserve an unlimited sequence of independent facts with unlimited precision. The practical goal is ongoing selective acquisition, compression, revision, and useful retention under stated resource bounds.

Closest comparison methods

EATA already selects reliable, nonredundant test examples and regularizes important weights. It is a direct comparator for the claim that redundant input can be excluded from expensive adaptation.

TTT layers make hidden state itself a model updated through self-supervised learning. Titans adds test-time neural memory with surprise-related updates and forgetting. Gated DeltaNet combines adaptive gating with targeted delta-rule modifications. These are direct comparators for changing a vector or matrix memory online; such memory updates should not automatically be equated with lasting changes in the deployed shared model.

SEAL learns to produce finetuning material and update directives, using downstream adaptation outcomes as feedback. Nested Learning and Hope explicitly study nested learning processes, multiple update frequencies, and self-modifying memory. They are close comparators for a system that regulates how it changes. Their existence does not settle the SAN hypothesis, but it prevents a broad claim that learned update control or multiple timescales is itself a new contribution.

Gradient Projection Memory provides a precedent for protecting previously useful directions. Adaptive resonance architectures provide an older precedent for stable online categorization and matching-versus-resetting. Equilibrium propagation provides an established route from recurrent settling to parameter learning. The proposed comparison must engage these mechanisms rather than compare only against naive sequential finetuning.

Dohare and colleagues distinguish loss of plasticity from forgetting. Their experiments motivate measuring the learner's future ability to acquire another task, not only its ability to retain old answers. Increasingly closed learning gates could create a well-preserved but inflexible network; that would contradict the goal of training that remains available after deployment.

First decisive study

Use a continuing stream in which the same primitives participate in different compositions and contexts. Include already mastered encounters, new relations made from familiar primitives, genuine corrections to familiar predictions, context switches that later recur, irrelevant random variation, and repeated independent evidence. Do not give the model hidden task identities or the experimenter's known-versus-new label.

Evaluate each episode before permitting its update. Keep ordinary inference adaptation separate from retained post-episode parameter learning. Supply the same permitted supervision or delayed outcome channel to every model. Use held-out compositions to measure transfer, not just memorization of the online stream.

The primary question is whether residual-trajectory-controlled plasticity yields lower future error at a matched retention constraint and resource budget than the strongest comparison controller. Report actual forward and backward computation, retained and temporary memory, parameter growth, candidate-evaluation overhead, and wall-clock time. Fewer nonzero activations do not establish a speed benefit when the same dense matrix operations still run.

The most important ablation replaces the residual-trajectory gate with a scalar-loss or gradient-surprise gate while keeping the backbone, candidate-update substrate, replay, acceptance tests, and total budget the same. An equally informed flexible gate should receive the same allowed history. Further controls remove carried receiving state, scramble historical ordering where valid, or replace recurrence with a matched feedforward computation.

A claim about settling requires measured settling differences, not a rule that explicitly assigns faster decay to examples labeled familiar by the experimenter. A claim about selective learning requires locating the retained change, not merely a lower final loss. A claim about continual competence requires old-task revisits and future-task learning, not one new task followed by a single evaluation.

The desired signature is that established relations retain competent readout with fewer unnecessary updates; new useful relations yield selective retained changes; verified corrections remain learnable even for familiar inputs; irrelevant noise does not monopolize capacity; and successful acquisition changes the next encounter's processing in the predicted direction. Failure of any component should be reported separately.

Deployment scope

A deployed learner can remain continually capable of adaptation without rewriting the shared core on every interaction. Initial changes can stay in bounded, reversible, user- or organization-scoped modules. Source permissions, provenance, poisoning resistance, deletion obligations, and rollback are requirements of the engineered system. A local adaptation success does not authorize global promotion. A self-regulating controller does not need permission to rewrite the system's external objectives or safety boundaries.

Paper boundary and next deliverable

A focused methods paper is justified if it supplies an executable receiver-conditioned residual mechanism, an explicit update policy, and comparisons that isolate the contribution of settling dynamics. Without those experiments, the honest product is a theory and protocol paper. Biological experiments motivate the decomposition but do not prove artificial performance. Artificial success would not by itself validate biological PWD, full NAPOT, or a theory of experience.

The immediate implementation target is a fixed-capacity continual learner that learns where to change from its own measured processing and independently scored consequences. It should be able to recognize a familiar situation, correct a familiar error, learn a new relation between old components, and demonstrate exactly which retained state caused each improvement.
