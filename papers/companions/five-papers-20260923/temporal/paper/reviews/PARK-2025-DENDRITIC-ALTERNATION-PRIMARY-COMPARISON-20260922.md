# Park et al. 2025: measured dendritic alternation is a stronger comparator

22 September 2026. Primary source: [Park et al., *Nature Communications* 16:1333 (2025)](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-025-55819-9). This is a source-to-claim review, not a reproduction or a transfer of mouse CA1 results to every neuron.

Park and colleagues used high-speed dendritic voltage imaging, targeted optogenetic stimulation, pharmacology and a conductance-based model in acute mouse hippocampal CA1 slices. They report a transient window in which back-propagating somatic action potentials elicited distal dendritic spikes. In a pooled step-stimulation analysis, dendritic spikes were initially absent and then occurred in a window that ended on average around 83 ms after stimulus onset; the relevant sample was 43 stimulated branch locations in 16 cells from 13 animals. Under a stronger wide-field condition they observed alternating dendritic-spike successes and failures—period doubling—in nine cells from nine animals, followed by eventual failures. Their mechanistic interpretation involves A-type potassium-channel inactivation opening a window and slow dendritic sodium-channel inactivation closing it. Their lower-dimensional and conductance-based simulations reproduce some measured motifs. The published [methods and source-data routes](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-025-55819-9#data-availability) identify DANDI accession 001266 and provided supplementary code.

This is **direct prior art for history-dependent dendritic readiness and alternating success/failure**, stronger than a purely theoretical refractory analogy. The numbers above are stimulation-relative event windows, **not** independent measurements of this paper's hard-window recovery parameter `R` or retained-content lifetime `M`; plugging them into Proposition 1 would be a category error. The measured period doubling is an alternating *propagation outcome* in a dendritic tree under a specific drive, not sequential independent branch contributions encoding A, B and C, and not evidence of a temporally experiencing cell.

There is also a substantive route constraint. In the tested optogenetic regime, Park et al. report broad distal dendritic-spike patterns and no observed dSpikes initiated at the stimulated branch; the initial local depolarization was instead diffusive. Thus a three-branch model cannot be justified by assuming that each input branch always launches a spatially confined regenerative event. A serious successor must declare cell type, stimulus regime, local versus global event identity, soma/bAP return, recovered branch readiness, content retention and downstream readout separately.

| New paper proposition | Park et al. relation | What is still missing |
|---|---|---|
| Recent activity changes dendritic readiness | Direct physiological precedent in one preparation | A branch-resolved parameterization for the exact candidate schedule. |
| Activity may alternate | Direct precedent for success/failure period doubling under wide-field drive | Three different branches taking turns, or content-bearing ordered assembly. |
| Earlier and later events can jointly matter | Their dendritic-synaptic collisions generate plateau potentials under tested stimulation | A declared receiver that preserves the order and ownership of distinct event contents. |
| Temporal organization contributes to experience | No experience measurement in the source | Separate network-level and cellular-phenomenal arguments and tests. |

Next analysis should first identify a bounded subset of the public DANDI/supplementary records that actually tracks branch identity, input order, spikes and recovery in the same preparation, inspect its license and size, and predeclare a source-specific extraction. Do **not** download the whole archive or treat a simulation trace as a biological replicate. Compare the candidate model against Park et al.'s mechanistic channel account and a nonoscillatory state-dependent memory model at matched input and output access. An imposed three-way cycle is not a scientific rival to these results.
