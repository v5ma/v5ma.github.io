# Additional review for the v1.1 release

18 October 2026. This is an AI-assisted author-workflow review, not independent scientific peer review. It covers the revised main arguments, the added Hesse comparison, conditional mathematics and protocol, publication links and source/figure preservation. It does not claim a fresh full-methods re-review of all 59 cited studies or a new execution of the older numerical fits.

## Findings fixed

1. **Prospective versus executed work.** Learning to Feel's older wording that Section 6 “tests” the joint claim could imply an executed experiment. It now says that the section proposes a test; the phase-specific hypothesis is described as developed, not biologically tested.
2. **Randomization-order ambiguity.** The new state-stratified perturbation was insufficiently distinguished from the primary pre-eligibility scheduling-policy randomization. The revised section separates an optional nested perturbation from that primary design and names its conditional target population.
3. **Pre-probe is not pre-learning.** A state measured before a randomized probe can still be caused by acquisition. Appendix N now explicitly limits its conditional randomization identity to the probe intervention; it does not identify acquisition effects by conditioning on a post-acquisition state. Repeated-probe history/carryover and participant/yoke-set inference remain explicit.
4. **Post-treatment adjustment.** “Beyond measured encoding and occupancy” could encourage adjusting away intervention consequences. The text now distinguishes primary randomized total effects from secondary joint/mechanistic analyses and does not claim that controlling measured mediators identifies a direct causal effect.
5. **Missing aggregation connection.** Temporal Ensembles now links the new content-selection discussion to its existing aggregation counterexample. A common gain on a pooled trace does not escape that equivalence; a content/state-specific receiving rule needs independently identified information and a comparably informed nonoscillatory control. Supplying a selector does not explain how it was learned.
6. **Mean rate versus observed spikes.** Appendix N's Poisson example now says that the total conditional mean rate is four, not that noisy observed spike counts stay fixed.
7. **Version-specific cross-citations.** The two papers cite the new counterpart version where the successor's arguments are used, with links to the preserved v1.0 records. In particular the supplement no longer points only to Temporal Ensembles v1.0 for results added in v1.1.
8. **Artifact availability versus access.** Links to omitted protocols, runtime records and verifiers are explicitly labelled availability notices. The public release does not imply that excluded publisher-derived code or the complete private replay archive can be downloaded.
9. **Current versus historical checks.** Statements about earlier complete execution/audit results are marked as recorded historical results. New release checks do not imply that all historical runtimes or biological studies were rerun.
10. **Abstract and version clarity.** The abstracts identify the state-selection refinement and its limits. Local draft labels are replaced only in the new release candidates, never in v1.0.

## Important conclusions retained

The Hesse preprint is a positive empirical comparison for temporally organized content, not proof of dendritic triplets or a global clock. Its split-neuron controls are substantive and are not dismissed. Fine image reconstruction, the paired-response state classifier, offline HMM inference, mean rate, tuning gain and independently measured phase remain distinct. The source's mean-dwell/autocorrelation convention is retained rather than importing a 60-ms SAN parameter.

The new covariance, Markov-switching and trace-gap arguments are elementary conditional results; no error was found in their stated algebra. The RCT identity is correct under its assumptions, but its experimental scope needed the clarification above. The seven arithmetic fixtures and all inherited adverse comparisons remain unchanged. Neither proof arithmetic nor publication establishes conscious experience.
