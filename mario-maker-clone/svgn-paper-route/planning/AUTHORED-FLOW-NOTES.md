# Authored flow, not surface count

This pass develops the first chapter as authored routes rather than scattering or multiplying geometry. The 15 road pieces are designed as a rising runway, a Clocktower curl/drop, a Canal decision, a Bellflower open hook that reverses horizontal movement, a returning cradle, and a Festival descent. The lower Canal sprint is shorter; the longer upper line has more geometry to explore and more pickup opportunities. Three ground entrances have declared approach windows. A miss can return to the existing populated road. Mail remains optional and the finish still advances the campaign.

The graph is stateful. A transition starts with an actual landing state, including speed, direction and position along a curve. No endpoint-proximity link or isolated reset trial is accepted as proof of a complete route. The primary upper and lower sequences carry state through all their pieces. Their modeled catch projections retain at least 90% of incoming velocity along the receiving surface; the rail engine's existing speed cap still applies on subsequent ticks. That percentage is not conservation of total energy and is not a measured frame rate.

The route grammar contains straight approaches, constant-radius tangent-continuous arcs and a cubic final descent. The first Clocktower curl has a physically open mouth: shortening its obsolete lead exposed the space needed for its departing rider. The Bellflower hook was rebuilt as a partial turn with a separate leftward catch and return, instead of an inward spiral whose apparent exit passed through its own road body. The lower terminal catcher was replaced by a longer descending/ascending swoop rather than forcing a late perpendicular landing. These are changes to actual geometry, not invisible speed resets.

Road-body clearance is checked independently of the game's one-way attachment line. The rehearsal uses a 20px disk bounding the 26x30 rider and the extruded roadway's 34px body, with capped segments. Live gameplay on this authored course gains the same swept contact test. A missed approach is gently deflected from the solid back/edge, without attaching or moving the rider to a target. Positive intended routes require zero body contacts. Perturbed misses may legitimately bounce and then return to the street; these outcomes are reported separately from clean, successful high transfers.

The expert bell-buoy branch has one nominal clean upper reconnection. A varied cast/release sample is not called uniformly successful: some attempts recover onto the street after a physical bump. This remains a mastery route, not a beginner requirement. Do not relabel all recoveries as successful peg-to-rail transfers. Model tests do not establish that it feels good to an unbriefed player.

Flow Coach extends the existing merged Route Workshop; it does not replace it. Its carried-state trace is read-only, is invalidated by an edit, and exports a model-only report. Actual 3D Playtest and its save isolation remain the editor's separate acceptance gate. The later chapters and all old expert course identities remain unchanged in this iteration. Editing a saved old level does not silently replace its geometry.

The release gates are: deterministic complete-route rehearsal, multi-speed/entry timing cases, independent spacing and body-clearance checks, actual native HTTP/3D road/upper/lower/whip runs, editor mutation/export/undo checks, visual inspection, and a public-file match after merge. Human judgments of enjoyable flow, route readability, repeated play and controller feel stay open until tested by people. No number of green assertions closes that gate.

Design reference: Smith, Whitehead and Mateas, Tanagra: A Mixed-Initiative Level Design Tool (2010), https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AIIDE/article/view/12379 . Its use of movement models and geometry constraints informs the separation of route intent from constraint validation; this implementation is not Tanagra and does not claim equivalent guarantees.
