# Prismatic materials: the actual game, not concept art

The user requested glass, diamonds, jewels, polished metal, translucency and shader effects informed by modern browser-graphics communities. This revision stays in the existing pinned Three r177 WebGPURenderer and uses physical node materials and original TSL shader expressions. It does not import a second engine, change a dependency version, reproduce franchise assets, or replace the game with a showroom. PlayCanvas, Babylon and A-Frame are useful references, not additional runtimes loaded into this side-scroller. No new WebXR session, controller mapping or headset mode is claimed; immersive camera comfort and stereo transparency need their own validation.

Opaque collision-backed rails retain their exact geometry. The road finish becomes sapphire enamel with clearcoat and thin-film iridescence; its edging becomes reflective champagne-gold metal. A restrained animated light strip sits within the fascia, away from the riding surface. The same courier model, mailboxes and existing pickup positions receive material accents. Faceted envelope seals preserve the recognizable paper artwork and disappear when their actual tile is collected. Real pegs gain a jewel accent without moving their target centers or their gameplay radius.

At most six glass-and-metal conservatories decorate the background. They are not collision surfaces or additional rewards. The first is visible near the opening neighborhood; upper ones sit behind the existing route composition. Original faceted crystal geometry, reflection lighting and procedural light filigree provide specular detail without remote image assets. Filigree is a caustic-style shader pattern, not physically computed caustics.

Prismatic is the default lighter preset: tinted alpha glass with clearcoat/environment reflection. Crystal+ deliberately opts into scene transmission, IOR, thickness, attenuation and spectral dispersion. It has a higher GPU cost. Classic restores original material objects and removes the added visual layer. There is no full-screen bloom, motion blur, camera shake or strobing flash. Bounded additive particles react to actual rail catches, whip contact/release and nitro use; a Fresnel shell follows an actually acquired shield. Their randomness is private and cannot consume the gameplay random stream. Motion-reduction preferences disable moving accents; pause freezes the shader clock with the simulation.

All particles, material objects, textures and buffers are owned by this pass. A fixed budget of 112 particles, 40 visible pickup jewels, 16 peg accents and 6 pavilions limits additions. Mode changes remove previous layers and restore original materials. Dynamic gem/spark batches are first submitted nonempty, following the native renderer lesson learned from the earlier invisible tether. The physical road, first-chapter recipes, ground-only completion, bonus accounting, editor documents, soundtrack and save namespaces remain unchanged. Preferences use a separate localStorage key. An optional material-module download failure must not block campaign or Workshop access.

Initial native run 34392030722 passed the actual full upper ride and all initial material/classic/refraction switches, motion settings, scene ownership and document-preservation checks. Visual inspection showed the first glass exhibits were too far off the opening view and the metallic edging needed more legible gold, so the finishing pass changes placement, half-dome ribs and color balance, not gameplay. Current acceptance must verify the final exact source and inspect its images; initial screenshots alone are not a final-release receipt.

The continuing native matrix covers real material changes, a complete high route, a real Cloudpost whip ride, nitro top/underside/top practice, a shield pickup and whole ground run, editor gestures/files, Ride Lab, First Flight and Hookline. Dedicated fault injection tests fail only optional module delivery and check preferences on reload. Every test controls the app through ordinary keys/UI; calculations and unit fixtures are reported separately. Chromium software WebGL is not native-WebGPU or physical-device performance certification.

Technical primary references: https://threejs.org/docs/pages/MeshPhysicalMaterial.html ; https://threejs.org/docs/pages/MeshPhysicalNodeMaterial.html ; https://threejs.org/manual/en/webgpurenderer.html ; pinned implementation under vendor/three.webgpu.js . Concepts used are clearcoat, environment reflection, transmission, iridescence, dispersion and node-authored shaders. No external shader code was copied into the app.
