2. Christopher Hitchens
Background: A journalist, literary critic, and author of God Is Not Great: How Religion Poisons Everything.

Key Arguments:

Morality Without Religion: Hitchens argued that morality can and does exist independently of religious belief and that religions often propagate immoral actions (e.g., crusades, witch hunts).

Religion as Man-Made: He highlighted inconsistencies and human flaws in holy texts as evidence that religions are human inventions.

Faith as Subjugation: Religion, he argued, often demands submission and discourages critical thinking, which hinders human progress.

Religion and Violence: Hitchens pointed out the historical and ongoing role of religion in conflicts and atrocities.

3. Sam Harris
Background: A neuroscientist, philosopher, and author of The End of Faith and Letter to a Christian Nation.

Key Arguments:

The Danger of Dogma: Harris argued that religious dogma leads to irrational beliefs and harmful practices, such as opposition to science and medicine.

Secular Morality: He advocated for a science-based approach to ethics, suggesting that human well-being can be objectively understood and improved without religion.

Critique of Islam: Harris frequently criticized Islam, arguing that its doctrines are especially prone to extremism in modern contexts.

Faith as Intellectual Laziness: Harris viewed faith as an excuse to stop questioning and seeking evidence.

Background: A philosopher and cognitive scientist, author of Breaking the Spell: Religion as a Natural Phenomenon.

Cultural Evolution of Religion: He proposed that religions evolved to solve social cohesion problems but are no longer necessary in modern society.

Faith as a Memetic Virus: Dennett suggested that religious ideas propagate like viruses, spreading through human culture by exploiting cognitive biases.

Encouraging Open Inquiry: Dennett urged believers to critically examine their faith rather than accept religious dogma uncritically.

Key Collaborative Themes

Faith lacks evidence: They argued that belief in God and religious doctrines often rely on faith rather than empirical evidence.

Religion causes harm: Many of their critiques focused on the negative effects of religion, from wars to repression of science and human rights.
