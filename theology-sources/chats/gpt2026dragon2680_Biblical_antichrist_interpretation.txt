- The key phrase “שָׁב֣וּעַ אֶחָ֑ד” (shăbûaʿ eḥād) literally “one seven/one heptad” is somewhat ambiguous: the word **שָׁב֣וּעַ** can mean “week” (as in 7 days) in later Hebrew, and in this prophetic context it is often taken to mean “seven years” (a heptad of years). ## 3. Comparison: Which is “older”?  
- Since both Hebrew and Aramaic derive from common Northwest Semitic roots, neither is strictly “older” in the sense of one being the parent of the other—they share ancestry.  
- In terms of **earliest attested written inscriptions**, Aramaic inscriptions from the 10th-9th centuries BCE are among the earliest solid evidence. citeturn0search14  
- Hebrew has earlier origins as a spoken idiom, but the earliest securely datable Hebrew inscriptions are less plentiful and a bit later in terms of wide attestation.

So one could say Aramaic has one of the **earliest written attestations** among Northwest Semitic languages, but that does *not* mean it is “much older” than Hebrew in all senses.

---

## 4. What about Cuneiform?  
- Cuneiform is a **script/system** (not a single language) used for languages like Sumerian, Akkadian, Hittite, etc., and it dates back to the **late 4th millennium BCE** (~c. 3400–3000 BCE) in Mesopotamia.  
- So yes—cuneiform in its earliest use is much older than both Hebrew and Aramaic.  
- Aramaic is not “nearly as old” as cuneiform in the sense of originating at the same time; its earliest inscriptions are thousands of years later.

---

## 5. Summary & nuance  
- **Aramaic**: Emerges clearly as a written language in the 10th-9th centuries BCE, becomes very influential by the 7th-6th centuries BCE.  
- **Hebrew**: Very ancient, but the surviving early inscriptions and literary attestations are less abundant; Hebrew’s use is continuous but its vernacular phase shifts.  
- **Cuneiform**: Much older—both semantically and historically—than either Aramaic or Hebrew.  
- Because Aramaic and Hebrew are siblings rather than one parent/child, it’s imprecise to assert “Aramaic is much older than Hebrew” without caveats.
