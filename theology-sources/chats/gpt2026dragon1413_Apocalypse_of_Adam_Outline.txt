### **1. Nag Hammadi Hermeticism: Mystical and Gnostic**

The main Hermetic texts in the **Nag Hammadi Library** are:

- **The Discourse on the Eighth and Ninth**
- **The Prayer of Thanksgiving**
- **The Asclepius 21–29 fragment**

These texts reflect what scholars call **“philosophical Hermeticism”** or **“religious Hermeticism”**—they are:

- **Deeply introspective**: Focused on inward ascent, illumination, and mystical rebirth.
- **Cosmic and dualistic**: The soul must ascend past **archons and planetary spheres**, just like in Gnostic cosmology.
- **Anti-materialist**: The body is a tomb (sōma sēma), and true knowledge frees the soul from the physical world.
- **Soteriological**: Emphasis on **gnosis as salvation**, very close to Sethian or Valentinian Gnosticism.
- **Less ritualistic or magical**: There's **no alchemy or spellcraft**, only meditative ascent and divine contemplation.

This suggests that **early Hermeticists and Gnostics shared a religious-philosophical world**, especially in **Roman Egypt**, likely exchanging ideas freely.

---

### **2. Renaissance Hermeticism: Magical and Neoplatonic**

The **Corpus Hermeticum** as it was known in the **Renaissance** came from **Byzantine Greek manuscripts**, translated by **Marsilio Ficino in 1463**.

This later version of Hermeticism evolved into:

- **Ritual magic and alchemy**: Emphasis on **correspondences, planetary influences, and magical ascent**.
- **Unity with the cosmos**: Less emphasis on *escaping* the world, more on **mastering it spiritually**.
- **Less dualism**: The material world is not always seen as evil or created by a lesser god.
- **Integration with Christianity**: Hermeticism was rebranded as a **"Prisca Theologia"**, an ancient wisdom tradition that prefigured Christianity rather than opposing it.

This shift allowed Hermeticism to **survive under Christian rule**, but it lost much of the **radical anti-cosmism and mythic rebellion** found in the Gnostic Hermetic texts.

---

### **3. Why Were the Nag Hammadi Hermetic Texts More Gnostic?**

- **Time and place**: These were written during the **2nd–4th centuries CE**, in a melting pot of **Alexandrian syncretism**, where Gnosticism, Neoplatonism, Judaism, Christianity, and Hermeticism all coexisted.
- **Shared cosmology**: Both Gnostics and Hermeticists believed in:
  - A transcendent unknowable God.
  - A hierarchy of cosmic beings (Aeons, archons, daemons).
  - The soul’s divine origin and its imprisonment in matter.
  - Liberation through inner revelation and ascent.

So yes—**Hermeticism was more aligned with Gnosticism back then**. Later, it merged with **Neoplatonism and Christian mysticism**, losing much of its original apocalyptic and anti-cosmic tone.
