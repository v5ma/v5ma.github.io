Micah Blumberg:
How many different early Christian/Gnostic groups were there?

Self Aware Networks GPT:
There were many distinct early Christian and Gnostic groups, movements, and sects, each with unique beliefs, teachings, and practices. During the 1st to 4th centuries CE, Christianity wasn't unified or monolithic—it existed as a diverse ecosystem of communities exploring radically different interpretations of Christ's message.

Here are **key early Christian/Gnostic groups** and their basic characteristics:

## ① **Valentinians (Valentinianism)**
- **Founder:** Valentinus (~100–160 CE)
- **Beliefs:** Sophisticated cosmology with aeons, emanations from the divine pleroma (fullness), and salvation through gnosis (direct knowledge).
- **Texts:** Gospel of Truth, Tripartite Tractate.

## ② **Sethians (Classic Gnostics)**
- **Beliefs:** Emphasis on Seth, third son of Adam and Eve, as spiritual ancestor. The world is seen as created by a false deity (demiurge).
- **Texts:** Secret Book of John, Gospel of Judas, Apocryphon of John.

## ③ **Thomasine Christians**
- **Beliefs:** Followers of teachings attributed to the Apostle Thomas, emphasizing self-discovery, enlightenment, and direct experiential gnosis.
- **Texts:** Gospel of Thomas, Book of Thomas the Contender.

## ④ **Marcionites**
- **Founder:** Marcion of Sinope (~85–160 CE)
- **Beliefs:** Radical dualism separating the Old Testament God (Demiurge) from the merciful God of Jesus. Accepted Paul and Luke’s gospel exclusively.
- **Texts:** Marcionite Gospel (modified Luke), selected Pauline letters.

## ⑤ **Carpocratians**
- **Founder:** Carpocrates (~2nd century CE), later represented by Marcellina in Rome.
- **Beliefs:** Soul reincarnation, spiritual equality, liberation through experiencing all things; some accusations of libertinism by opponents.
- **Texts:** No surviving full texts, fragments in writings of church fathers.

## ⑥ **Basilideans**
- **Founder:** Basilides (early 2nd century CE)
- **Beliefs:** Complex cosmology, the illusion of Christ's crucifixion, and knowledge as the means to liberation from ignorance.
- **Texts:** Fragments recorded by church fathers; possibly influenced the Gospel of Judas.

## ⑦ **Ophites (Serpent Gnostics)**
- **Beliefs:** Revered the serpent in Genesis as a symbol of wisdom or gnosis, opposed the creator-god as an evil or ignorant deity.
- **Texts:** Mentioned by early Church Fathers, no independent texts clearly survive.

## ⑧ **Cainites**
- **Beliefs:** Honored biblical figures traditionally considered villains (Cain, Judas, etc.) as heroes opposing the false God (Demiurge).
- **Texts:** Gospel of Judas possibly reflects some Cainite ideas.

## ⑨ **Ebionites**
- **Beliefs:** Jewish-Christian community emphasizing adherence to Jewish law, viewed Jesus primarily as Messiah and prophet, not divine.
- **Texts:** Gospel of the Ebionites (fragmentary references).

## ⑩ **Nazarenes**
- **Beliefs:** Jewish-Christian sect maintaining strong Jewish identity, Torah observance combined with belief in Jesus as Messiah.
- **Texts:** Gospel of the Nazarenes (lost, known through references).

## ⑪ **Encratites**
- **Founder:** Tatian (disciple of Justin Martyr)
- **Beliefs:** Asceticism, sexual abstinence, rejection of wine and meat.
- **Texts:** Tatian’s Diatessaron (harmonization of four canonical gospels).

## ⑫ **Montanists (New Prophecy)**
- **Founder:** Montanus (~170 CE), prophetess Prisca and Maximilla.
- **Beliefs:** Emphasis on prophecy, charismatic revelation, and apocalyptic expectation. Initially mainstream, became heretical due to extremism.
- **Texts:** Oracles, prophecies—known through citations by church historians.

## ⑬ **Manichaeans**
- **Founder:** Mani (216–274 CE)
- **Beliefs:** Dualistic religion combining Gnostic Christian elements with Zoroastrianism, Buddhism, and Hinduism. Salvation through knowledge and ascetic practice.
- **Texts:** Manichaean scriptures like the Living Gospel, Book of Giants, and the Kephalaia.

## Other lesser-known groups:
- **Barbeloites**
- **Borborites**
- **Phibionites**
- **Simonians (followers of Simon Magus)**
- **Cerinthians (followers of Cerinthus)**
- **Docetists (belief that Christ’s body was illusory)**

It's difficult to determine an exact number due to incomplete historical records, deliberate suppression by orthodox authorities, and overlap between groups. But scholarly estimates typically indicate there were **at least two dozen distinct groups** with variations and subsects, possibly many more.
