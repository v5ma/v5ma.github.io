### 1. **Paul Being Dated to the 2nd Century?**

Mainstream scholarship places Paul’s letters between **48–64 CE**, with most scholars dating his authentic epistles (like Romans, 1 Corinthians, Galatians, etc.) to the **50s CE**. These are the earliest surviving Christian writings and predate the Gospels.

However, **some mythicists and radical critics** have argued that Paul’s letters were either **forged in the 2nd century** or **heavily edited** by later Christians. Their reasons include:

- Theological similarities with later Gnostic or proto-Catholic debates.
- Alleged anachronisms or advanced theological development.
- Absence of detailed biographical information about Jesus (e.g. no mention of miracles, parables, Mary, Bethlehem, etc.).
  
These arguments are **rejected by most historians**, who consider the core Pauline epistles to be authentic mid-1st-century documents, partly because:

- They’re referenced by early 2nd-century sources like **Clement of Rome** (c. 96 CE) and **Ignatius of Antioch**.
- Their internal theological struggles match what would be expected from a new movement still in tension with Judaism and paganism.

---

### 2. **First-Century Extrabiblical Mentions of Jesus as Forgeries?**

There are a few **early non-Christian references** to Jesus, but some have contested authenticity:

- **Josephus (Jewish historian, c. 93 CE)**: In *Antiquities of the Jews*, there's a passage (Testimonium Flavianum) mentioning Jesus. Most scholars think it was **partially altered** by later Christian scribes, but **a core mention of Jesus is likely authentic**.
  
- **Tacitus (Roman historian, c. 116 CE)**: Mentions *Christus* who was executed under **Pontius Pilate**. This is generally considered **genuine** and independent.

- **Suetonius, Pliny the Younger**, and others: These are **brief and indirect**, often referring to *Christians* rather than Jesus directly.

Mythicists often argue these sources are either:

- **Later Christian interpolations**, or
- So vague they could refer to someone else.

Mainstream historians accept that while these references are limited and sometimes corrupted, **they support the idea that Jesus was a real person** known to followers early on.

---

### 3. **What Is Mythicism?**

**Jesus mythicism** is the belief that Jesus never existed as a historical person but was a mythological or celestial figure, possibly created through syncretism with dying-and-rising gods (like Osiris, Dionysus, or Mithras). Leading voices include:

- **Richard Carrier** (Ph.D. in ancient history): Argues Jesus began as a celestial being only revealed through visions (especially Paul), later historicized in the Gospels.
  
- **Earl Doherty**: Emphasized the lack of biographical detail in Paul and the cosmic Christ figure.

- **Robert Price**: A New Testament scholar who also advocates a mythical Jesus, influenced by comparative religion.

Critics of mythicism (Bart Ehrman, Maurice Casey, Larry Hurtado, etc.) argue that:

- The idea of a myth-only Jesus doesn't align with early Jewish thought.
- Paul's letters reflect belief in a real man who was crucified in living memory.
- Mythicists selectively use evidence and ignore social-historical context.
