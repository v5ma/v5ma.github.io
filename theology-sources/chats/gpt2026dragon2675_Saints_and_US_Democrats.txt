Self Aware Networks GPT:
Short answer: it depends what you mean by “saint.”

• New Testament/biblical usage (`hagios` = “holy ones”): all living Christians are “saints.” So, by this definition, there are millions of “modern saints” alive today—any baptized believer walking around right now.

• Roman Catholic & Eastern Orthodox (official/canonized sense): no. Canonization/glorification happens after death, so there are no officially recognized “living saints.” Living people admired for holiness may be informally called “saints,” but that’s not an official title. (On the Catholic track you’ll see “Servant of God” → “Venerable” → “Blessed” → “Saint”; these stages are normally post-mortem.)

• Most Protestant traditions: they keep the NT sense, so every living believer is a “saint,” not just a spiritual elite.

• Latter-day Saint (LDS) usage: “Saints” is the community’s self-name; all living members of the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints are “Latter-day Saints.”

• Colloquial speech: people sometimes call unusually compassionate living people “saints,” but that’s just a compliment, not a biblical or ecclesial status.
