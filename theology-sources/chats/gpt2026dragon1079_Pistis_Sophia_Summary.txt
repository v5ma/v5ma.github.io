*Pistis Sophia* ("Faith Wisdom") is a foundational text within Gnostic Christianity, notable for its complex cosmology, mystical dialogue, and teachings attributed to Jesus after his resurrection. Here's a structured outline summarizing its main sections and themes:

## General Overview
- **Nature of the Text**: A Gnostic scripture describing Jesus' teachings on spiritual ascent, redemption, cosmology, and the mysteries of the universe, primarily delivered in a post-resurrection dialogue between Jesus and his disciples.
- **Composition**: Generally dated between the late 2nd and early 4th century CE. Survives primarily in a Coptic manuscript.

---

## I. Introduction and Ascension of Jesus
- Jesus returns from the divine realms, dressed in radiant garments, to instruct his disciples.
- He reveals his journeys through heavenly spheres and describes his transcendence beyond the cosmic hierarchies.

## II. The Story of Pistis Sophia
- Pistis Sophia ("Faith Wisdom"), a central figure representing a fallen divine being, seeks redemption.
- Sophia is depicted as trapped by lower cosmic powers (archons), lamenting her plight through prayers and hymns.
- Jesus narrates Sophia's struggles, repentance, and ultimate salvation.

### A. The Fall of Sophia
- Sophia seeks the Light but is deceived and drawn into chaos.
- Sophia’s misguided desire to approach divine Light without proper authority leads to her fall.

### B. Sophia's Repentance and Prayers
- Sophia cries out through a series of lamentations (13 repentances), pleading for deliverance.
- Her repentances symbolically represent the soul’s spiritual awakening and striving for enlightenment.

### C. Intervention and Salvation
- Jesus describes how the prayers of Pistis Sophia are heard and her redemption begins.
- The Savior sends divine messengers, who rescue her from the darkness and restore her to higher realms.

## III. Teachings on Cosmology and Aeons
- Jesus describes an intricate hierarchy of celestial beings and regions ("Aeons").
- Discussion of the rulers (archons) and their oppressive roles over human souls.
- Explanation of the relationships between different cosmic forces and the necessity of overcoming them for spiritual liberation.

### A. The Structure of the Universe
- Division into material and spiritual worlds.
- The distinction between lower cosmic forces (archons) and higher spiritual beings.
- Detailed description of various spiritual regions, gates, and pathways.

### B. Role of Archons
- Archons as cosmic authorities attempting to imprison souls.
- Their oppressive power is challenged through the secret knowledge revealed by Jesus.

## IV. Mysteries, Rituals, and Sacraments
- Jesus reveals secret rituals and mysteries to his disciples for achieving spiritual liberation.
- Specific initiation rites, prayers, and symbolic acts are discussed to help the soul ascend through the cosmic realms.

### A. Baptism and Ritual Practices
- Baptism of Water, Fire, and the Holy Spirit described as key rituals.
- Significance of sacred names, seals, passwords, and secret utterances.

### B. Transmission of Esoteric Knowledge
- Jesus instructs disciples on how to properly perform rituals and use divine names for protection against cosmic adversaries.

## V. Dialogue with Disciples
- Extensive dialogues between Jesus and his disciples (especially Mary Magdalene, Peter, John, Thomas, Andrew, and Philip).
- Disciples pose questions seeking clarification on cosmology, spiritual mysteries, and the soul’s redemption process.
- Mary Magdalene prominently engages with Jesus, highlighting her status as a spiritually enlightened disciple.

### A. Role of Mary Magdalene
- Mary Magdalene emerges as the chief interlocutor, expressing profound spiritual insight and receiving praise from Jesus.
- Symbolizes the role of feminine wisdom (Sophia) within Gnostic theology.

### B. Clarifications and Questions
- Disciples’ queries focus on understanding the nature of suffering, redemption, divine justice, and spiritual liberation.

## VI. Ethical Teachings and Moral Exhortations
- Instructions emphasizing the importance of moral purity, compassion, spiritual discernment, and the rejection of worldly attachments.
- Encouragement to maintain spiritual vigilance against deceitful cosmic rulers and false teachers.

## VII. Final Revelations and Conclusion
- Jesus reveals final mysteries, particularly the highest realms of divine Light and the path to eternal union with the Ultimate God.
- Jesus concludes by blessing his disciples and charging them to disseminate his teachings discreetly among worthy individuals.

---

## Major Themes:
- Spiritual redemption through inner knowledge (gnosis).
- The soul’s struggle against ignorance and oppressive cosmic powers.
- Feminine wisdom and spiritual intuition (Pistis Sophia and Mary Magdalene).
- Complex hierarchical cosmology reflecting a spiritual ascent.
- The transformative power of repentance, prayer, and ritual.

---

This outline provides a structured entry-point into the rich and complex narrative and theology of *Pistis Sophia*, reflecting its importance as a foundational Gnostic text and its role in illuminating ancient Christian mystical thought.

Thus, the key insight Mary receives in the **Gospel of Mary Magdalene** is that **ultimate spiritual freedom is found through inner gnosis, self-awareness, and the overcoming of internal spiritual obstacles rather than through external rituals or authorities.**

Actually, **Pistis Sophia** was **not** found in the Berlin (or "Akhmim") Codex (which contains the *Gospel of Mary Magdalene*). It has a separate and distinct source.

Here's the clarified explanation:

---

## Origin and Discovery of *Pistis Sophia*

- **Manuscript:**  
  *Pistis Sophia* was preserved in a single, ancient Coptic manuscript known as the **Askew Codex**, not the Berlin Codex. This manuscript is named after its discoverer, Anthony Askew, who acquired it in the late 18th century.

- **Discovery**:
  - Found around **1773**, possibly near Akhmim in Egypt.
  - Purchased by British physician and collector **Dr. Anthony Askew**.
  - Later, it became part of the **British Museum's** (now British Library’s) manuscript collection in **1785**, where it remains today.

- **Manuscript Details**:
  - Composed on papyrus in the **Sahidic dialect of Coptic**.
  - The manuscript itself dates roughly from the **4th century CE**, although the original text is believed to have been composed in Greek during the 2nd–3rd century CE.

- **Content and Significance**:
  - Contains extensive dialogues between Jesus and his disciples post-resurrection.
  - Details sophisticated Gnostic cosmology, rituals, prayers, and teachings centered around Pistis Sophia’s journey of redemption.

---

## Difference from the Berlin Codex (Gospel of Mary):

- **Berlin Codex** (Akhmim Codex), discovered in **1896**, is the source for the *Gospel of Mary Magdalene* and other texts such as the *Apocryphon of John* (partially) and *The Acts of Peter*.
- **Pistis Sophia**, by contrast, was part of the earlier discovery associated with the **Askew Codex**, named after Anthony Askew.  

Thus, while both texts are part of early Christian Gnostic literature, their manuscript sources are entirely separate, even though their themes and theology strongly complement one another.
