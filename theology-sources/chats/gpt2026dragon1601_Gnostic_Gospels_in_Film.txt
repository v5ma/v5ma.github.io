How many of the Gnostic Gospels have been turned into films?

Self Aware Networks GPT:
Very few of the **Gnostic Gospels** have been directly adapted into films, but some **films have been influenced by their themes or incorporated fragments** of their narratives. Here's an overview:

### Direct Adaptations
No major feature film has **faithfully adapted** an entire Gnostic Gospel like the *Gospel of Thomas*, *Gospel of Mary*, or *Gospel of Judas* in the same way the canonical Gospels have been adapted in films like *The Passion of the Christ* or *Jesus of Nazareth*.

### Indirect or Thematic Adaptations
Several films, however, **draw heavily on Gnostic ideas**—such as secret knowledge (gnosis), the illusion of the material world, divine spark within, and the false god (demiurge). Examples include:

#### 1. **The Last Temptation of Christ (1988)** – Directed by Martin Scorsese
- Based on the novel by Nikos Kazantzakis, it explores a deeply human, psychologically conflicted Jesus.
- **Themes**: Dual nature of Christ, illusion vs. reality, inner struggle—resonates with Gnostic views of spiritual enlightenment and false appearances.

#### 2. **Stigmata (1999)**
- Plot revolves around the discovery of the *Gospel of Thomas* and the Catholic Church's efforts to suppress it.
- **Key Quote**: “The Kingdom of God is inside you and all around you, not in mansions of wood and stone.” (This is from the Gospel of Thomas).
- It directly uses Gnostic sayings and critiques ecclesiastical power.

#### 3. **The Matrix Trilogy (1999–2003)**
- Not explicitly about Jesus or the Gospels, but Gnostic to the core: reality is an illusion, salvation comes through awakening, and the Architect (Demiurge) is not the true source of divinity.
- **Neo as Gnostic Christ figure**.

#### 4. **Mary Magdalene (2018)**
- While primarily drawn from canonical narratives, it attempts to rehabilitate Mary’s role and includes elements inspired by the *Gospel of Mary*, particularly in portraying her as an **independent spiritual authority**.

#### 5. **Jesus: The Lost Years (2007, History Channel)**
- A documentary that explores the **Gnostic view** of Jesus' teachings and includes references to *Gospel of Thomas* and *Gospel of Judas*.

---

### Notable Mentions (but not direct adaptations)
- **The Gospel of Judas** (2006, National Geographic) – A dramatized documentary about the discovery of the gospel and its implications.
- **The Da Vinci Code** (2006) – While not based on a Gnostic gospel, it explores **Gnostic-influenced ideas** about Mary Magdalene and secret knowledge suppressed by the Church.

---

### Summary
There is no **feature film adaptation** of any Gnostic Gospel in full. However, films like *Stigmata*, *The Last Temptation of Christ*, *Mary Magdalene*, and *The Gospel of Judas* integrate **Gnostic texts and themes** either directly or allegorically. The door remains open for a **more direct adaptation**—something like a cinematic version of the *Gospel of Mary* or *Thomas* could be profoundly original if developed with depth and care.

Micah Blumberg:
Has there ever been a movie about Isaiah?

Self Aware Networks GPT:
No, there has never been a major film **specifically dedicated to the life of the prophet Isaiah** as the central character.

### However, Isaiah has appeared in or influenced the following contexts:

#### 1. **Biblical Epics and Docudramas**
- Some **documentary-style or Christian educational films** briefly mention Isaiah, particularly in reference to his prophecies about the coming of the Messiah (e.g., *For Unto Us a Child is Born*, *The Bible: In the Beginning*, or TV miniseries like *The Bible* produced by Roma Downey and Mark Burnett).
- In these, Isaiah is usually **quoted** or represented as a **voiceover or brief vision**, but not explored as a full character.

#### 2. **Jesus Films**
- In films about Jesus (e.g., *The Gospel of John*, *The Passion of the Christ*, *Jesus of Nazareth*), Isaiah’s words are often **quoted by characters** to highlight fulfillment of prophecy, especially:
  - *Isaiah 7:14* – "Behold, a virgin shall conceive…"
  - *Isaiah 53* – The Suffering Servant passage, often interpreted as predicting Jesus’ death.
- Again, Isaiah is not depicted on screen as a prophet in his own time.

#### 3. **Thematic Influence**
- Some allegorical or prophetic films (e.g., *The Book of Eli*) have **Isaiah’s themes of justice, redemption, and divine vision**, but not Isaiah himself.
- Isaiah’s message of **ethical monotheism, social justice, and cosmic vision of peace** has indirectly influenced spiritual and philosophical films, especially those engaging with Jewish and Christian prophetic tradition.

---

### Why No Isaiah Film?
Isaiah's story is not structured like a biopic—it spans many chapters, contains visions, poetry, and prophetic declarations, and lacks a clear narrative arc like Moses or David. This may have discouraged filmmakers.

---

### Summary
There has **never been a full film** devoted to Isaiah’s life, but his words are **quoted in Jesus films**, and his **influence is profound** across religious cinema. A **dramatized film of Isaiah’s visions**, political conflicts, and cosmic prophecies—perhaps focusing on his confrontation with kings and his mystical visions of God (Isaiah 6)—could be a groundbreaking cinematic project.
