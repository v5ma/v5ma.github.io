Short answer: yes—these are two different works that only share the title.

- #1 The “Ethiopic Apocalypse of Peter” (sometimes just called *Apocalypse of Peter*) is the 2nd-century proto-orthodox tour-of-heaven-and-hell text. Our fullest form survives in Geʿez (Ethiopic) manuscripts; related Greek fragments were found at Akhmim and elsewhere. It’s a revelation discourse by Jesus that shows Peter punishments of sinners and rewards of the righteous. Scholars generally treat the Ethiopic text as closer to the original than the Akhmim Greek because of length and patristic echoes. citeturn0search0turn0search7

It presents a distinctly Gnostic theology (e.g., a docetic view of the Passion; Jesus revealing hidden knowledge and even “laughing” at the ignorance of persecutors) and polemicizes against proto-orthodox believers. Canon note (Ethiopian Bible): Although Ethiopia preserves the full Geʿez text, the *Apocalypse of Peter* is not part of the standard 81-book Orthodox Tewahedo biblical canon (which does include unique works like 1 Enoch and Jubilees). It’s an apocryphon in that tradition. citeturn1search2

- **Matthew & Luke** are commonly dated ≈80–90 CE; **Acts** ≈70–90 CE. citeturn2search4turn2search6  
(That’s decades **before or around** Josephus’ *Antiquities* in 93/94 CE, not after it.) citeturn0search8

• **Claims that Luke used Josephus are debated and unproven.**  
A minority holds Luke knew *Antiquities* (e.g., arguments about shared names/events), but many historians conclude similarities reflect **shared historical knowledge**, not literary dependence; where we would expect tell-tale **verbal agreement and sequence**, it’s not there. citeturn1search0turn0search20

• **Some famous “parallels” actually cut against dependence.**  
- **Acts 5** mentions **Theudas** *before* **Judas the Galilean**, whereas Josephus places Theudas in the 40s CE (long **after** Judas c. 6 CE). If Luke were copying Josephus, this is an odd “mistake”; it looks more like **independent** (or different) information. citeturn0search2turn0search14  
- On other data points (census issues, priestly names, Herodian chronology), Luke and Josephus often **diverge** in details—again not what we see when one text copies another.

• **We also have early, non-canonical Jesus traditions independent of Josephus.**  
The **Egerton Gospel** papyrus is late-2nd/turn-of-3rd century and shows Jesus traditions circulating **outside** the NT—parallel to, not derived from, Josephus. citeturn1search1

# What proponents usually claim—and why it falls short

- **Claim:** “Luke–Acts cites Josephus for dates and events.”  
  **Problem:** No sustained **verbal** or **structural** dependence is demonstrable; overlaps can be explained as two authors writing Greco-Roman history about the same figures (Herod, Pilate, priests, revolts). Major reference works and genre studies stress the broader historiographic context, not Luke’s reliance on Josephus. citeturn1search0

- **Claim:** “The Gospels are 2nd century, so they *must* use Josephus.”  
  **Problem:** The **manuscripts** and **mainstream dating** don’t support a wholesale 2nd-century composition. Mark ≈70; Matthew/Luke ≈80–90; John ≈90–110—with papyri copies following not long after. citeturn2search0turn2search4

# Bottom line

- We have **early Gospel papyri** and a **late-1st-century** scholarly consensus for composition.

- The simplest reading of the evidence: **no, the Gospels are not 2nd-century rewrites of Josephus.** At most, Luke–Acts and Josephus are parallel historians of the same era, using different sources. citeturn1search0turn0search8
