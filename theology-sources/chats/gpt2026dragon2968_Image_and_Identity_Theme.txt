Across the Fallout canon, several real-world countries are explicitly mentioned or clearly implied, mostly in pre-War historical context rather than as active nations during the post-apocalyptic era. China is the most central foreign nation in Fallout lore. It is the United States’ primary geopolitical rival before the Great War, and the Sino-American conflict over resources is a direct cause of the nuclear exchange. Chinese soldiers, espionage efforts, submarines, propaganda, and technology are referenced repeatedly, and China is treated as an advanced, centralized power comparable to the U.S. rather than a vague adversary.

Canada is explicitly mentioned and uniquely important because it is annexed by the United States before the war. Fallout depicts Canada as occupied rather than allied, with civil unrest, executions, and propaganda showing U.S. control. This annexation is one of the clearest signs that the pre-War United States had become openly imperial and authoritarian.

Mexico is mentioned in several places, usually as a neighboring country affected by instability, migration, or military spillover from the United States. It is treated as an external nation rather than an annexed one, but it appears in dialogue, terminal entries, and background lore.

The United Kingdom is referenced in pre-War corporate and cultural material, indicating it still existed as a recognizable nation before the bombs fell. Mentions are sparse and usually tied to business, technology exchange, or historical context rather than active politics.

Germany is mentioned in older Fallout titles, primarily in relation to pre-War technology, engineering reputation, and historical references. These mentions reinforce that Europe existed as a recognizable geopolitical space before the global collapse.

Russia, or the Soviet Union by implication, appears indirectly through Cold War imagery, ideological references, and historical framing. Fallout’s alternate timeline diverges after World War II, but echoes of Cold War rivalries remain part of the cultural background.

Japan is referenced mainly through corporate, technological, and cultural cues, often indirectly. Like other non-Chinese nations, it is acknowledged as existing pre-War but is not central to the main conflict.

Venezuela is mentioned in Fallout 4 era lore as a pre-War nation hosting infrastructure and research activity, reinforcing that South America still existed as a geopolitical region in Fallout’s timeline, even if it was not central to the resource war narrative.

Other countries, such as France and Italy, appear occasionally through cultural references, product branding, or historical notes, but these mentions are minimal and serve mostly to ground Fallout’s world as a distorted version of real 20th-century geopolitics rather than a fully mapped global setting.

Overall, Fallout consistently treats the rest of the world as real, but distant. The lore is intentionally U.S.-centric, using foreign countries primarily to explain how extreme American expansionism, paranoia, and resource obsession escalated into global annihilation.
