- After the life of Jesus, early Christianity began to separate from Judaism, particularly as it spread to non-Jewish populations in the Roman Empire. - By the time the Talmud was fully developed (2nd-6th centuries CE), Christianity had already become a distinct religion, and the Talmud's debates about Jewish law were seen as unnecessary or incompatible with Christian beliefs.

### **5. Misunderstandings and Suspicion**
- In the Middle Ages, many Christians misunderstood the Talmud, viewing it as anti-Christian or subversive. This led to persecution of Jews and even the burning of Talmudic texts in some Christian societies.
- These historical tensions created a long-standing mistrust and lack of engagement with the Talmud in Christian communities.

---

### **6. Focus on Scripture Alone**
- Many branches of Christianity, particularly Protestantism, emphasize **sola scriptura** ("scripture alone") as the basis for faith and practice. This approach excludes oral traditions like the Talmud, which is seen as an addition to the written Word of God.

---

### **7. Practical and Cultural Differences**
- The Talmud is deeply tied to Jewish culture, history, and identity. Its discussions of Jewish law, rituals, and ethics are specific to Jewish life, making it less relevant to Christians, whose practices are shaped by different traditions and doctrines.

---

### **Conclusion**
Christianity does not embrace the Talmud because it is rooted in a fundamentally different approach to scripture, law, and salvation. For Christians, Jesus Christ fulfills the role of teacher, interpreter, and savior, making the rabbinic debates and legal elaborations of the Talmud unnecessary. Historical tensions and theological divergence further solidified this separation, leaving the Talmud as a uniquely Jewish text.
