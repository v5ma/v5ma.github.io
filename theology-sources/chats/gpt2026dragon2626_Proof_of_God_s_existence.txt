6. **Aggregate.** Plug normalized metrics into Eq. (1).  
7. **Validation.** Perturb coherence (targeted phase noise, coupling blockers) and check that TCI changes track behavioral/control competence.

### 4. Methods Box A — **Plants**

**Preparation.**  
- **Substrate & sensors:** extracellular electrodes across leaves, stem, and roots; voltage‑sensitive or calcium‑sensitive dyes/indicators where feasible; slow imaging of ionic/pressure waves.  
- **Task stimuli (tomography‑like):** rotating/sliding light bars; spatially controlled humidity or chemical line‑sources via microfluidics; mechanical micromotions.  
- **Controls/perturbations:** reversible ion‑channel modulators, osmotic shocks, or volatile agents known to disrupt electrical signaling; phase‑jittered entrainment tones of light intensity to specifically degrade coherence without destroying amplitude.

**Protocol.**  
1. Baseline: present structured stimuli while recording \(x_i(t)\), \(y_t\) (e.g., stomatal aperture, tendril motion, turgor).  
2. Fit \(\mathcal{H}_\Theta\) to predict \(y_t\) and infer \(\hat{s}_t\).  
3. Compute \(\mathrm{TCI}\) and behavioral reconstruction accuracy (e.g., source localization of the light/chemical line).  
4. Perturb: apply coherence‑disrupting intervention; re‑measure TCI and task performance.

**Predictions.**  
- Task phases requiring integration across organs raise \(R^{(\omega)}\) (slow bands) and cross‑frequency coupling; \(\mathrm{TCI}\) increases.  
- Coherence‑disrupting perturbations reduce \(\mathrm{TCI}\) and degrade render‑guided behaviors (e.g., hydrotropism accuracy, closure timing).  
- Recovery of coherence tracks recovery of performance (dose–response).

### 5. Methods Box B — **Microorganisms & biofilms**

**Preparation.**  
- **Substrate & sensors:** microelectrode arrays and fluorescent potential reporters in bacterial or fungal colonies; microfluidic arenas to sculpt nutrient/antibiotic gradients.  
- **Task stimuli:** moving nutrient peaks (scanned line‑integrals); alternating antibiotic masks; mechanical shear pulses.  
- **Controls/perturbations:** quorum‑sensing inhibitors, ion‑channel blockers, or spatial decouplers (gel barriers) to reduce effective coupling without killing cells.

**Protocol.**  
1. Record spatiotemporal voltage/metabolic waves \(x_i(t)\) and colony‑level outputs \(y_t\) (growth fronts, sporulation decisions).  
2. Fit \(\mathcal{H}_\Theta\) and infer \(\hat{s}_t\); compute \(\mathrm{TCI}\).  
3. Manipulate coupling/coherence; reassess \(\mathrm{TCI}\) and decision accuracy (e.g., navigating moving nutrient sources).

**Predictions.**  
- High‑coherence metabolic waves precede coordinated decisions; \(\mathrm{TCI}\) spikes at decision onsets.  
- Disrupting coherence selectively reduces \(\mathrm{TCI}\) and yields fragmented, myopic responses.

### 6. Falsifiability, controls, and reporting

- **Pre‑registration:** specify bands, window sizes, null procedures, and \(\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\delta\).  
- **Nulls:** phase‑randomized surrogates; spatial shuffles; amplitude‑preserving but phase‑scrambled controls.  
- **Double dissociation:** manipulations that change amplitude but not phase should **not** move TCI much; phase‑specific manipulations should.  
- **Generalization tests:** train \(\mathcal{H}_\Theta\) on one stimulus family; test TCI on structurally novel but homologous tasks (measures counterfactual capacity).  
- **Open data:** publish raw time series, derived \(C^{(\omega)}\), code, and a checklist of analysis choices.

### 9. Limitations & scope

- TCI is **task‑relative**; absence of evidence is not evidence of absence.

- Parameter choices must be fixed **a priori** and justified; we recommend sensitivity analyses.
