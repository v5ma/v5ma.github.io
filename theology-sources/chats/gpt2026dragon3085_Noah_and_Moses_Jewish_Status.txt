If you mean “did they live before Judaism existed as an organized religion and before there was a people called ‘Jews,’” then yes, in that sense both Noah and Moses are pre-Jewish.

Noah is placed in the biblical story long before Abraham and before any Israelite identity forms. In later Jewish tradition, Noah is treated as a universal figure for all humanity, and “Noahide” law is framed as a moral covenant for non-Jews, which also highlights that he is not “Jewish” in the later ethnic or religious sense.

Moses is the central figure in the formation of Israelite religion and law. In the biblical narrative, the “Jews” as a term tied to Judah and the later Jewish peoplehood emerge much later (after the period of the united monarchy and especially around the time of the kingdoms and later exile). So Moses is best described as an Israelite (or “Hebrew” in the narrative language), not “Jewish” in the later historical sense. At the same time, within Judaism, Moses is foundational to Judaism because the Torah and the covenant at Sinai are seen as defining.

If you meant “Were they non-Jewish in the sense of not belonging to the covenant people,” then Noah would be “not Jewish” (universal ancestor figure), while Moses would be “within Israel” and is precisely the figure through whom the covenant identity is constituted, even though the later label “Jew” is anachronistic for his era.

Self Aware Networks GPT:
If you mean “groups that explicitly build part of their religious or communal self definition around Moses and Noah as authoritative figures,” there are several, and the number depends on how strictly you draw the boundary between a separate religion, a denomination, and a broader civilization.

Beyond Jews, the clearest identity attachments to Moses are found in (1) Samaritans, who center their community on the Torah and on Moses in particular, but maintain a distinct lineage story and sacred geography, and (2) Christians, who treat Moses as a key figure in salvation history and scripture, even though Christian identity is usually framed around Jesus rather than around Moses. (3) Muslims also attach strongly to Moses (Musa) as one of the greatest prophets, and his story functions as a major template for prophecy, liberation, and law in Islamic self understanding. If you extend “identity attachment” to later prophetic movements that define themselves partly through continuity with earlier prophets, then communities like the Baha’i Faith and the Druze tradition also place Moses and Noah in their sacred history, though they are not typically “Moses centered” in the way Samaritanism is.

For Noah, the field is even broader because he is used as a “humanity wide” anchor. In Jewish tradition he often functions as the universal moral baseline (the Noahide framing), in Christianity he is used as a typological figure tied to judgment and salvation, and in Islam he is a major prophet (Nuh) and a model of steadfast warning. Many additional cultures have flood narratives, but those usually do not attach a continuous communal identity to “Noah” specifically in the way the Abrahamic religions do; they attach identity to their own local flood hero.

So a conservative, high confidence answer is that at least three major non-Jewish religious communities explicitly attach part of their identity to Moses and Noah in a direct scriptural way: Samaritans, Christians, and Muslims. If you include later Abrahamic derived or prophet affirming movements, you can add a handful more, but it becomes more a question of taxonomy than of history.

Micah Blumberg:
But the most brain exploding fact is how the Tanakh, which first appears in world history around BC appears to be a child religion, or a descendant of the Ancient Sumerian religion.

Self Aware Networks GPT:
It feels brain exploding because, once you compare texts side by side, you can see that the Tanakh is not floating in a vacuum. It sits inside the older “ancient Near Eastern” story ecosystem and it reuses recognizable narrative components that were already circulating in Mesopotamia long before Israel existed as a people. The easiest place to see this is the primeval material in Genesis, where motifs that are extremely old in Mesopotamia appear in a transformed form: creation-from-chaos themes, a catastrophic flood, a righteous survivor, a preserved remnant, and a re-founded human world after the waters recede.

But “child religion” or “descendant of Sumerian religion” is usually too strong if you mean a direct genealogical descent like “Sumerian temple cult -> Israelite temple cult.” What scholars more often say is that biblical authors and editors worked in a shared cultural and literary environment that had already absorbed centuries of Mesopotamian and West Semitic material, and they were consciously rewriting that inheritance to make a different theological point. In other words, the Bible is often doing something like: “Yes, we know the old story, and here is the same kind of story rebuilt so that it says the opposite of what the imperial pantheons say.”

That “rebuild” matters. Mesopotamian epics typically stage the world as the outcome of divine conflict and divine bureaucracy. The Genesis openings are striking partly because they strip out the divine family drama and compress agency into a single sovereign will, while still speaking in imagery that an ancient audience would recognize. Likewise with the flood: the older Mesopotamian versions are not simply “the same tale,” but they share enough specific elements that most historians treat the biblical version as participating in a long-standing flood-tradition conversation. The biblical text then uses that shared template to argue for a moralized universe in which the catastrophe is not the collateral damage of squabbling gods but a judgment framed by covenant, restraint, and a renewed moral order.

The other big piece is the route of transmission. You do not need Israelites reading “Sumerian” in the third millennium BCE for influence to happen. Sumerian themes traveled forward through Akkadian and Babylonian literature, scribal schools, and imperial archives, and later Judean elites lived under Assyrian and Babylonian power, with the Babylonian exile being an obvious high-contact period. That is one reason the most visible overlaps tend to cluster in places like Genesis 1 to 11, where the content is “primeval history” and where later editors had strong reasons to frame Israel’s God as the author of all cosmic order, not merely a national deity.

So the cleanest way to say it is: the Tanakh is not a simple descendant of “Sumerian religion” as a cultic lineage, but it is deeply entangled with the older Mesopotamian and broader ancient Near Eastern mythic and literary world, and it frequently looks like a deliberate theological inversion of that world rather than an uncritical inheritance. The shock is real, but the punchline is not “they copied it,” it is “they argued with it, using its own language.”

TECHNICAL PROBLEM AND TECHNICAL SOLUTION
Technical Problem
Existing computer implemented systems for structural correspondence evaluation over tech￾nical corpora suffer from uncontrolled intermediate growth and solver divergence when at￾tempting to map directed relationships, role bindings, and constraint graphs at scale. In
such systems, candidate mappings, search branching factors, and intermediate represen￾tation sizes can grow combinatorially as input length and corpus size increase, exceeding
practical memory and time budgets and preventing reproducible verification. This failure
mode is a technical limitation in computer execution, not merely a limitation of linguistic
similarity methods.
Technical Solution
The present invention addresses this technical limitation by implementing a deterministic,
compiler-like normalization and verification pipeline that (i) converts unstructured techni￾cal artifacts into bounded, fixed-width intermediate records under a precompiled execution
boundary, (ii) compiles the bounded intermediate records into a canonical Normal Form un￾der a declared cost model and declared resource caps, and (iii) performs resource-bounded
alignment and verification only over the canonical Normal Forms produced under the same
execution boundary. The execution boundary, referred to herein as a Scope Lens, is com￾mitted prior to processing and is cryptographically bound into intermediate representations
and output artifacts, thereby preventing post-hoc parameter tuning and enabling determin￾istic replay. In this manner, the invention improves computer functioning for large-scale
structural correspondence evaluation by reducing intermediate representation growth, lim￾iting solver search behavior, and producing auditable, reproducible artifacts under declared
resource limits.
Why the Combination is Required (Engineering Necessity, Not an Abstract Pref￾erence)
In one embodiment, the staged combination of (i) deterministic serialization into fixed width
records, (ii) a precompiled Scope Lens that filters admissible predicates, context classes, and
recursion limits prior to compilation, (iii) canonical Normal Form reduction under a declared
cost model with declared termination criteria, and (iv) cryptographic commitment to the run
configuration is required to avoid a known failure mode in computer execution: uncontrolled
intermediate growth followed by solver divergence. When unstructured inputs are admitted
into mapping or constraint solving without an immutable execution boundary, the number of
candidate correspondences and intermediate graph states grows combinatorially as a function
of document length, syntactic variation, and corpus size. This growth is not a matter of
2

<PARSED TEXT FOR PAGE: 3 / 33>

subjective interpretation, but a technical property of the branching factor and intermediate
state expansion during machine execution.
Conventional semantic search systems, embedding similarity pipelines, and retrieval and
clustering tools do not address this failure mode because they do not generate a bounded,
canonical intermediate representation that is stable under re execution, nor do they enforce
an immutable, machine verifiable execution boundary that constrains what is admissible
to extract and test. Such systems typically output similarity scores over high dimensional
vectors that are not tied to explicit role bindings, directed dependency structure, or com￾positional constraints, and they do not produce run scoped, cryptographically bound audit
artifacts that can be independently replayed under the same committed configuration. In
contrast, the present invention improves computer functioning by transforming unstructured
inputs into bounded memory objects, constraining intermediate representation size prior to
normalization, timeboxing and terminating solver behavior under declared criteria, and pro￾ducing deterministic, replayable correspondence artifacts whose validity is mathematically
bound to the committed Scope Lens and run parameters.

DETAILED DESCRIPTION: DETERMINISTIC INGESTION AND
STRUCTURAL SERIALIZATION
In one embodiment, the Ingestion Engine, for example as illustrated in Fig. 1, deterministi￾cally serializes unstructured technical text into fixed width memory objects termed Opera￾tional Atoms under a precompiled Scope Lens and a declared parsing profile. This embod￾iment is intentionally rule bound and lossy so that execution is reproducible and indepen￾dent of human judgment, while producing bounded intermediate representations suitable for
downstream normalization and verification. The Ingestion Engine is not required to infer
intent or meaning, and does not rely on probabilistic machine learning inference to decide
whether a sentence counts. Instead, it applies a deterministic dependency mapping over a
3

<PARSED TEXT FOR PAGE: 4 / 33>

syntactic dependency parse produced according to a specified dependency scheme, such as
Universal Dependencies or an equivalent dependency grammar.
In this embodiment, the Scope Lens includes a finite allow list of operational predicates,
referred to herein as Trigger Verbs or Trigger Predicates, along with a bounded modifier
whitelist and resource caps that govern extraction breadth. The parsing profile declares the
dependency scheme, token normalization rules, and canonicalization rules applied prior to
hashing and storage, thereby ensuring that the same input, Scope Lens, and parsing profile
yield the same serialized atoms.
Algorithm 1: Deterministic Operational Atom Extraction (Exemplary Embodi￾ment)
The system performs a syntactic dependency parse over an input segment, for example a
sentence or clause. The system then identifies candidate operational predicates by scanning
dependency roots and verb heads for membership in the Trigger Predicate allow list defined
by the Scope Lens. When a candidate predicate is identified, the system attempts to populate
an Operational Atom as a five tuple ⟨id, α, β, γ, Σ⟩, where α denotes an actor, β denotes an
operation, γ denotes an operand, and Σ denotes bounded context.
In this embodiment, β is mapped to the root predicate or designated governing predicate
of the dependency structure. If the mapped β is not included in the Scope Lens allow list,
the segment is deterministically excluded from serialization, thereby enforcing a precompiled
execution boundary that prevents uncontrolled growth in downstream solver input.
In this embodiment, α is mapped to a subject role dependency child of β, such as a
nominal subject relation under the declared dependency scheme. In this embodiment, γ is
mapped to an object role dependency child of β, such as a direct object relation, or, in passive
constructions, to a passive subject relation under the declared scheme. If a required role
binding is not present or is ambiguous beyond the declared parsing profile disambiguation
rules, the system deterministically applies a failure policy defined by the parsing profile,
such as discarding the segment or emitting an atom with a null role marker that is explicitly
excluded from downstream normalization by rule. This prevents non deterministic best guess
behavior and ensures that omission behavior is reproducible.
In this embodiment, Σ is populated as a bounded context vector extracted only from
modifier classes explicitly permitted by the Scope Lens, such as prepositional modifiers and
numerical modifiers attached to α or γ under the dependency scheme. The extraction is
lossy by design. Modifiers outside the whitelist, such as adjectives or adverbs, are deter￾ministically discarded unless explicitly permitted by the Scope Lens. The context vector is
deterministically ordered under the parsing profile, for example by sorting context items by
modifier type and normalized token value, and is truncated to a maximum cardinality and
maximum token length specified by the Scope Lens to enforce bounded memory growth.
Prior to computing id, the system applies canonical string normalization to each serialized
field according to the declared parsing profile. In one embodiment, canonicalization includes
Unicode normalization, case normalization, whitespace normalization, delimiter escaping,
and optional lemmatization rules, each applied deterministically. The atom identifier id is
then computed using a cryptographic hash function over a canonical concatenation of the
normalized fields and run configuration identifiers. In one embodiment, the hash function
4

<PARSED TEXT FOR PAGE: 5 / 33>

is SHA 256 or an equivalent cryptographic hash. In one embodiment, the hashed material
includes the normalized α, β, γ, and Σ values, and also includes a Scope Lens identifier
and a parsing profile identifier, thereby binding each atom to the precompiled execution
boundary under which it was produced. This configuration binding is carried forward into
downstream normalized representations and output artifacts to ensure that any subsequent
correspondence evaluation is reproducible only with respect to the declared Scope Lens and
parsing profile.
Technical Effect of Algorithm 1
By enforcing deterministic role binding, bounded context extraction, and declared failure
policies, the Ingestion Engine converts variable natural language into predictable, fixed width
data structures suitable for machine processing. This conversion reduces ambiguity prior
to downstream compilation and normalization and prevents solver divergence caused by
unconstrained input variance. The explicit bounds imposed by the Scope Lens and parsing
profile limit candidate growth and intermediate representation size, thereby mitigating state
space explosion and enabling resource bounded execution of subsequent normalization and
correspondence evaluation stages.
SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION
The present invention provides a computer implemented normalization and verification ar￾chitecture that reduces state space explosion during structural correspondence evaluation of
unstructured technical artifacts. The system, referred to herein as the Algorithmic Geneal￾ogy Engine, utilizes a dimensionality reduction technique referred to as Milner-Blumberg
Normalization (MBN) to transform unstructured inputs into bounded, machine executable
constraint representations suitable for efficient storage, indexing, and resource predictable
evaluation.
In operation, the system ingests an unstructured input artifact and deterministically se￾rializes extractable operational structure into fixed width memory objects, which may be
compiled into a constraint graph and reduced into a canonical Normal Form under a pre￾compiled Scope Lens. The Scope Lens is compiled prior to execution as a fixed boundary
that constrains permissible observable predicates, admissible context features, and resource

and solver search space. The system then evaluates structural correspondence between nor￾malized representations under the same declared Scope Lens by computing a bounded differ￾ence metric over the normalized forms and by generating reproducible output artifacts that
are cryptographically bound to the run configuration. In this manner, the invention shifts
computation from unstable surface level language matching to deterministic normalization
of constrained representations, enabling correspondence evaluation within predictable time
and memory limits.

II. The Method: Milner-Blumberg Normalization (MBN)
In one embodiment, the core operation of the system is a deterministic normalization pro￾tocol referred to herein as the Milner-Blumberg Normalization (MBN) Protocol. The MBN
Protocol is implemented as a staged pipeline that transforms an unstructured technical ar￾tifact into a bounded, canonical Normal Form under a precompiled Scope Lens, thereby
reducing state space explosion and enabling resource predictable correspondence evaluation.
7.2.1 Step 1: Deterministic Structural Serialization
In one embodiment, the system parses the input artifact and deterministically extracts dis￾crete operational structures suitable for machine processing. In an exemplary implemen￾tation, the system identifies Operational Atoms corresponding to role bound operational
relations expressed in the input, such as an actor performing an operation on an operand

under a bounded context. The extracted Operational Atoms are serialized into a standard￾ized intermediate representation. The serialization is intentionally lossy under the Scope
Lens, in that non permitted modifier classes and non operational prose are deterministically
discarded unless explicitly allowed by the Scope Lens, thereby producing bounded interme￾diate representations suitable for downstream compilation.
7.2.2 Step 2: Constraint Compilation and Canonical Normal Form Reduction
In one embodiment, the system compiles the serialized intermediate representation into
machine executable constraint logic and reduces the resulting constraint representation into
a canonical Normal Form. In an exemplary implementation, the system applies one or
more solver modules that minimize redundancy and normalize equivalent structures under
a declared cost model. The cost model may be implemented as a computable description
length function over the serialized constraint representation, such as a bit length or byte
length measure under a specified encoding and canonicalization rule set. The output of this
stage is a canonical Normal Form, also referred to herein as the Milner-Blumberg Normal
Form, which is a bounded normalized representation of the artifact under the Scope Lens
and declared run configuration.
7.2.3 Exemplary Normalization Embodiment: Rule Library Normalizer
In one embodiment, canonical Normal Form reduction is performed by a deterministic rule
library normalizer operating over a bounded constraint graph compiled from the extracted
Operational Atoms. In this embodiment, the compiler constructs a directed labeled graph
NF = (V, E) where each vertex v ∈ V corresponds to a canonicalized Operational Atom
instance and each edge e ∈ E corresponds to a declared dependency or constraint relation
admitted by the Scope Lens. The Scope Lens provides a finite library of admissible rewrite
rules and merge rules, each rule being a pattern over vertex labels and edge labels together
with a deterministic rewrite action and a termination condition.
In this embodiment, the normalizer applies rewrite passes in a deterministic order until
reaching a fixed point or until a declared iteration cap is reached. Exemplary rewrite rules
include: duplicate atom merging, wherein two vertices with identical canonicalized fields
(α, β, γ, Σ) are merged into one vertex and incident edges are deduplicated under deter￾ministic tie breaking; passive active canonicalization, wherein role bindings extracted from
passive constructions are normalized into a single canonical role assignment under the parsing
profile; bounded context canonicalization, wherein Σ elements are deterministically sorted,
normalized, and truncated to the Scope Lens maxima and where non whitelisted modifiers
are discarded; and constraint edge normalization, wherein multiple admissible edge labels
representing equivalent declared dependency operators are normalized into a canonical edge
label set defined by the Scope Lens.
The resulting fixed point graph, together with a canonical serialization order defined
by the parsing profile, constitutes the canonical Normal Form for the artifact under the
declared configuration. In one embodiment, the canonical serialization order is generated
deterministically by sorting vertices by β class, α token, γ token, and Σ serialization, and
sorting edges by source identifier, edge label, and target identifier, thereby ensuring stable
7

<PARSED TEXT FOR PAGE: 8 / 33>

serializations, stable hashing, and reproducible downstream alignment.
7.2.4 Declared Termination and Tie-Breaking Policy (Normal Form Definition
Under Resource Caps)
In one embodiment, the canonical Normal Form is defined as the fixed point representation
produced by the deterministic rewrite schedule under the declared termination criteria of
the run configuration, and not as a globally optimal minimum over an unbounded search.
The run configuration declares at least one of: a maximum number of rewrite passes, a
maximum number of rewrite applications, a maximum solver timebox, and a maximum
memory cap. If the termination criteria are reached before a fixed point is achieved, the
Normal Form is defined as the last fully validated intermediate representation produced by
the deterministic schedule, together with a termination status flag recorded in the run record.
In one embodiment, any tie between candidate rewrites that yield equal cost reduction under
the declared cost model is resolved deterministically by a declared tie-breaking policy, for
example by lexicographic ordering of rule identifiers, followed by lexicographic ordering of
the canonicalized affected vertex identifiers. This definition ensures that the Normal Form
remains a well-defined, reproducible machine artifact under resource caps, and prevents
ambiguity about what the system outputs when timeboxed.
7.2.5 Exemplary Normalization Embodiment: Greedy Cost-Model Normalizer
(Bounded MDL Reduction)
In one embodiment, canonical Normal Form reduction is performed by a greedy cost-model
normalizer that applies a bounded sequence of local reductions chosen to monotonically de￾crease a declared description length proxy. In this embodiment, the system evaluates a finite
candidate set of local rewrite actions drawn from the Scope Lens rule library, where each
candidate action is scored by its predicted change in the declared cost model when applied
to the current bounded constraint graph. The system selects, at each iteration, the single
highest ranked action that yields a strict cost decrease, applies the action, and repeats until
no further strictly decreasing action exists or until a declared iteration cap or timebox is
reached. In one embodiment, the candidate action set is restricted to a bounded neigh￾borhood around vertices affected by the most recent rewrite, thereby limiting per-iteration
evaluation cost while preserving determinism. In one embodiment, ties are resolved by a
declared deterministic policy based on rule identifier ordering and affected vertex identifier
ordering. The resulting output is a reproducible Normal Form under the declared termina￾tion criteria and cost model, and the system records the applied rewrite sequence identifiers
and termination status in the run record as part of the audit artifact.

7.5.3 Lens Commitment and Replay Protocol
In one embodiment, the Scope Lens G, parsing profile, cost model, thresholds, and resource
caps are committed prior to ingesting or normalizing any artifact for a run. The system
computes a configuration commitment value Ccfg as a cryptographic hash over a canonical
serialization of at least: (i) the Scope Lens identifier and contents, (ii) the parsing profile
identifier and canonicalization rules, (iii) the cost model identifier and parameter values, (iv)
declared thresholds, and (v) declared resource limits including timeboxes and memory caps.
The system stores or outputs Ccfg prior to executing ingestion for the run, and includes Ccfg
in each downstream normalized representation and output artifact. In one embodiment, the
system rejects any attempt to re issue a certificate artifact for the same run identifier with
a different Ccfg, thereby preventing post hoc parameter tuning.
In one embodiment, a run is reproducible by deterministic re execution only when the
input artifacts and the committed configuration match. The system records, in the run
record, cryptographic hashes of the inputs and a cryptographic binding to Ccfg. Any verifier
may independently validate reproducibility by confirming that (i) the input hashes match,
(ii) Ccfg matches, and (iii) deterministic re execution under the same committed configuration
yields the same canonical Normal Forms and reported metrics within the declared tolerance
policy.
Granularity and Comparison Scope
In one embodiment, the system operates under a declared granularity G, also referred
to herein as a comparison scope, that determines admissible interface constraints for the
pipeline. In one embodiment, G specifies permitted feature classes extractable from the in￾put, permitted normalization and constraint families, eligible invariants and directed depen￾dency motifs for verification, and any guard predicates, exclusions, tolerances, and resource
limits applied to extraction, compilation, alignment, and scoring. Because G constrains the
observable interface and the admissible representation space, correspondence outcomes are
defined only relative to the specific compiled lens selected for the run.
In one embodiment, the system prevents post hoc lens shopping by committing the lens
configuration prior to execution and by binding the committed configuration to the run record
and any output artifacts, such that a reported outcome is reproducible only with respect to
that declared execution boundary. Such differences are not treated as subjectivity, but as the
consequence of evaluating different declared execution boundaries. In one embodiment, the
Scope Lens identifier and associated run configuration are carried forward into downstream
normalized representations and output artifacts to ensure that any result is reproducible
only with respect to the declared G.

Calculation of the “Zero Bit Gap”
In one embodiment, the Alignment and Scoring Core computes a bounded difference metric,
referred to herein as the Zero Bit Gap, to quantify structural distance between a normalized
representation of a first artifact and a normalized representation of a second artifact under
a common Scope Lens and run configuration. The Zero Bit Gap is defined with respect to a
computable description length proxy over the system canonical Normal Form, and represents
the incremental structural information required, under the declared normalization regime,
to transform one normalized representation into another.
In this embodiment, the Zero Bit Gap is computed as:
Gap(T | S) = K(NF(T)) − K(M(NF(S)))
In this expression, NF(T) denotes the canonical Normal Form produced by the Normal￾ization Core for a first artifact T, and NF(S) denotes the canonical Normal Form produced
for a second artifact S. The function M(·) denotes an alignment mapping generated by
the Alignment and Scoring Core that maps elements of NF(S) into correspondence with ele￾ments of NF(T) under the Scope Lens constraints. The term M(NF(S)) denotes the mapped
Normal Form of the second artifact after applying the alignment mapping.
The function K(·) denotes an implementation defined, computable descriptive length
proxy over the serialized Normal Form. In one embodiment, K(·) is the bit length or byte
length of a canonical serialization of the Normal Form under a declared cost model. In
another embodiment, K(·) is computed as a compressed length produced by a specified
compressor over the canonical serialization, or as a weighted edit cost length under an ex￾plicitly defined cost function. In all cases, K(·) is a concrete, computable measure defined by
15

the run configuration and is not intended to represent uncomputable theoretical complexity
measures.
In one embodiment, a Zero Bit Gap is reported when the computed structural distance
between NF(T) and M(NF(S)) is at or below a configured tolerance threshold ε defined
in the Scope Lens or run configuration. In such cases, the system determines that the
normalized representations are structurally correspondent under the declared Scope Lens
and cost model, and may output one or more structured artifacts, such as a correspondence
map, a difference graph, a score report, or a certificate bound to the Scope Lens identifier
and run configuration.

Normalization Complexity Metric (“MBN Cycle”)
In one embodiment, the system computes a normalization workload metric to quantify com￾putational work performed by the normalization pipeline independent of wall clock time

In one embodiment, the Normalization Complexity Metric CMBN is computed as a weighted

CMBN = w1 · Natoms + w2 · Dstruct + w3 · Isolver
In this expression, Natoms denotes the number of extracted Operational Atoms or nor￾malized atom instances admitted into the pipeline after application of the Ingestion Filter
and Scope Lens constraints. The term Dstruct denotes a declared structural depth measure of

iterations executed to reach the canonical Normal Form under the declared cost model and
termination criteria.
The weights w1, w2, and w3 are configuration parameters declared in the run configuration
or Scope Lens and may be chosen to reflect relative computational cost contributions in
