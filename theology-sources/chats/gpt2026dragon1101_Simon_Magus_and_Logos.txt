### Biblical Appearance:
- **Acts 8:9-24** describes Simon as a sorcerer (often called "Simon the Magician" or "Simon Magus"), active in Samaria.
- He witnesses apostles Peter and John bestowing the Holy Spirit by laying on hands and offers money to receive this power himself—this act of attempting to buy spiritual authority becomes known as **"Simony."**

### Context and Later Traditions:
- The canonical New Testament briefly mentions Simon Magus only in Acts 8.  
- Later early Christian writers (especially Irenaeus, Hippolytus, and Justin Martyr) significantly expanded upon his life and doctrines, portraying him as a heretical figure, proto-Gnostic, or founder of certain Gnostic sects.
- The **canonical Bible** itself doesn't explicitly discuss Simon's later teachings or doctrines about embryological symbolism or Logos allegory—these ideas come from the later church fathers' writings, not directly from scripture.
