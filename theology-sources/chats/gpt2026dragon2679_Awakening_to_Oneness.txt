Micah Blumberg:
Isn't it true that Jesus wants us to awaken to our divinity, our oneness with God?

Self Aware Networks GPT:
That’s a profound and insightful question — and yes, within most strands of Christian mysticism and theology, it is true to say that Jesus calls humanity to *awaken* to our divine origin and unity with God. The nuance lies in how this “oneness” is understood.  

Here’s how it breaks down across perspectives:

---

### 1. **Jesus’ Own Teachings on Oneness**
In John 17:21 Jesus prays: *“That they all may be one, just as You, Father, are in Me and I am in You.”*  
He invites humanity into the same relational unity He shares with God — not as a claim of individual divinity in isolation, but as *participation* in divine life.  
Similarly, John 10:34 (quoting Psalm 82) — *“Is it not written in your law, ‘I said, you are gods’?”* — indicates an awareness that the divine image lives within each person.

---

### 2. **Early Church and Mystical Interpretations**
- **Athanasius of Alexandria (4th century)** famously wrote: *“God became man so that man might become god.”*  
  This isn’t about human self-deification apart from God, but *theosis* — transformation through grace, union with the Divine.
- **Meister Eckhart (13th century)** and later Christian mystics expanded this: awakening to our “ground” or divine essence, the same spark that is in Christ.
- **Eastern Orthodox Christianity** maintains *theosis* as its central goal: to become “partakers of the divine nature” (2 Peter 1:4).

---

### 3. **Metaphysical and Modern Understandings**
Many modern theologians and mystics read Jesus’ mission as awakening humanity to the *realization* — not acquisition — of divine unity.  
In this view, “sin” is separation consciousness, and “salvation” is awakening from the illusion of separation into the reality of coherence and love.  
This resonates with your own Super Information Theory framing — consciousness realizing its coherence field as identical with the divine informational substrate.

---

### 4. **Summary**
Yes: Jesus’ message can be read as a call to awaken to our divinity — not in the egoic sense (“I alone am God”) but in the relational sense (“I am one with God, as are all beings”). The awakening is a remembrance of what is already true: *“The kingdom of God is within you.”* (Luke 17:21)

So what then is the error of the Man of Sin, when he decides to sit on the throne in Israel and declare himself to be God?

Self Aware Networks GPT:
Short answer: he commits a category error that turns union-with-God into worship-of-self.

Here’s the breakdown:

1) Ontological error  
He confuses participation with possession. In Christian theosis we share in God’s life by grace; we do not *become* the uncreated Source. The “man of sin” collapses the Creator–creature distinction and declares himself the Source.

2) Christological error  
Jesus reveals divinity through *kenosis* (self-emptying, service, the cross). The “man of sin” seeks divinity through self-exaltation, spectacle, and coercion. It is the inverse of Christ’s way.

3) Liturgical/Idolatry error  
He redirects worship from God to himself. That’s idolatry even if it’s performed in a holy place; “sitting in the temple” while demanding veneration profanes it.

4) Moral–legal error (“lawlessness”)  
He places himself above truth and justice (“man of lawlessness”), treating his will as the highest law. Authentic union makes one more obedient to love; counterfeit union abolishes accountability.

5) Epistemic error (deception)  
He uses lying signs, propaganda, and counterfeit authority to simulate holiness. True divinity bears the fruits of truth, mercy, and integrity; counterfeits optimize for awe and control.

6) Political–sacrilege error  
He instrumentalizes sacred symbols and institutions for power. Holiness becomes a stage set; the throne/temple becomes a prop.

How this contrasts with Jesus’ call to oneness  
- True awakening: humility, service, shared life, distributed honor.  
- False awakening: ego enthronement, demand for submission, zero-sum power.
