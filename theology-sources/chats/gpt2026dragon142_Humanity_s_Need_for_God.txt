Why does the bible think that humanity is doomed to failure without god? I'm trying to understand what is the goal or reason or incentive for mankind to have a relationship with God?

In the Bible, the idea that humanity is "doomed to failure without God" stems from several core theological themes. These are rooted in the nature of humanity, the purpose of creation, and the relationship between God and humankind. Here are a few key ideas to help clarify this perspective:

### 1. **The Fall and Original Sin**
   The Bible teaches that humanity was originally created in harmony with God. However, in the story of Adam and Eve in Genesis, humanity fell from that state of grace by disobeying God's command (Genesis 3). This is known as **original sin**, which created a separation between humans and God. The Bible suggests that without God's guidance and presence, humans are naturally inclined to sin, leading to moral failure, suffering, and death.

   **Reason for Relationship with God**: Without reconciliation with God, humanity remains in a fallen, broken state. The relationship with God is seen as a way to restore what was lost through the Fall.

### 2. **Dependence on God for Righteousness**
   Many biblical texts, especially in the New Testament, emphasize that humans cannot achieve true righteousness or salvation on their own. In the Book of Romans, Paul argues that "all have sinned and fall short of the glory of God" (Romans 3:23). The Bible often depicts human efforts to be righteous without God as futile because of humanity's fallen nature.

   **Reason for Relationship with God**: God is seen as the source of righteousness and holiness. The Bible teaches that through faith in God and acceptance of His grace (particularly through Jesus Christ in the New Testament), people can be redeemed and transformed.

### 3. **God as the Source of Life and Purpose**
   The Bible presents God not just as a moral authority but as the very source of life and meaning. In the Book of John, Jesus says, "I am the way, the truth, and the life" (John 14:6). The idea is that without a relationship with God, humanity lacks true purpose and fulfillment. The Book of Ecclesiastes, for example, describes life as ultimately meaningless ("vanity of vanities") without God.

   **Reason for Relationship with God**: A relationship with God gives life meaning, direction, and fulfillment. It connects humanity to a divine purpose beyond mere existence.

### 4. **Salvation and Eternal Life**
   Central to Christian belief is the idea that humanity is in need of salvation, and this can only be achieved through a relationship with God. The New Testament teaches that through Jesus' death and resurrection, people are offered a path to eternal life. This is a gift of grace, not something humans can earn (Ephesians 2:8-9).

   **Reason for Relationship with God**: The relationship with God is seen as the means by which humans can receive eternal life and escape the consequences of sin, including spiritual death and separation from God.

### 5. **Reconciliation and Restoration**
   The Bible’s narrative is one of reconciliation—restoring the broken relationship between God and humanity. God's love for humanity is portrayed as unconditional, but the separation caused by sin must be addressed. This is why in Christianity, Jesus is seen as the mediator between humanity and God (1 Timothy 2:5), providing a way for people to be reconciled to God.

   **Reason for Relationship with God**: The goal is reconciliation—restoring the intended relationship between humans and their Creator, allowing them to experience the fullness of life that God originally intended.

### Summary of the Incentive for Relationship with God:
- **To overcome sin and moral failure** that humans cannot conquer on their own.
- **To find true righteousness and meaning** through God’s grace.
- **To receive salvation and eternal life**, escaping spiritual death.
- **To restore the intended relationship** between humans and God, living in harmony and purpose.

In essence, the Bible’s view is that without God, humanity is spiritually incomplete and unable to achieve the ultimate purpose for which it was created. The relationship with God is seen as a means of redemption, transformation, and fulfillment of humanity’s divine potential.
