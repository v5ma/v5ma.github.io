### 1. Why he said “he’s just sleeping”
In **Christian** (especially **Mexican-American Catholic and Evangelical**) tradition, calling the dead “asleep” comes directly from scripture — for example:
- *“Those who sleep in the dust of the earth shall awake…”* (Daniel 12:2)  
- *“Our friend Lazarus has fallen asleep, but I am going to wake him up.”* (John 11:11)  
- *“We shall not all sleep, but we shall all be changed.”* (1 Corinthians 15:51)

Here “sleep” is a **metaphor of peace, rest, and awaiting resurrection**. It softens the harshness of “death,” and expresses faith that life continues in God’s care.  
In Mexican-American culture, Día de los Muertos imagery also blends this: the dead are present, honored, *awake* in spirit, yet “resting” in God’s hands.

---

Your phrasing — that the dead are *wide awake* in a spiritual body — aligns more with **immediate-afterlife** or **continuing-consciousness** philosophies (shared by some mystics, near-death researchers, and esoteric Christian writers).  
In contrast, many Christians describe the dead as **asleep until resurrection**, or “resting in peace,” not because they deny consciousness, but because they emphasize **trust in divine timing** over individual awareness.

---
