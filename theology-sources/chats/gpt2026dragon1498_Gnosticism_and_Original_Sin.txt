The Gnostics did not teach that human beings are morally depraved sinners cut off from God in the way that later Catholic theology describes “original sin.”  Rather, they posited a transcendent, unknowable Father above a lesser, ignorant creator-god (the Demiurge) who fashioned the material universe as a flawed or even hostile prison.  Humanity was thought to possess a divine spark trapped in matter, and the core problem to be overcome was cosmic ignorance, not inherited guilt.  Many Gnostic texts explicitly emphasize illusion and enlightenment over sin and repentance citeturn1view0turn0search3.

The doctrine of original sin, by contrast, was formulated in the late fourth and early fifth centuries by Augustine of Hippo within what became the Catholic tradition.  Augustine argued that Adam’s disobedience introduced a hereditary guilt transmitted through concupiscence, rendering all his descendants inherently sinful.  This theology of inherited guilt is absent from Gnostic systems and only emerges centuries after the earliest Gnostic movements citeturn2search0.

Isaiah 53:10 falls within the “Servant Song” of Deutero-Isaiah, a prophetic poem dating from the Babylonian exile that portrays a righteous servant who suffers vicariously for others.  The phrase “it pleased the Lord to crush him” expresses the paradox of God accomplishing salvation through the servant’s sacrificial suffering, not a perverse delight in torture.  Jewish interpreters understood this as the servant making an offering for sin and then being vindicated, and Christian tradition reads it messianically, as foreshadowing Christ’s atoning death citeturn3search0.

Aaron Abke’s suggestion that Gnostics believed their god gleefully demanded the ritual torture and murder of a son figure distorts both Gnostic theology and the Isaiah text.  Gnostic writings never celebrate divine cruelty; they lament the material world’s suffering and offer gnosis as escape.  The supreme Father is wholly transcendent and above suffering, and the Demiurge is more ignorant than malevolent.  No genuine Gnostic source endorses a god pleased by violence against a savior citeturn1view0turn0search3.
