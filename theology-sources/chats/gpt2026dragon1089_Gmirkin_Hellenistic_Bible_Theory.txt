### Key Points of Russell Gmirkin’s Argument:

1. **Late Composition**:

3. **Greek Mythology and Bible**:  
   He sees the Hebrew Bible as reflecting a conscious attempt by Jewish scholars to engage in dialogue with, respond to, or rival Greek mythologies and historiographical traditions.

4. **Use of Hellenistic Historiography**:  
   He suggests the biblical authors modeled their narratives and genealogies on Greek historiographical methods, particularly those of Berossus (Babylonian priest who wrote in Greek) and Manetho (Egyptian priest who also wrote Greek histories).

### Implications of His Theory:
- Challenges traditional dating (that biblical texts, especially the Pentateuch, originated centuries earlier).
- Suggests that biblical monotheism, laws, and cosmology were at least partly shaped by Greek philosophical influences.
- Repositions the Hebrew Bible within a broader, cross-cultural context involving Greek, Egyptian, and Babylonian intellectual traditions.

### Scholarly Reception:
- **Controversial and debated**: While many mainstream scholars accept some Greek influence in later biblical texts (especially during the Persian or post-exilic periods), Gmirkin’s dating of the entire Pentateuch to the third century BCE is widely contested and viewed as radical by traditional biblical scholars.

Russell Gmirkin thus offers a compelling but highly contested perspective on biblical origins, rooted in cross-cultural literary analysis and historiographical studies of the Hellenistic period.
