Rage Against the Machine (RATM) aligns with Gnosticism in some thematic ways, particularly in their critique of oppressive systems and their call for awakening, but there are key differences in focus and philosophy.

### **Alignment with Gnosticism**
1. **Rejection of the Demiurge (Oppressive Systems)**
   - Gnosticism teaches that the world is ruled by an ignorant, oppressive deity (the Demiurge) who keeps humanity trapped in material ignorance.
   - RATM’s lyrics often focus on political and economic oppression, portraying governments, corporations, and media as forces that deceive and control the masses—akin to the Demiurge and Archons in Gnostic thought.

2. **Liberation Through Knowledge (Revolutionary Awareness)**
   - In Gnosticism, salvation comes through gnosis (hidden knowledge) that allows individuals to see through the illusion of the material world.
   - RATM’s music functions as a kind of modern gnosis, aiming to awaken people to systemic corruption, urging them to resist and overthrow it.

3. **The Illusion of Freedom (False Reality)**
   - Gnostics argue that the material world is a prison, and true reality lies beyond it.
   - RATM critiques the illusion of freedom in capitalist and authoritarian societies, showing how systems maintain control even when they appear to offer choice (e.g., “Land of the Free? Whoever told you that is your enemy” from *Know Your Enemy*).

### **Where RATM and Gnosticism Differ**
1. **Material vs. Spiritual Liberation**
   - Gnosticism is ultimately about transcending the material world, while RATM is focused on changing it.
   - RATM’s message is about direct political action, whereas Gnosticism often views the world as beyond saving.

2. **The Nature of Evil**
   - Gnosticism sees the material world as inherently flawed because it was created by the Demiurge.
   - RATM doesn’t necessarily view existence itself as corrupt, just the power structures that have taken control.

3. **Who is the Enemy?**
   - In Gnosticism, the enemy is metaphysical—the Demiurge and the spiritual ignorance it imposes.
   - RATM’s enemy is more concrete—governments, police, corporations, and capitalism.

### **Conclusion**
Rage Against the Machine’s philosophy can be seen as a kind of **political Gnosticism**, where oppressive systems function like the Demiurge and the Archons, and revolutionaries act as the bringers of gnosis. However, they are more focused on real-world action rather than spiritual transcendence. If one were to synthesize their message with Gnosticism fully, it would lean toward a **radical, liberation-focused form of Gnosticism**, where both political and spiritual awakening are needed to escape the “matrix” of oppression.
