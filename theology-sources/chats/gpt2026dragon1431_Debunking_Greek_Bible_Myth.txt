The claim that *all* old Jewish Bibles descend from a **Greek version from the 7th century A.D.** is **false** and reflects a misunderstanding of both Jewish textual history and biblical translation.

### Here's why this is incorrect:

---

### 1. **Hebrew Texts Precede Greek Translations**
- The **Hebrew Bible (Tanakh)** was composed in **Hebrew** (with some Aramaic) between approximately **1200 BCE and 100 BCE**, centuries before the emergence of Greek versions.
- The **Dead Sea Scrolls**, discovered at Qumran and dated to **200 BCE – 70 CE**, include Hebrew biblical texts that **predate** any 7th-century Greek version. These are **direct evidence** of a pre-Greek Hebrew tradition.

---

### 2. **The Septuagint Is Greek, But Not Jewish-Canonical**
- The **Septuagint (LXX)** is a **Greek translation** of the Hebrew Bible, begun in the 3rd century BCE in Alexandria. It was used by **Greek-speaking Jews** and later **early Christians**, but it **never replaced** the Hebrew text in **Rabbinic Judaism**.
- After the 1st century CE, the Jewish community increasingly **distanced itself from the Septuagint**, favoring Hebrew and Aramaic texts instead.

---

### 3. **Masoretic Text Is Hebrew and Older Than the 7th Century Greek Claim**
- The **Masoretic Text (MT)** is the authoritative Hebrew text in Rabbinic Judaism. The **Aleppo Codex** (10th century CE) and **Leningrad Codex** (1008 CE) are complete versions, but the **Masoretic tradition** dates back to at least the **2nd century CE**, with much older roots in pre-Christian manuscripts.
- Again, **Dead Sea Scrolls** confirm many of these Hebrew traditions well before the alleged "7th-century Greek" point.

---

### 4. **Samaritan Pentateuch and Targums Provide Additional Hebrew Lineages**
- The **Samaritan Pentateuch** is a version of the Torah in ancient Hebrew script, **independent** of the later Masoretic tradition, and diverging in some content. It shows that **multiple Hebrew lineages** of the Bible existed.
- **Aramaic Targums** (translations of the Hebrew Bible used in synagogue readings) further prove the centrality of Hebrew texts and their interpretation **within Judaism**, without reliance on Greek.

---

### Summary
**Claim**: All old Jewish Bibles descend from a Greek version from the 7th century A.D.  
**Reality**:  
- Hebrew Bibles are **much older** than any Greek version.
- Jewish tradition is based on **Hebrew** texts, not Greek.
- Greek versions like the Septuagint were used by some Jews but ultimately **rejected** in Rabbinic Judaism.
- Archaeological finds (like the Dead Sea Scrolls) **prove the existence of Hebrew Bibles centuries before the 7th century A.D.**
