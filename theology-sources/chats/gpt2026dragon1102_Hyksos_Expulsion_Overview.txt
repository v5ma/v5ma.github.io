The **Hyksos Expulsion** refers to the historical event in ancient Egypt around **1550 BCE**, during which the Egyptian rulers drove out the **Hyksos**, a foreign dynasty that had ruled over Lower Egypt (the northern part of Egypt, including the Nile Delta) for roughly a century (approximately 1650–1550 BCE).  

### Background:
- The **Hyksos**, meaning "**rulers of foreign lands**," were predominantly Semitic-speaking peoples from the Levant (modern-day Syria-Palestine region).
- Around **1650 BCE**, they settled in the Nile Delta and gradually established political dominance. The period of Hyksos rule is known as the **Second Intermediate Period** of Egypt.

### Reasons for Expulsion:
- **Cultural and political tensions** emerged as Egyptians resented foreign rule.
- Hyksos brought advanced military technology to Egypt, notably the **horse-drawn chariot**, improved bows, and bronze weaponry, which Egyptians later adopted.
- Rising nationalistic sentiment and political ambition in Upper Egypt (the southern region) led to rebellion and resistance.

### Key Figures:
- **Seqenenre Tao (Taa)** initiated early resistance against the Hyksos but was killed in battle.
- His son, **Kamose**, continued the campaign, successfully pushing the Hyksos back towards the Delta region.
- Eventually, **Ahmose I**, brother of Kamose, led decisive military campaigns that expelled the Hyksos completely.

### Result:
- The Hyksos were expelled from Egypt around **1550 BCE**. Ahmose I established the **18th Dynasty**, initiating the prosperous and powerful **New Kingdom** era.
- Egypt reunified under indigenous rule, embarking on a period of territorial expansion, military strength, and cultural flourishing.

### Significance:
- This expulsion marked the beginning of Egypt’s golden age—the **New Kingdom**—characterized by powerful pharaohs like **Hatshepsut**, **Thutmose III**, **Amenhotep III**, **Akhenaten**, and **Ramesses II**.
- It demonstrated Egypt’s resilience and ability to integrate foreign technology and methods, transforming Egypt into a formidable empire in the ancient Near East.

In short, the **Hyksos Expulsion** was the pivotal moment that restored Egyptian unity and set the stage for one of the most prosperous eras in Egyptian history.

Yes, Ancient Egypt's historical records document multiple periods of significant weakening, instability, and disruption, often marked by societal collapse, internal conflict, foreign invasions, or environmental disasters. Here are some key historical examples:

- **Cause:** Collapse of the Old Kingdom.
- **Characteristics:**
  - Central authority collapsed.
  - Severe drought and famine, recorded on inscriptions (e.g., the famine depicted on the tomb of Ankhtifi).
  - Egypt fragmented into competing regional powers.
- **Historical Impact:**
  - Marked a period of chaos lasting about a century until reunification under Mentuhotep II, who initiated the Middle Kingdom (~2050 BCE).

### 2. **Second Intermediate Period (~1650–1550 BCE): Hyksos Period**
- **Cause:** Hyksos invasion and foreign occupation.
- **Characteristics:**
  - Egypt split: foreign Hyksos rulers dominated the north, Egyptians ruled Upper Egypt in the south.
  - Loss of autonomy and national pride.
- **Historical Impact:**
  - This was perceived by Egyptians as a humiliation, remembered later as a trauma.
  - Eventually led to the expulsion of the Hyksos, restoration of Egyptian independence, and ushered in the New Kingdom, Egypt’s imperial golden age.

### Particularly Relevant: The Late Bronze Age Collapse (circa 1200 BCE)
- Although Ramesses II’s reign (19th Dynasty) was stable, after his death and during the reign of his successors, Egypt faced significant destabilization and decline:
  - **Invasions and raids:** Particularly the "Sea Peoples," causing widespread destruction.
  - **Internal issues:** Economic stress, famine, administrative failures.
  - **Loss of territories:** Egypt lost many lands, including holdings in Canaan.
- **Historical Records:**  
  - Recorded in inscriptions at Medinet Habu under Ramesses III (c.1186–1155 BCE), depicting battles with foreign invaders and clearly stating Egypt faced severe threats and disruptions.

### **Documented Great Weakening:**
Ancient Egyptians openly recorded times of trouble, but typically framed these events as periods of chaos that were later overcome (e.g., intermediate periods and invasions). Such events include:

| Period                 | Date              | Description / Weakening Event                         |
|------------------------|-------------------|-------------------------------------------------------|
| **First Intermediate Period** | 2181–2055 BCE  | Collapse, famine, drought, decentralization |
| **Hyksos Rule (Second Intermediate Period)** | 1650–1550 BCE | Foreign rulers dominating Lower Egypt, seen as national humiliation |
| **End of New Kingdom (Late Bronze Age Collapse)** | ~1200–1070 BCE | Decline under invasions by "Sea Peoples," internal decline, economic collapse. |
| **Third Intermediate Period** | 1069–664 BCE   | Egypt fractured again into multiple kingdoms, weakened significantly. |
