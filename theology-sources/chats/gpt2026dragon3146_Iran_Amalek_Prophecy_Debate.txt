That sounds like a paraphrase of a Jewish apocalyptic motif, but not a precise standard quote as written.

The best-known version is from Yalkut Shimoni on Isaiah, where a midrashic passage says that in the year when the Messiah is revealed, the king of Persia will provoke an Arab king, and the nations will be thrown into turmoil. That passage is often brought up in modern end-times discussions because people map Persia to Iran. But that is not the same thing as a clean line saying, "At the end of days Persia will be your implacable enemy = Gog."

There are really three layers that often get collapsed together.

First, "Persia" in classical Jewish texts is the historical Persian power, which modern readers often map onto Iran.

Second, Gog and Magog are a separate apocalyptic framework, mainly from Ezekiel 38-39, later interpreted in many ways. Some interpreters try to identify modern nations with Gog's coalition.

Third, later commentators and modern teachers often fuse these themes, so Persia becomes one of the end-times enemies, or even part of a Gog-like alignment.

So yes, there is a real tradition in which Persia appears as a major end-times antagonist. But no, the exact formula you wrote is not the standard canonical wording I would rely on without checking the original source. It sounds more like a compressed sermon version or interpretive gloss than a direct textual citation.

Also, "Persia = Gog" is stronger than many sources actually say. In Ezekiel, Persia is explicitly listed as one of the nations associated with Gog's coalition, not necessarily identical with Gog himself. That distinction matters. In the text, Gog is the chief figure, while Persia is one of the allied peoples.

So the careful formulation would be this: some Jewish apocalyptic traditions present Persia as a major end-times enemy, and Ezekiel places Persia among the nations aligned with Gog. But saying "Persia is Gog" is an interpretive simplification, not the most exact reading.
