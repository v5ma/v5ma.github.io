When Jesus speaks of His oneness with God, such as in John 10:30 where He says, "I and the Father are one," He is affirming a profound unity at the heart of the divine relationship. The Christian tradition, particularly in its mystical strains, interprets this as not only descriptive of Jesus’ unique relationship with the Father but also as an invitation extended to believers. When you are invited to bring Jesus into your heart, it implies participation in that same divine unity—an invitation to share, in a measure appropriate to human creatures, that oneness and direct communion with God.

From the perspective of the "first person" encounter with God, this is significant. The three “perspectives” of experiencing God can be broadly understood as:

**1. First Person (I):**  
This perspective refers to the intimate, direct awareness of God within oneself, beyond the sense of separation. Rather than relating to God as an external “Other,” one experiences a sort of interior union. Mystical traditions in Christianity—such as those of Julian of Norwich, Meister Eckhart, and Teresa of Ávila—describe moments where personal identity is so surrendered into divine presence that the believer senses an immediate, unitive experience with God. This is the dimension where “Christ in you” (Colossians 1:27) is not just a theological concept, but a lived reality, blurring the boundaries between your being and the divine life dwelling within you.

**2. Second Person (You):**  
In the second person experience, God is encountered as the “Other,” a personal deity to whom you pray, with whom you converse, and whom you worship. The language of relationship—loving God, trusting God, obeying God—is central here. It’s the mode in which much of Christian prayer and devotion takes place, understanding God as a “You” that loves, guides, and saves us. When Jesus invites people to “follow me,” He is standing before them as a clearly distinct second-person figure they can love, trust, and learn from.

**3. Third Person (He/She/It):**  
The third person perspective finds God disclosed in the grandeur of creation, the harmony of the cosmos, the laws of nature, and the moral order underpinning life. Here, God is often spoken of as “He,” “She,” or “It,” a transcendent presence one observes and contemplates. In this mode, one might find God’s fingerprints everywhere in the universe—what theologians and believers sometimes call “natural revelation.”

---

**Connecting Jesus’ Oneness with God and Inviting Him into Your Heart:**  
When Jesus says, “I and the Father are one,” He reveals a divine intimacy in the very heart of God’s being. For believers, entering into relationship with Jesus isn’t merely about emulating His teachings or interacting with Him as an external figure. To “bring Jesus into your heart” suggests a shift toward a more immediate, first-person experience of the divine. It hints at participating in that unity from within, rather than observing it from without.

In this sense, the act of accepting Jesus into your heart can be understood as opening yourself to the first-person perspective of God. It doesn’t eliminate the valuable second-person relationship—where you speak to Jesus as “You”—nor does it dismiss the third-person understanding of God as the Creator and Sustainer you see reflected in nature. Rather, it complements these modes of experiencing God. Over time, a believer might cycle through or integrate all three perspectives: talking to God (second person), contemplating God in creation (third person), and ultimately, experiencing an inner resonance where “it is no longer I who live, but Christ who lives in me” (Galatians 2:20), reflecting the first-person perspective of unity.

**In summary:**  
Jesus’ oneness with the Father and the invitation to bring Him into your heart can indeed be seen as opening a pathway toward the first-person experience of God. This does not deny the other modes—relational and cosmic—but allows the believer to taste something of the divine unity from within, to be united with God in a personal, intimate, and transforming way.
