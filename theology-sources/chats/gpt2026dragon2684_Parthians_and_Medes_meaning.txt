3) Darius I (Hystaspes) anachronism hypothesis  
Some propose Daniel conflated a later, well-known “Darius” (Darius I, r. 522–486 BCE) with the earlier fall of Babylon. This would make “Darius the Mede” a retrospective literary/chronological smoothing.

• Cyrus was ethnically Persian but had deep Median ties (he overthrew his Median grandfather Astyages in 550 BCE and absorbed Media; his mother was traditionally said to be Median).

**Who they were (at a glance):**

- **Seqenenre Taa II** – Theban king who likely **began the revolt** against Hyksos rule; **died from severe cranial injuries**, as shown by medical imaging of his mummy. citeturn5search1  
- **Kamose** – **Successor to Seqenenre**, continued campaigns north against the Hyksos and south against Kush; his **Year 3 stelae** from Karnak are key sources; died before final victory. citeturn4search1  
- **Ahmose I** – **Younger relative (son of Seqenenre; brother/younger kinsman of Kamose)** who **finished the expulsion of the Hyksos** and founded the 18th Dynasty. citeturn0search8turn4search2

> **Why the ranges?** Second‑Intermediate/early‑New‑Kingdom dates rely on fitting regnal evidence (like “Year 3” of Kamose), radiocarbon studies, and later king lists; different scholarly chronologies (high/low) shift these reigns by a few decades. The **sequence is secure**, but **exact years** remain debated. citeturn4search2
