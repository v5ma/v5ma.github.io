# “Apocalypse of Weeks” (1 Enoch 93; 91) mapped to history (schematic)

1) **Week 1:** Enoch’s era (mythic pre-Flood).  
2) **Week 2:** Flood/Noah.  
3) **Week 3:** “Plant of righteousness” chosen → commonly linked to **Abraham** (c. 2nd millennium BCE).  
4) **Week 4:** “Visions/Law for all generations” → **Torah** (Sinai) / formation of Israel (literary memory; texts compiled much later).

- **“Exile” in Enoch:** refers to the **First Temple’s destruction and Babylonian deportations** (586 BCE), not Rome’s 70 CE event (that would be later in other apocalypses).

What is a Jubilee period?

Self Aware Networks GPT:
A **Jubilee period** (Hebrew *Yovel*) is a sacred time cycle in the Hebrew Bible—specifically in **Leviticus 25**—marking a reset of land, property, and social debts.

### Structure
- **Seven “weeks” of years:** 7 × 7 = 49 years.  
- The **50th year** is the **Jubilee** itself.  
  → It follows seven sabbatical cycles.

### What happens in a Jubilee year
1. **Rest for the land:** the soil lies fallow (no planting or harvesting, as in a sabbatical year).  
2. **Release:** slaves and debt-servants are freed.  
3. **Restoration:** hereditary lands return to their original families; long-term leases end.  
4. **Proclamation:** a trumpet (*shofar*) sounds on the Day of Atonement announcing “liberty throughout the land” (Lev 25 : 9–10).

### Symbolic meaning
- It expresses **divine ownership of the land** (“the land is mine; you are strangers and sojourners with me,” Lev 25 : 23).  
- Economically it prevents permanent inequality; spiritually it models **renewal and forgiveness**.

### Later and interpretive uses
- **Second-Temple groups (like Qumran)** used *jubilees* as **chronological blocks**—e.g., 10 jubilees = 490 years—to structure history and prophecy (cf. 11QMelchizedek).  
- **Apocalyptic and rabbinic writers** later treated the Jubilee cycle as a cosmic rhythm of history and judgment.

So:  
**1 Jubilee = 49 years (7 × 7)**, with the **50th year** serving as the sacred reset.
