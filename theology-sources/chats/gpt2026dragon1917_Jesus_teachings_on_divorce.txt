What did Jesus say about divorce

Self Aware Networks GPT:
Jesus spoke about divorce primarily in the Gospels of Matthew, Mark, and Luke. His teachings emphasized the seriousness of marriage and limited the acceptable reasons for divorce.

In **Matthew 19:3-9**, Jesus was asked whether it was lawful for a man to divorce his wife for any reason. He responded:

> “Have you not read that at the beginning the Creator ‘made them male and female,’ and said, ‘For this reason a man will leave his father and mother and be united to his wife, and the two will become one flesh’? So they are no longer two, but one flesh. Therefore what God has joined together, let no one separate.”

When asked why Moses permitted divorce, Jesus replied:

> “Moses permitted you to divorce your wives because your hearts were hard. But it was not this way from the beginning. I tell you that anyone who divorces his wife, except for sexual immorality, and marries another woman commits adultery.”

In **Mark 10:2-12**, Jesus gave a similar teaching, with no exception clause included. He said:

> “Anyone who divorces his wife and marries another woman commits adultery against her. And if she divorces her husband and marries another man, she commits adultery.”

In **Luke 16:18**, He stated:

> “Anyone who divorces his wife and marries another woman commits adultery, and the man who marries a woman divorced from her husband commits adultery.”

In essence, Jesus reaffirmed the sanctity of marriage as intended from creation, discouraged divorce except possibly in cases of sexual immorality (according to Matthew), and described remarriage after divorce as adultery.

His teaching was stricter than the prevailing norms of His time, particularly those that permitted divorce for almost any reason.

Self Aware Networks GPT:
Yes, you’re referencing an important moment where Jesus challenges strict legalism using the example of King David. This conversation occurs in **Mark 2:23-28** and **Matthew 12:1-8**.

When the Pharisees accused Jesus’ disciples of breaking the Sabbath law by picking grain, Jesus responded by citing **1 Samuel 21**, where David, when hungry, ate the consecrated bread reserved for priests. Jesus then made His key statement:

**Mark 2:27-28**:
> “The Sabbath was made for man, not man for the Sabbath. So the Son of Man is Lord even of the Sabbath.”

This means that the Sabbath, like other aspects of the Law, was created to serve and benefit humanity, not to enslave them.

In **Matthew 12:7**, He added:
> “If you had known what these words mean, ‘I desire mercy, not sacrifice,’ you would not have condemned the innocent.”

By invoking David’s example and this teaching, Jesus emphasized that compassion, human need, and mercy take precedence over rigid legal adherence. This principle underlies much of His approach to the Law: it exists to serve life, not to dominate or burden it.
