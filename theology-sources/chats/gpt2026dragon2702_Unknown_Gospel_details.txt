## What it is (and what survives)

- **Object**: Two tiny Greek papyrus fragments from Oxyrhynchus (Egypt), housed at the Bodleian Library (MS. Gr. th. e. 8 [P]). Paleographically they’re **late 3rd / early 4th century**. citeturn6view0  
- **Format**: The pieces come from a **large, paginated codex**, written in **two columns per page**. Visible page numbers include **139, 174, [1]76**, which shows how substantial the book once was. The **largest piece is ~6.3 × 13.1 cm**. citeturn8view0  
- **Extent**: What we have preserves the **tops of six pages** (139; [138/140]; 173; 174; 175; 176) split between two fragments. Because >30 pages separate the two pieces, **it’s not certain they came from the same work**, though that remains possible. citeturn3view0turn8view0  
- **First edition**: Published in *The Oxyrhynchus Papyri*, vol. 10 (1914) by Grenfell & Hunt. citeturn5view0

## What the fragments say (content in four short scenes)

The text is extremely fragmentary—no complete line survives—so any reconstructions are cautious. Still, four distinct passages can be discerned across the six preserved page‑tops: citeturn8view0

1. **A vision report** (p. 173, col. ii): the speaker says he was burdened; **“Jesus appeared in a vision”** and encourages him. No direct NT parallel. citeturn3view0turn8view0  
2. **A hostile interrogation about “new teaching/baptism”** (p. 174, col. i): someone challenges Jesus about “what new teaching” and “what new baptism” he proclaims (cf. the phrase “new teaching” in **Mark 1:27**, but the wording/situation differ). citeturn3view0turn8view0  
3. **Meal with sinners + the physician saying** (p. 175, col. ii): scribes, Pharisees, and priests are angry because Jesus **reclines among sinners**; he replies, **“Those who are healthy have no need of a physician …”**—a clear **parallel to Mark 2:17 // Luke 5:31**, but in a different verbal/narrative form. citeturn3view0turn8view0

## Independence from the canonical Gospels

- Editors and commentators note that **none of the distinctive (“redactional”) features** of the canonical evangelists are visible in the surviving lines; where parallels exist, the **phrasing and arrangement differ**, indicating **textual independence** rather than copying. Crossan classed it with **“sayings gospels”** (closer to a Q‑like collection than to Thomas) based on what little we can see. citeturn3view1turn6view0  
- Example: placing **“pray for your enemies”** next to **“whoever is not against you is for you”** is **non‑canonical sequencing**; likewise the **meal scene** uses distinct narration (“reclining in the midst of sinners”) before the physician proverb. citeturn3view0

- **Manuscript date**: late 3rd–early 4th century (paleography). citeturn6view0

## Why it matters (“so what?”)

1. **Independent Jesus tradition**: The two clearest overlaps—**Mark 2:17** and **Luke 9:50**—appear in **non‑canonical forms**, suggesting the author drew on **streams of Jesus tradition** that circulated **outside** (or alongside) our four Gospels. This supports the picture of **multiple, fluid collections of sayings and brief scenes** in the first two centuries. citeturn3view0turn6view0  
2. **Genre indicator**: With its **vision report** + **sayings cluster** + **mini‑controversy**, P.Oxy. 1224 looks like a **compact “ministry” or sayings‑oriented gospel**, not a passion‑narrative or infancy gospel. That aligns it conceptually with **Q‑type** collections (now lost) rather than with Thomas’s format—though the fragments are too scanty for certainty. citeturn8view0turn3view1  
3. **Codex scale**: Pagination into the **170s** implies a **substantial book**, reminding us that **non‑canonical Jesus books** could be **lengthy** and **widely used**, not just marginal scraps or amulets. citeturn8view0

## Cautions & open questions

- **Fragmentary state**: No complete line survives; **reconstructions are highly conjectural**. We must be careful not to over‑systematize the work’s theology or structure. citeturn3view1  
- **Unity**: Since the numbered pages are widely separated, it is **uncertain** whether both fragments are from **the same composition** or from **different works bound together**. Either way, the **form of the sayings** still witnesses to a **non‑canonical tradition stream**. citeturn8view0
