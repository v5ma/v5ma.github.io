The *Negative Confession*—also known as the *Declaration of Innocence*—is one of the most profound spiritual texts in ancient Egyptian religion. It appears in the *Book of the Dead*, a guidebook placed in tombs to help the deceased navigate the afterlife.

At the heart of the Negative Confession is a ritual moment in the *Hall of Ma’at*, where the soul stands before 42 divine judges. The soul does **not** ask for mercy. Instead, it **asserts moral purity**—not through boasting, but through alignment with the cosmic order.

Each line begins with:

These are statements of what the soul has *not* done to violate Ma’at. Examples include:

- “I have not lied.”  
- “I have not stolen.”  
- “I have not slain men and women.”  
- “I have not caused pain.”  
- “I have not polluted myself.”  
- “I have not been angry without cause.”  
- “I have not shut my ears to truth.”  
- “I have not spoken falsely.”  
- “I have not defiled the wife of a man.”  
- “I have not diminished the offerings of the gods.”  

There are 42 such declarations—each corresponding to a *nome* (region) of Egypt and its associated deity. This isn’t a casual checklist; it’s a cosmic mirror. The soul is proclaiming that it lived in harmony with *Ma’at*, not just in action but in spirit.
