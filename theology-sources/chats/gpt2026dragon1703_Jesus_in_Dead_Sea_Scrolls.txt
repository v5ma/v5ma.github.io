Jesus Christ is **not explicitly mentioned by name** in the Dead Sea Scrolls.

### Key facts:

1. **No direct mention of “Jesus,” “Yeshua,” “Christ,” or “Messiah Jesus” appears in the Dead Sea Scrolls.**
   The scrolls were written between approximately **250 BCE and 50 CE**, overlapping with Jesus's lifetime but primarily reflecting **sectarian Jewish thought before and around his time**, particularly that of the **Essenes**, a separatist apocalyptic group.

2. **Some figures in the Scrolls resemble messianic expectations that align loosely with Jesus’s portrayal**, such as:
   - A **Teacher of Righteousness** who suffered at the hands of a “Wicked Priest.”
   - Prophecies of **two messiahs**: one priestly and one kingly.
   - Themes of **end-times, resurrection, forgiveness, and spiritual renewal**.

   However, these are **not references to Jesus himself**, but rather **messianic archetypes** present in Second Temple Judaism, of which Christianity later became one trajectory.

3. **Why this matters:**
   Some fringe or speculative theories (often from sensational books or internet claims) try to argue that Jesus or early Christians were Essenes or that the scrolls “prove” early Christianity. However, **scholars do not support this view**. While **thematic parallels exist**, the Dead Sea Scrolls **predate Christian writings and do not contain Christian doctrine**.

4. **Overlap:**
   Concepts in both the Scrolls and the New Testament include:
   - Baptismal purity rituals (e.g., mikveh-style cleansings).
   - Apocalyptic judgment and resurrection.
   - Community of the elect or “sons of light.”
   - Scriptural reinterpretation for an inner circle.

These shared elements reflect the **broader religious environment** of the time rather than **proof of direct connection**.
