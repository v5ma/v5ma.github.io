Here's what we have from that period and shortly after:

---

### **1. Paul’s Death – Traditional View**
**No canonical New Testament text** describes Paul’s death. However:

- **The Acts of the Apostles** ends with Paul under house arrest in Rome, awaiting trial before Caesar. It does not say what happens next.
- The **earliest reference** to Paul’s death comes from **1 Clement** (written c. 96 CE), traditionally attributed to Clement of Rome. He writes:

  > “Through envy Paul also obtained the reward of patience… having taught righteousness unto the whole world, and reached the furthest bounds of the West…” (*1 Clement* 5:5–7)

  This implies Paul **died a martyr**, possibly in Spain or Rome.

- Later tradition (from the **2nd century on**) consistently holds that **Paul was beheaded in Rome** during the persecution of Christians under **Emperor Nero** (c. 64–68 CE). This appears in:
  - **Eusebius’ *Ecclesiastical History*** (early 4th century),
  - **Acts of Paul** (late 2nd century apocryphal text).

---

### **2. Simon Magus’s Death – Apocryphal and Legendary**

**Simon Magus** is a more enigmatic figure, and **his death is not mentioned in canonical texts**. The few sources that address it are **legendary**, found in **2nd- and 3rd-century texts**, particularly in **anti-heretical Christian writings**.

- **Acts of Peter** (late 2nd century):  
  This apocryphal text presents Simon Magus in Rome as a **sorcerer opposing Peter**. It describes his **attempt to ascend into the sky by magic**, during which **Peter prays**, and **Simon falls to his death**—a humiliating defeat showing the power of the true God over false magic.

- **Irenaeus**, in *Against Heresies* (c. 180 CE), refers to Simon as the **founder of Gnostic heresy**, but does **not describe his death**.

- **Hippolytus** (early 3rd century) adds more mythic details to Simon's philosophy but again relies on **hostile theological framing**, not firsthand accounts.

---

### **Historical Reality?**

- For **Paul**, martyrdom in Rome is plausible and widely attested by **early church tradition** even if not in contemporary Roman records.
- For **Simon Magus**, most of what survives is **polemical fiction**, shaped by efforts to **vilify him as the father of all heresies**. His death story is **symbolic**—a theological narrative more than a historical report.

---

### **Summary**

**No first-century document** describes the deaths of Paul or Simon Magus.  
- Paul’s death is **alluded to in 1 Clement (c. 96 CE)** and later developed into a consistent tradition of martyrdom in Rome.  
- Simon Magus’s death appears in **legendary anti-heretical texts** like *Acts of Peter* (late 2nd century), in which he dies **falling from the sky** during a magical stunt.

These accounts serve **ideological functions**—Paul as martyr, Simon as heretic—reflecting the **emerging power struggles** between proto-orthodox Christianity and rival sects in the second century.

Is Hebrew spoken much more today? Is it a revived language?

Self Aware Networks GPT:
Yes, **Hebrew is spoken much more today**, and it is one of the **most successful examples of a revived language in human history**.

---

### **1. Ancient to Liturgical Use**
- **Biblical Hebrew** was a spoken language in ancient Israel until around the **5th–3rd century BCE**, when **Aramaic** gradually replaced it as the dominant spoken language.
- Hebrew remained alive for **religious, literary, and scholarly purposes**, used in prayer, scripture, and rabbinic texts—but not as a daily spoken language.

---

### **2. The Revival of Spoken Hebrew**
- In the **late 19th century**, during the rise of **Zionism**, Jewish thinkers sought to reestablish a Jewish homeland—and with it, a unifying language.
- **Eliezer Ben-Yehuda**, often called the "father of modern Hebrew," played a key role. He and others:
  - Adapted biblical and medieval Hebrew for modern use.
  - Created **thousands of new words** to describe modern concepts.
  - Raised the first children to speak only Hebrew at home in modern times.

---

### **3. Modern Hebrew Today**
- **Modern Hebrew** (*Ivrit*) is the **official language of the State of Israel**.
- It is spoken as a **native language** by **millions of Israelis**.
- It is also taught and spoken by Jewish communities worldwide to varying degrees.
- While **Modern Hebrew** is based on ancient forms, it includes many **new constructions**, **loanwords**, and **influences** from Yiddish, Arabic, Russian, English, and other languages.

---

### **4. Is it a true revival?**
Yes—but it’s also a **reinvention**:
- It's **based on ancient roots** (especially Biblical and Mishnaic Hebrew),
- But its **grammar, vocabulary, and usage** have been modernized.
- **Linguists debate** whether it's a direct continuation or a new language built from old materials—but either way, the revival is extraordinary.

---

### **Summary**
Hebrew is indeed a **revived language**, spoken fluently by **millions today**. Once nearly extinct as a spoken tongue, it now thrives as a **living, national, and cultural language**—a rare example of successful linguistic resurrection in modern times.
