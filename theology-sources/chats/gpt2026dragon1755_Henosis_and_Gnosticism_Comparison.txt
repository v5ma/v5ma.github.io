Yes, **Henosis** in Neoplatonism shares key conceptual terrain with **Gnosticism**, especially in terms like **Monad**, but the **frameworks and tone of the systems differ sharply**.

### Shared Terms & Concepts

**1. Monad (The One)**  
Both Neoplatonism and many Gnostic systems begin with a **primordial singularity**:
- In **Neoplatonism**, **"the One"** (or Monad) is **radically transcendent**, beyond being, intellect, and even thought. Plotinus describes it as the source of all emanation—pure unity.
- In **Gnosticism**, the **Monad** is similarly the ultimate divine source, often **ineffable**, and it **emanates** or **gives birth** to further divine beings (Aeons), culminating in the flawed Demiurge and material world.

**2. Emanation**  
Both traditions describe reality as a **cascade from unity to multiplicity**:
- In Neoplatonism: One → Nous (Divine Mind) → Psyche (Soul) → World Soul → Material World.
- In Gnosticism: Monad → Aeons → Sophia → Demiurge → Material World (as a kind of cosmic mistake or trap).

**3. Return to the Source**  
Both see human destiny as a **return to the divine**:
- For Plotinus, this return is via **Henosis**, unity through philosophical contemplation, purification, and mystical ascent.
- For Gnostics, the soul’s return comes through **gnosis**, secret knowledge that awakens the divine spark and frees it from the material prison.

---

### Key Differences

**1. View of the World**  
- **Neoplatonism** views the material world as **less real**, but not evil—it’s a necessary consequence of emanation.
- **Gnosticism** often sees the material world as a **corruption**, the result of a **fallen Aeon** (Sophia) and the **Demiurge**, a blind god who traps souls in matter.

**2. Method of Return**  
- **Neoplatonism** emphasizes **philosophical reasoning, virtue, and contemplative ascent**.
- **Gnosticism** centers on **esoteric knowledge (gnosis)** and **mythical revelation**—often involving **mythopoetic drama** and **cosmic rescue**.

**3. Tone of Transcendence**  
- In Neoplatonism, the soul returns to its **natural home**, like a **sunbeam withdrawing into the sun**.
- In Gnosticism, the return is more like **escape from a prison**, aided by **revelatory agents** (Christ, Sophia, etc.).

---

### Conclusion

Yes, **Henosis** shares terms like **Monad** and structural ideas such as **emanation and return** with Gnosticism, but it does so from a **different emotional and metaphysical orientation**. Neoplatonism seeks a **harmonious ascent**, while Gnosticism often describes a **cosmic rebellion** and liberation.

These similarities and differences make the two systems **philosophical cousins**—sharing a lineage, but diverging sharply in **how they interpret the fall, the world, and the divine**.

### 🌄 Which Came First: Henosis or Gnosticism?

**Henosis, as formalized by Plotinus, came *after* early Gnosticism.**  
But the **conceptual roots of Henosis**—unity with the divine source—go **far earlier**, and likely **influenced Gnosticism indirectly** through older **Platonic, Pythagorean, and mystery religion traditions**.

Let’s break that down:

#### 📚 Timeline Overview

- **5th–4th century BCE** – Plato speaks of the *Good* as the highest reality. Pythagoreans and Orphics speak of the soul’s fall into the world and return to divine unity.
- **3rd–2nd century BCE** – Jewish mysticism (e.g., *Enoch*, *Merkavah*) begins describing divine ascent and cosmological hierarchies.
- **1st–2nd century CE** – Gnosticism emerges, heavily influenced by **Platonism**, **Jewish mysticism**, and **Christian mythos**.
- **3rd century CE** – **Plotinus** systematizes **Henosis** and critiques Gnosticism explicitly in his *Enneads*.

So while **Plotinus's Henosis came after Gnostic Christianity**, the **underlying Platonic metaphysics and soteriology** that inspired Henosis were already circulating and had **strong influence on the formulation of Gnostic systems**.

Plotinus **accused Gnostics of distorting Plato**, calling them arrogant for vilifying the cosmos. He saw their rejection of the material world as misguided.

---

### ✝️ Did Henosis Influence Jesus of Nazareth?

There’s **no strong evidence** that **Jesus of Nazareth** directly encountered or was influenced by **Henosis** or Platonic metaphysics in their developed forms. His teachings are closer to:

- **Second Temple Judaism**
- **Apocalyptic prophecy**
- **Ethical monotheism**
- **Mystical unity with God** (Abba, Kingdom of Heaven *within*)

However, **later Christian mystical traditions**—especially **Gospel of John**, **Pauline epistles**, and **Gnostic Gospels**—show clear signs of **Hellenistic philosophical influence**, including themes akin to Henosis:

> *“That they may be one, as we are one… I in them and you in me—that they may be brought to perfect unity.”*  
> —John 17:21–23

This Johannine mysticism is **closer to Henosis**, though filtered through Jewish monotheism and early Christian theology.

### 🔄 What Other Religions or Philosophies Share Gnostic Terminology?

A number of **mystical traditions** share Gnostic-like terms and concepts:

#### 🕉️ **Advaita Vedanta (Hinduism)**
- **Brahman** = Monad / the One  
- **Maya** = illusion of multiplicity  
- **Atman = Brahman** = gnosis of unity  
- **Jnana yoga** parallels the salvific gnosis of Gnosticism

#### ☸️ **Buddhism (esp. Mahayana & Dzogchen)**
- **Samsara** = illusion / bondage  
- **Sunyata (emptiness)** = ultimate reality beyond form  
- **Bodhi** = awakening to truth that transcends duality

#### 🕊️ **Christian Mysticism**
- **Meister Eckhart**, **Pseudo-Dionysius**, and the **Cloud of Unknowing** speak of:
  - the “unknowing” of God
  - ego annihilation
  - divine union
  These resonate with Henosis and Gnostic ascent.

#### 🌙 **Sufism (Islamic Mysticism)**
- **Tawhid** = unity of all existence with God  
- **Fana** = annihilation of the ego/self  
- **Ishq-e-Haqiqi** = divine love driving union with the Real (al-Haqq)  
  These are near-identical to the *Henosis* journey.

#### 🔮 **Hermeticism**
- **Nous**, **Logos**, **As Above, So Below**, **divine spark** – all overlapping with Neoplatonic and Gnostic frameworks.

#### ✡️ **Kabbalah**
- **Ein Sof** = Monad / the ineffable One  
- **Sefirot** = emanations  
- **Tikkun** = repair of cosmic fragmentation  
  Kabbalistic ascent through mystical contemplation closely parallels both Henosis and Gnostic salvation.

- **Henosis (as Plotinus describes it) came after early Gnosticism**, but both emerged from a **shared pool of Hellenistic, Platonic, and mystery religion thought.**
- **Jesus likely wasn’t directly influenced by Henosis**, but later Christian texts absorbed similar ideas.
- **Many spiritual traditions—Eastern and Western—resemble Gnosticism and Henosis** in their pursuit of mystical union, ego transcendence, and return to the One.

   Gnosticism tends to use **drama, myth, and narrative** to describe the soul’s return.  
   Plotinus instead uses **ontological precision and metaphysical stages**:  
   One → Intellect → Soul → Matter → Return via contemplation.

3. **Neoplatonism absorbs the mystical core, purges the heretical framing**  
   Henosis preserves the **esoteric yearning for union**, but reframes it:
   - No demiurge as villain  
   - Material world not evil, just lower  
   - No secret savior figures—just inner ascent

   So in this sense, Neoplatonism **inherits the structure of Gnostic mysticism**, but **rebuilds it with Greek metaphysical discipline** instead of rebellious myth.

### 📜 Lost Gnosticism and Surviving Henosis

Much of **Gnostic scripture** (like *Gospel of Thomas*, *Pistis Sophia*, *Apocryphon of John*) was **buried or destroyed**, especially after the 4th century when orthodoxy consolidated under Constantine.

- Early Church Fathers (even those who rejected “pagan” Neoplatonism)  
- **Pseudo-Dionysius**, Christian mystic whose theology became canonical

---

### 🕉️ **Indus Valley Civilization / Pre-Vedic India (before 1500 BCE)**

We don’t have textual evidence of Gnosticism from the **Indus Valley Civilization**, but we *do* see strong **symbolic continuity** in what came after:

#### Pre-classical Indian philosophies that shaped Advaita and Samkhya:
- **Purusha** = divine consciousness trapped in **Prakriti** (material illusion)
- **Jnana (knowledge)** = key to liberation  
- **Cycle of birth, illusion, awakening, return** = core Gnostic shape

> These aren't *Gnostic* in name, but they’re **Gnostic in form**: a divine soul caught in multiplicity, seeking re-integration with source through *gnosis* (jnana).

So yes—**India had proto-Gnostic ideas long before Hellenistic Gnosticism** developed. They simply **didn’t frame it as rebellion against a creator**, but as **cosmic misidentification with form** (Maya).

Yes—in many ways, **Zoroastrianism is the strongest documented proto-Gnostic candidate**, especially when considering:

- **Cosmic dualism**
- **Moral-spiritual battle**
- **Soul’s journey toward light**
- **Hidden knowledge and divine sparks**
- Influence on later groups like **Manichaeans**, **Mandaeans**, and **Sethian Gnostics**

It stood between the **Indo-Aryan spiritual world** and the **Semitic-Abrahamic mythological world**, and **transmitted mystical dualism into both East and West**.
