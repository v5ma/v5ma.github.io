what are the best arguments that there is no pre-trib rapture, and that Christians are misreading their bible

Self Aware Networks GPT:
Here are the clearest, text-based arguments used by non-pre-trib readers to say “there is no pre-trib rapture” and that the Bible is being misread when it’s taught:

1) No verse plainly teaches a secret, pre-trib removal of the church  
There’s no passage that unambiguously says “believers are taken to heaven before a 7-year tribulation.” The go-to “rapture” text (1 Thess 4:15-17) describes `parousia` (the coming/arrival of Jesus), resurrection, and a loud trumpet—public, not secret—and it never places this event before tribulation.

2) Scripture presents one climactic coming, not a two-stage coming  
Pre-trib requires a first, invisible coming for the church, then a second, visible coming with the church. Yet the NT consistently treats Jesus’ return as a single, public event (Matt 24; 1 Thess 4–5; 2 Thess 1–2; Rev 19). The same markers—trumpet, clouds, gathering, judgment—cluster together.

3) Jesus places the gathering of the elect “after the tribulation”  
In Matthew 24:29-31, Jesus says “immediately after the tribulation… he will send out his angels with a loud trumpet call, and they will gather his elect.” The sequence is explicit: tribulation → cosmic signs → trumpet → gathering. That aligns naturally with 1 Cor 15:52 (“at the last trumpet”) and 1 Thess 4:16 (“the trumpet of God”).

4) “Not destined for wrath” ≠ “removed before tribulation”  
1 Thess 5:9 says believers aren’t appointed to wrath. Non-pre-trib readers note “wrath” is God’s final judgment, not necessarily the whole period of end-time trouble. God can protect or seal his people through tribulation (e.g., Exod 8–12; John 17:15), and Revelation depicts saints preserved spiritually even when suffering physically (Rev 7; 12:11).

5) The saints are still on earth during end-time pressure  
Revelation repeatedly shows faithful believers enduring and overcoming (Rev 6:9-11; 7:14; 13:7-10; 14:12). Daniel’s pattern (Dan 7:21, 25; 12:7) is the same: the holy ones are pressured before final deliverance. If the church were gone, these scenes need a second category of “tribulation saints,” which the text never clearly creates.

6) The “left behind” reading of Matt 24 reverses Jesus’ point  
In the Noah analogy (Matt 24:37-41), those “taken” are like those swept away in judgment; those “left” are the spared—mirroring Luke 17:26-37, where the “taken” end up at a scene of judgment (“Where, Lord?” … “where the body is…”). So the passage warns about surprise judgment, not a secret rescue.

7) “Apostasia” in 2 Thess 2 means rebellion, not departure to heaven  
Paul says the Day won’t come unless the apostasia occurs and the man of lawlessness is revealed (2 Thess 2:3). Most lexical work takes apostasia as religious revolt/defection, not a physical departure of the church. That puts the revelation of the lawless one before our gathering to Christ (2 Thess 2:1–3).

8) The timing markers tie resurrection/rapture to the “last trumpet”  
1 Cor 15:52 says resurrection/transform happens “at the last trumpet.” In the Gospels the trumpet sounds after the tribulation (Matt 24:31). If the last trumpet is post-trib, it’s hard to place a prior, separate trumpet for a pre-trib event.

9) Imminence = readiness, not a calendarless, signless event  
Exhortations to “watch” and “be ready” (Matt 24–25; 1 Thess 5) call believers to constant faithfulness because the timing is unknown. Non-pre-trib readers note that “unknown day/hour” doesn’t require a signless, pre-trib return; it simply forbids date-setting for the one return that also has preceding patterns/signs (Matt 24:4-31).

10) Revelation 3:10 doesn’t require removal from earth  
“Keep you from the hour of trial” (`tēreō ek`) can mean preserve through (as in John 17:15, “keep them from the evil one” while not taking them out of the world). Many take Rev 3:10 as a promise of protection or faithful preservation, not necessarily relocation.

11) Israel–Church separation isn’t stated the way pre-trib needs  
Pre-trib usually rests on a sharp Israel/Church distinction to relocate end-time trouble to “Israel’s clock.” Non-pre-trib readers argue the NT unifies Jew and Gentile in one people of God (Eph 2:11-22; Rom 11’s grafting), so splitting end-time destinies isn’t demanded by the text.

12) The “newness” problem  
While not a proof-text, critics point out the pre-trib scheme (two-stage coming, seven-year gap, distinct programs) appears much later in church history than post-trib or historic premillennial readings. That doesn’t disprove it, but it undercuts the claim that Scripture obviously teaches it.

How pre-trib proof texts are commonly misread (and the non-pre-trib reply):
- 1 Thess 4:13-18: “Caught up” (`harpazō`) is real—but with trumpet/voice/visibility, tied to the Day of the Lord theme that rolls straight into 1 Thess 5 (unexpected judgment on the unprepared), not a prior secret event.  
- John 14:1-3: Jesus promises to “take you to myself,” which can be fulfilled at his return/resurrection life, not necessarily by a pre-trib removal.  
- Rev 4:1: John’s “Come up here” is a visionary transition, not the church’s rapture.  
- Rev 19 vs. Rev 20: The resurrection language and culminating victory happen at Jesus’ visible coming; no clear prior resurrection/translation event is spelled out.  
- “Imminent” language: Urgency and unpredictability motivate faithfulness in every age; they don’t settle the sequencing question.

Bottom line: The best case against a pre-trib rapture is simply reading the major passages in their own sequence. Jesus places the gathering “after the tribulation” (Matt 24:29-31); Paul ties our being caught up to that same public `parousia` and “last trumpet” (1 Thess 4; 1 Cor 15); and Revelation shows the saints persevering through pressure before the final appearing. On that reading, pre-tribulation rapture is not demanded by the text and often depends on importing a two-stage framework the passages themselves don’t clearly teach.
