### Similarities Between Islam (Sunni/Shia) and Judaism:
1. **Strict Monotheism**:
   - Islam and Judaism both emphasize absolute monotheism. In Islam, this is known as *Tawhid*, and in Judaism, it is expressed in the *Shema* prayer: "Hear, O Israel: The Lord our God, the Lord is one."
   - Christianity, in contrast, incorporates the concept of the Trinity, which Islam and Judaism reject as a form of polytheism.

2. **Prophetic Tradition**:
   - Both Islam and Judaism view prophets as human messengers of God and reject the divinity of Jesus. 
   - Islam regards Jesus (*Isa*) as a prophet, not the Son of God, while Judaism generally views him as a historical figure (if at all).

3. **Law-Based Religion**:
   - Islam’s *Sharia* (Islamic law) and Judaism’s *Halakha* (Jewish law) both emphasize detailed legal systems governing daily life, including diet, worship, and interpersonal relations.
   - Christianity tends to emphasize grace, faith, and spiritual transformation over legal observance.

4. **Scriptural Similarities**:
   - Islam’s Quran contains many narratives found in the Torah, such as those of Abraham (*Ibrahim*), Moses (*Musa*), and Noah (*Nuh*). The emphasis on shared patriarchs further highlights their common heritage.

5. **Religious Practices**:
   - Both Islam and Judaism have specific dietary laws (*halal* and *kosher*) and ritual practices. For example, both prohibit the consumption of pork and mandate forms of ritual slaughter.
   - Circumcision is a shared practice in both traditions.

---

### Differences from Christianity:
1. **Rejection of Jesus’ Divinity**:
   - Judaism and Islam explicitly reject the idea that Jesus is the Son of God or part of a divine Trinity, a cornerstone of Christian faith.
   
2. **Concept of Salvation**:
   - Christianity places central importance on salvation through Jesus’ death and resurrection. Judaism and Islam emphasize obedience to God’s laws and moral conduct for attaining God’s favor.
   
3. **Clergy and Intermediaries**:
   - Islam and Judaism lack an institutionalized priesthood akin to the Christian clergy. In Islam, imams and scholars guide worship but do not serve as mediators between God and humans. Similarly, Judaism has rabbis as teachers but no equivalent to Christian priests.

---

### Nuances in the Sunni, Shia, and Jewish Comparison:
- **Sunni Islam**: Closer to Judaism in its simplicity and emphasis on *Tawhid*, strict scriptural adherence, and focus on the law.
- **Shia Islam**: Shares with Judaism an esoteric dimension, particularly in Shia ideas of hidden knowledge (e.g., the Hidden Imam) and spiritual leadership, which can parallel the role of rabbinic sages in Judaism.

---

Yes, both Sunni and Shia Islam share a theological and doctrinal closeness to Judaism compared to Christianity. This is especially evident in monotheism, law-based religious practices, and the rejection of Jesus' divinity. Christianity diverges more significantly due to its focus on Christ as a divine savior, the Trinity, and its theological emphasis on grace over law.
