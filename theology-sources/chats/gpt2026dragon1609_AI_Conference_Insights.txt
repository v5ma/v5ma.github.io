**Summary**

## 1. Project kickoff  
Type a log-line or paste a treatment. Flow parses it into a timeline with empty “clips” and a shared asset bin; each clip inherits global style cues such as aspect ratio or grade. citeturn0search8turn0search1  

## 2. Asset pool  
You can drag in reference images, audio beds, or 3-D files—or just request them with prompts. The library stays addressable by name (e.g., “use the neon-lit alley background”) across later generations. citeturn0search8turn0search4  

## 3. Scene builder

## 4. Iteration loop

## 5. Audio polish

## 6. Assembly & export  
Clips snap together; a single “Compile” produces a ProRes or MP4 plus caption file. One click publishes straight to YouTube or Drive. Flow keeps prompt provenance metadata with the render. citeturn0search3turn0search8  

## 7. Collaboration & governance

### Why it matters  
By collapsing asset creation, editing, and mastering into promptable tasks, Flow turns “movie making” into an iterative chat—letting a solo creator finish in hours what once took teams and weeks.
