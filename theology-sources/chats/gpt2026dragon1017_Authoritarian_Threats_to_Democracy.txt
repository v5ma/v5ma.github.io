Self Aware Networks GPT:
Yes, several scholars argue that ancient Egyptian religious concepts significantly influenced early Judaism. Though terms like "stolen" are often debated, the idea of cultural borrowing, integration, and reinterpretation is widely supported.

### Examples of Egyptian Concepts Found in Judaism:

1. **Monotheism / Henotheism (Akhenaten's Aten Cult)**:
   - **Akhenaten (1353–1336 BCE)** introduced a form of monotheism, worshiping Aten, the solar disk, as the sole divine power.
   - Some suggest this early Egyptian monotheistic revolution influenced Hebrew concepts of monotheism during or after the Israelites’ Egyptian captivity.

2. **Creation and the Word (Logos)**:
   - Egyptian texts often depict creation as occurring through divine speech (Ptah spoke creation into existence).
   - Judaism similarly describes creation by divine speech: *"And God said, let there be light…"*

3. **Afterlife and Judgment (Ma'at)**:
   - Egyptian religion prominently featured moral judgment after death, overseen by Osiris, weighing hearts against Ma'at (truth and justice).
   - Later Judaism (particularly post-exilic and Second Temple Judaism) adopted ideas of divine judgment, resurrection, and moral accountability after death—concepts less prominent in earlier Hebrew scriptures but central afterward.

4. **Wisdom Literature**:
   - Egyptian wisdom literature, such as the "Instructions of Amenemope" (around 1300–1075 BCE), significantly parallels the Hebrew book of Proverbs.
   - Proverbs contain verses closely mirroring Amenemope’s instructions, strongly indicating cultural and literary borrowing.

5. **The Ark of the Covenant and Portable Shrines**:
   - Egyptian religious rituals included portable shrines or sacred arks for carrying divine symbols or statues.
   - The Hebrew Ark of the Covenant concept may have origins in Egyptian ceremonial traditions of portable shrines.

6. **The Concept of a Savior and Messiah Figure**:
   - Egyptian religion included the figure of Horus, a divine savior who overcame evil and restored cosmic order.
   - Jewish messianic ideas, developed fully later, resonate symbolically with earlier Egyptian mythological motifs of divine redeemers.

### How This Borrowing Occurred:

- **Cultural Contact**: Israelites' proximity and prolonged presence in Egypt provided ample opportunities for exchange.
- **Syncretism**: Integrating foreign beliefs and rituals was common among ancient cultures, not necessarily considered negative or unethical at the time.
- **Adaptation and Transformation**: Rather than simply appropriating ideas, Israelites adapted these ideas uniquely, aligning them with their distinctive worldview and theology.

### Scholarly Perspective:

Most historians view the relationship as cultural exchange or borrowing rather than outright theft. Ancient societies naturally influenced each other. While early Israelites integrated and reshaped Egyptian elements, they gave them new, distinctly Hebrew meanings, forming the foundation for later Judaism and ultimately Christianity and Islam.
