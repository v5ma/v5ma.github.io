In the story, Inanna descends to the Underworld, ruled by her sister **Ereshkigal**, the goddess of death and the underworld. Inanna's journey represents death, transformation, and rebirth. Here's a summary of key events:

1. **Descent to the Underworld**:

3. **Death**:

4. **Rescue and Resurrection**:

5. **Rising After Three Days**:
   - Inanna is resurrected after three days and allowed to leave the Underworld, but under the condition that she finds a replacement to take her place.

6. **Raising the Dead**:
   - In some interpretations of the myth, Inanna's resurrection causes life to return to the earth. In some versions, it is said that the dead rise with her, symbolizing the cyclical nature of life, death, and rebirth.

### Themes and Symbolism:
- **Death and Rebirth**: Inanna's story symbolizes the natural cycles of life, fertility, and the seasons, much like later myths of resurrection.
- **Rising of the Dead**: The association with raising the dead underscores her role as a goddess of life and transformation.
- **Three Days**: The three-day period is a motif found in various myths and religious traditions, symbolizing a liminal period before renewal or transformation.

This myth has fascinated scholars due to its parallels with later resurrection stories, including those found in Christianity, and its profound exploration of mortality, power, and transformation.
