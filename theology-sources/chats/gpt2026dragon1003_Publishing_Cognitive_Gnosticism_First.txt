## Why Should You Publish Elsewhere First?

1. **Wikipedia’s “No Original Research” Policy**  
   Wikipedia’s guidelines explicitly state that articles must be based on *previously published*, reliable sources. If you propose a completely new concept on Wikipedia *without* third-party sources discussing it, it will likely be flagged as “original research” and possibly be deleted.

2. **Notability Requirements**  
   For a topic to have a dedicated Wikipedia article, it must meet the notability guidelines. One of the key ways to prove notability is to show that multiple independent, reliable sources have covered the topic in some depth. By publishing (and hopefully getting outside coverage or peer commentary), you help build that notability.

3. **Verifiability & References**  
   Wikipedia requires *verifiable* information. Having your concept published on a platform that is recognized and citable (for example, a peer-reviewed journal, a well-regarded online publication, or reputable academic-oriented platforms) increases the trustworthiness of the sources you’d be using.

4. **Building Credibility**  
   Publishing “Cognitive Gnosticism” in a recognized channel provides a timestamp and a reference that anyone can look up. It also helps you gather external feedback, refine your idea, and demonstrate that others find it notable or important enough to discuss.
