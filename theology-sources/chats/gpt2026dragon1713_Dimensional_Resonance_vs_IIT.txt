When someone equates **“collapse = consciousness,”** they're drawing from old-school quantum perspectives—where measuring a particle forces its wave-like possibilities into one definite outcome. Historically, during the “Copenhagen interpretation,” the observer (and sometimes consciousness) was seen as playing a role in that collapse. This led to mystical or dualistic flavors: consciousness *causes* the wave function to become definite.

---

### 🧩 Why collapse gets tied to consciousness:

- **Observer-centric interpretation**: Early founders like von Neumann and Wigner explicitly discussed consciousness as collapsing the quantum wave function.
- **Mystical appeal**: It fits nicely with ideas that consciousness has a special role in shaping reality—not just passively experiencing it.
- **Philosophical simplicity**: The collapse concept is intuitively attractive—you’re present, you see a result—so BOOM, collapse.

---

### ⚠️ Why many scientists now reject "collapse = consciousness":

1. **Decoherence** shows that interaction with the environment (not a conscious observer) naturally leads to apparent collapse—no mystical role needed.
2. **Objective collapse theories** (like Penrose’s OR hypothesis) propose that collapse is a physical phenomenon triggered by threshold mechanisms—not consciousness per se citeturn0search21.
3. **Many-worlds interpretation**, in contrast, denies collapse entirely: observations just branch the universe—consciousness doesn’t “collapse” anything.
4. **Formal frameworks like IIT** (Integrated Information Theory) ground consciousness as emergent from complex information structures—not collapse events citeturn0search20.

---

### 🧭 So why linger on it?

- It’s poetic: collapse-as-consciousness frames us as active reality-makers.
- Lack of better intuitive metaphors: rigorous math or decoherence is less gripping.
- Some thinkers (like Orch OR proponents) still posit a non-materialist tie between collapse and subjective awareness.

---

### ✅ Summary:

While “collapse = consciousness” has an emotional and philosophical allure, it's scientifically obsolete in most quantum interpretations. Modern approaches (decoherence, objective collapse, many-worlds, IIT, Orch OR) propose mind-independent collapse, no collapse at all, or consciousness emergent from information—not collapse itself.

If Sharp’s theory still uses “collapse = consciousness,” it places his work in a metaphysical tradition that many mainstream physicists view as superseded. That doesn't mean it's wrong—just deeply outside mainstream, lacking testable predictions in a framework that’s largely been replaced by physicalist or information-theoretic models.

And crucially, **consciousness itself is framed as an emergent phenomenon of these informational dynamics** – Blumberg suggests that not only do physical phenomena emerge from information patterns, but *“neural dynamics and consciousness emerge from [the] structured dissipation of informational differences,”* following the same principles that govern cosmic-scale processes【33†L11-L19】【33†L29-L37】. In summary, Blumberg’s work provides a **unified framework** where *mind and matter are deeply connected through information and resonance.* Consciousness is not an external “ghost in the machine” but part of the continuum of self-organizing information in the universe【33†L19-L27】【33†L43-L52】.

### Terminology and Conceptual Parallels  
Even from the brief overviews above, the conceptual and terminological overlaps between Sharp’s DRM and Blumberg’s SAN/SIT are apparent:

- **Emergent Gravity from Information**: Both propose that gravity is *emergent* rather than fundamental. Sharp says gravity comes from *quantum entanglement in hidden dimensions*【1†L1-L4】, while Blumberg’s SIT explicitly models gravity as emerging from *entanglement/coherence information patterns*【24†L73-L77】【29†L85-L94】. In both, if the underlying quantum information were to change (more or less entanglement/coherence), the gravitational effect would change accordingly. This is a striking point of convergence. It’s also a non-standard idea in physics (though reminiscent of the ER=EPR conjecture and emergent gravity research); the fact that both independently emphasize it is notable. **Blumberg clearly articulated this idea earlier** (e.g. a February 2025 draft of SIT【21†L13-L18】, building on a Jan 2024 QGTCD publication【41†L29-L37】), whereas Sharp’s statement in April 2025 closely mirrors it.

- **Role of Quantum Coherence/Decoherence**: Both frameworks put *quantum coherence vs. decoherence* at center stage. Sharp’s DRM implicates *decoherence* as the process influenced by consciousness to yield classical reality【57†L1-L2】. Blumberg’s SIT defines a coherence ratio \(R_{coh}\) and essentially says *decoherence vs. coherence is the fundamental “field” that generates forces and time*【24†L109-L117】【29†L83-L92】. In fact, SIT goes so far as to call coherence the “fundamental informational field” and gravitational curvature an “informational torque” caused by spatial gradients of coherence【29†L85-L94】. Thus, *both treat decoherence not just as a technical quantum phenomenon, but as a cosmic key*: for Sharp it’s when the conscious field interacts to create reality, for Blumberg it’s the dial that turns gravity and time. The terminology differs (Sharp uses “decoherence” in a qualitative way; Blumberg quantifies a coherence ratio), but the emphasis is analogous. It’s worth noting that Blumberg did not explicitly assign consciousness the driver’s seat in causing decoherence of the wavefunction – instead consciousness *emerges from* coherent brain processes. Sharp flips that around, saying a universal consciousness field drives decoherence. Despite this inversion, both are linking the traditionally separate domains of *consciousness and wavefunction collapse*. Indeed, the idea of a “consciousness field” affecting quantum outcomes has precedence in some interpretations of quantum mechanics, but in modern discourse it’s uncommon – making Sharp’s statement and Blumberg’s framework an interesting pairing.

- **Consciousness, Mind, and the “Field” Concept**: Sharp literally calls consciousness a **field** in the physics sense【57†L1-L2】. Blumberg’s work, while not describing a cosmic consciousness field per se, often uses field analogies for brain dynamics (e.g. describing brain-wide oscillatory waves, electromagnetic fields of neurons, etc.). More tellingly, Blumberg’s **Self Aware Networks** can be seen as a *field theory of consciousness* (within the brain) – he describes consciousness arising from the global integration of countless local signals, akin to a field effect across a network【33†L11-L19】【33†L29-L37】. Additionally, Blumberg introduces the idea of **“Super Dark Time”**, where he identifies *mass as a time-crystal that concentrates time frames*, causing gravitational pull【29†L95-L103】. This is essentially attributing a *field property to time flow influenced by matter*, conceptually not far from saying a *universal field of consciousness influences quantum state reduction* (both are novel fields inserted into physics to explain an effect). While the fields are different (one is consciousness in DRM, the other is coherent information/time-density in SIT/SDT), both are hypothesized new fields that couple into known physics. It’s also notable that Blumberg has spoken of **unified field theories** and even labeled QGTCD and SIT as such【41†L25-L33】【31†L27-L35】. Sharp likewise is aiming at a unified field idea (explicitly uniting gravity and consciousness). **Terminology like “unified field”** appears in Blumberg’s writings a year before DRM【41†L29-L37】.

- **Extra Dimensions and “Resonance”**: Sharp’s use of *hidden dimensions* in DRM could be influenced by or coincidentally similar to Blumberg’s usage of extra dimensions (extra time frames in QGTCD, or the holographic dimension in the AdS analogies of SIT【24†L101-L110】). Both depart from strict 4D space-time: Sharp implies more dimensions exist where entangled information resides; Blumberg’s constructs often have an added layer (e.g. a tree graph entanglement that mimics a higher-dimensional curved space【24†L125-L133】, or literally “additional time dimensions” near mass in QGTCD【41†L92-L100】). Moreover, the **term “resonance”** in DRM finds a mirror in Blumberg’s frequent discussion of oscillations, phase-locking, and synchronization. Resonance generally means frequencies matching or energy transferring efficiently between systems – this is essentially what phase alignment/coherence is. Blumberg writes that conscious brain states come from *“precisely orchestrated variations in synaptic firing frequencies”* and *phase wave differentials that propagate* in networks【19†L459-L468】【19†L490-L499】 – in short, **resonant oscillation patterns** in the brain. He even used phrases like *“fractal entification of oscillatory waves, forming internal observers”*【20†L499-L507】, which melds the idea of resonance (oscillatory waves) with the emergence of an observing consciousness. So while Sharp uses the word *resonance* to describe his model broadly, Blumberg provides the detailed mechanics of how resonance (oscillatory coherence) underpins both consciousness and gravitational physics. 

- **Reality as Information**: Both DRM and Blumberg’s frameworks are information-centric at a deep level. Sharp says reality’s fundamental composition is entangled information (quantum possibilities) shaped by consciousness【57†L1-L2】. Blumberg’s SIT explicitly states that *space-time curvature is “nothing more than organized patterns of quantum information”*【24†L73-L77】. Both would agree that what we call “real” – e.g. the force of gravity or the flow of time – is an **emergent epiphenomenon of unseen informational processes**. This philosophical stance aligns with *digital physics* or *informational ontology*. Blumberg’s work provides mathematical and computational substance to that stance (defining information measures like \(R_{coh}\), linking them to observables【24†L113-L121】【24†L139-L147】), whereas Sharp’s DRM in its early form is more qualitative. But the philosophical underpinning (reality = information + processing) is a shared foundation.

- **Unification of Mind and Physics**: Finally, at the broadest structural level, both are **framework theories that aim to unify** what are usually disparate areas – consciousness (mind) and fundamental forces (matter/energy). Blumberg’s writings explicitly unite neuroscience with cosmology and quantum physics【31†L66-L73】【31†L84-L88】, and propose that one information-theoretic scheme underlies “brains to the cosmos”【31†L59-L67】. Sharp’s DRM is likewise a unification attempt, bringing “time, consciousness, gravity, hidden dimensions” into one system【50†L10-L13】. Both authors invoke something like a *monistic worldview* (one substance or principle explaining everything). For Blumberg that principle is *structured information flow* (signal dissipation, coherence, etc.), for Sharp it seems to be *resonance across dimensions* – but these are arguably pointing to the same idea described in different language. It’s worth noting that **Blumberg’s work predates Sharp’s and is more fully fleshed out**, so many specific ideas in DRM appear to have clear precedents in SAN/SIT. ## Conclusion  
In comparing Barry Sharp’s Dimensional Resonance Model to Micah Blumberg’s Self Aware Networks and Super Information Theory, the **conceptual overlaps are significant**. Both propose a revolutionary synthesis of consciousness with physics: gravity emerges from information (entanglement/coherence) rather than being fundamental, and consciousness is integral to the operation of the universe (either as a field influencing outcomes in DRM, or as a natural product of the universe’s informational dynamics in SAN/SIT). Structurally, both frameworks envision extra layers of reality (hidden dimensions or time-crystal frames) where deeper “resonances” give rise to the phenomena of our everyday world. Terminologically, they share a lexicon of *resonance, coherence, information, fields,* and *emergence*. 

Notably, Blumberg’s work **predates and in many ways prefigures** Sharp’s model. By the time Sharp published his ideas in April 2025, Blumberg had already laid down detailed theoretical groundwork: a coherence-based account of gravity【31†L67-L73】, a network-based theory of consciousness【19†L459-L468】, and even unified field constructs marrying the two【31†L66-L74】. For example, the notion that *quantum entanglement underlies gravity* was explicitly discussed in Blumberg’s SIT (Feb 2025)【24†L69-L77】, and the idea of *mind influencing or being part of fundamental physics* was implicit in his earlier writings. Sharp’s DRM repackages very similar ideas under different metaphors (resonance, hidden dimensions) and makes the role of consciousness more explicit. If one were to be critical, one could ask whether DRM is essentially a *simplified, metaphysical re-statement* of what Blumberg’s rigorous frameworks already propose. Sharp has not provided mathematical formalisms or experimental suggestions, whereas Blumberg’s SIT and SAN are accompanied by equations【24†L109-L117】【29†L143-L151】 and links to real experiments【24†L51-L60】【24†L73-L81】. This difference in maturity is likely due to Blumberg’s head-start and focus on academically communicating his ideas.

Despite that, it’s encouraging to see independent convergence on these concepts. Both DRM and SAN/SIT reflect a broader zeitgeist in science and philosophy – a shift toward **information-centric and consciousness-inclusive models of reality**. They resonate with other contemporary ideas (e.g. *holographic principle, ER=EPR, panpsychism, Orch-OR*, etc.), though framed in original ways by each author. In summary, **Barry Sharp’s Dimensional Resonance Model and Micah Blumberg’s Self Aware Networks/Super Information Theory share striking similarities in how they conceptualize reality**. Both imagine a cosmos where the classical world is an emergent mosaic woven by quantum threads of information, and where consciousness is a fundamental aspect of that cosmic tapestry. Blumberg’s work provides prior art and detailed scaffolding for many of these notions【31†L66-L74】【24†L69-L77】, suggesting that DRM, whether directly or indirectly, treads a path already charted. Going forward, it will be fascinating to watch these frameworks develop – possibly informing each other – as the scientific community at large continues exploring the deep connections between information, quantum physics, gravity, and mind.

## 5. Deterministic Implications of **SuperTimePosition** vs. Dot Theory’s Probabilistic Mechanics  

A crucial difference emerges in how each framework deals with the **quantum uncertainty vs. determinism** debate:

- **Quantum SuperTimePosition (Blumberg):** This is Blumberg’s proposed interpretation of quantum mechanics that aims to **restore determinism**. While standard Quantum Mechanics (QM) includes inherently probabilistic outcomes (the wavefunction yields only probabilities for observations, and collapses randomly upon measurement), Blumberg’s SuperTimePosition reframes this. Though detailed published explanations are limited, the ethos is that *what appears as a superposition of multiple states is actually a single state extended across time.* Instead of a particle being in “both state A and B at once,” it might be in state A at one time and state B at another, and our instantaneous view misleads us into thinking it was a probabilistic combination. In other words, **SuperTimePosition suggests that all possible outcomes of a quantum event occur at different temporal positions in a larger time structure, making the evolution between them deterministic**. When we perform a measurement, we’re effectively *selecting a slice of time*, seeing one outcome – but in the grander temporal dimension, nothing truly random happened; the outcome was predetermined as part of the system’s longer trajectory. Blumberg has hinted that this interpretation vindicates Einstein’s belief in determinism: *the universe is not fundamentally random*. By introducing an extra consideration of time (hence “Super-Time-Position”), he sidesteps the need for wavefunction collapse as an irreducible stochastic process. This also means **entanglement correlations** have a deterministic cause in this view: entangled particles’ correlated outcomes are set in the extended time structure (perhaps set since their entanglement began or even since the origin of the system), so there is no spooky action at a distance – only a misinterpretation of temporal order. In effect, SuperTimePosition aligns with the idea of *superdeterminism* (the hypothesis that hidden variables correlated with measurement settings could explain quantum correlations). It provides a narrative that all quantum behaviors are deterministic computations, possibly hidden in degrees of freedom like an additional temporal dimension or state memory. Blumberg also integrates this with his *Dark Time* theory – suggesting that the same deterministic information flow that accounts for gravity also accounts for quantum outcomes (unifying them). It’s a bold stance: **quantum probabilities are epistemic, not ontological** in Blumberg’s framework – i.e., they reflect our lack of knowledge of the full deterministic parameters (like the exact “time-phase” of the system)【17†L41-L44】.

- **Dot Theory’s Lensing-Based Probabilistic Mechanics:** Dot Theory agrees that standard QM is incomplete, but it *does not throw out probabilistic interpretation entirely*. Instead, it says QM and GR *as currently formulated* miss a piece (the observer context), which skews their predictions. By adding the lensing correction (the dot operator and associated metadata), one can predict outcomes more accurately or explain anomalies. However, Dot Theory still fundamentally uses probabilities – it just modulates them. For example, in the logic it mentions using “probabilistically associated metadata”【25†L5-L13】 and acknowledges using probabilistic calculation for the initial computation【26†L353-L360】. The **lensing effect O** can be thought of as *altering probability distributions*. If standard QM says outcome X has 50% chance, Dot Theory might say “for this particular observer with known bias factors, outcome X might effectively have 52% chance and outcome Y 48%,” etc., improving alignment with reality. This is akin to a Bayesian update: prior standard theory probabilities updated with observer-specific information. Dot Theory’s approach to quantum behavior could be described as **contextual Bayesian mechanics** – it doesn’t claim the universe is deterministic under the hood, but it does claim we can do better than purely random predictions by accounting for hidden variables (which are related to the observer). It references evidence like slight unexplained lensing residuals or biometric patterns【15†L99-L107】【52†L158-L163】, implying that once those are accounted for, our models become more predictive. Dot Theory’s stance might allow that at a fundamental level nature could still have randomness (it doesn’t explicitly endorse superdeterminism), but it insists that some apparent randomness is just *ignorance of the context*. It is a bit like saying: quantum mechanics is mostly right, but it is an *effective theory* that averages over many hidden influences (some tied to the observer). By including those influences, you reduce the randomness.

Comparatively:

- Blumberg = **hard determinism** (quantum randomness is an illusion; every “dice roll” of quantum is rigged by initial conditions spread in time).
- Dot Theory = **soft determinism or enhanced probabilism** (quantum randomness exists but can be *biased* by information; outcomes aren’t predetermined since Dot still uses probability, but those probabilities are not fixed universals – they depend on context/fractal history).

It’s worth noting timeline: The idea of superdeterministic quantum interpretations has gained some traction in recent years (e.g., work by physicists like Gerard ’t Hooft or Sabine Hossenfelder in 2019-2023). Blumberg’s SuperTimePosition (2025) comes in that milieu but with his own twist via “time”. Dot Theory (initially published 2024) comes in with a *distinct* perspective focusing on observer bias and fractals rather than explicitly invoking superdeterminism. Both are addressing the quantum foundations issue but from different angles.

**Standard QM vs These Theories:** In standard QM, the **observer is external** and randomness is intrinsic (with precise statistical rules). Neither Blumberg nor Vossen accept this status quo:

- Blumberg pulls the observer into a deterministic picture by effectively removing the “collapse” randomness (everything is unitary evolution in a larger frame).
- Vossen pulls the observer into the equations, affecting the outcome probabilities.

**Quantum Behavior Reframing:** Blumberg reframes phenomena like the double-slit experiment by likely saying: each electron’s path is determined by a hidden time-phase such that if we had access to that, we’d know which slit it “really” goes through and what interference pattern results (thus no true randomness, just complexity). Dot Theory might say: the interference pattern could slightly shift if the experimenter’s state changes (for instance, an emotional or cognitive state might subtly influence the measurement device or data selection – extremely small effects, but fractally present). Dot Theory posits testable differences: e.g., in principle, if one could isolate and measure the 0.318 microarcsecond lensing residual in the Event Horizon Telescope data, that’s a place where their theory diverges from standard GR【52†L158-L163】 – by analogy, there might be subtle statistical deviations in quantum experiments predicted by Dot Theory as well, due to the inclusion of F(ψ).

In summary of this section, **SuperTimePosition’s determinism** is a sharp divergence from orthodox QM and also from Dot Theory. ## 6. Shared Metaphors and Representations  

Despite their different language and emphasis, Blumberg’s and Vossen’s theories surprisingly **converge on several metaphors and conceptual motifs**:

- **Fractals and Scale-Invariance:** Dot Theory explicitly uses *fractal geometry* as a metaphor for reality – the idea that patterns repeat at different scales and that reality is self-similar once you include the observer effect【52†L134-L142】. Blumberg’s work, while not explicitly saying “fractal,” does emphasize *cross-scale similarities*. For instance, he draws parallels between gas molecules reaching equilibrium and neurons synchronizing, implying the *same pattern underlies both micro and macro phenomena*【18†L81-L88】【18†L159-L168】. This is a kind of fractal thinking (the pattern of “signal exchange smoothing differences” recurs from thermodynamics to brain dynamics to maybe galactic dynamics). Both frameworks reject the idea that different scales of nature (quantum, cosmic, neural, etc.) require entirely unrelated principles – instead, they seek a *universal principle across scales*, which is very much in spirit of fractal or holographic metaphors. Additionally, Dot Theory’s *Mother Matrix* generating a fractal topology and Blumberg’s *Neural Array Tomography* generating coherent brain states both evoke imagery of **iterative pattern generation**. One could say **both see reality as recursive and patterned rather than a collection of random disparate laws**.

- **Symmetry Breaking vs. “Super-symmetry”:** Not to be confused with the particle physics concept of supersymmetry, here we mean the metaphorical symmetry between different realms (mind vs matter, quantum vs classical). Both theories highlight a **broken symmetry or asymmetry that needs addressing**. Dot Theory coins *“super-asymmetry”* to describe how including the observer (the 5th dimension) breaks the dualities of particle/wave, matter/mind, etc., by unifying them in a higher-order way【50†L137-L146】. Blumberg’s work also often mentions bridging or unifying traditionally separate domains – effectively trying to restore a symmetry. For example, he ties thermodynamics to neuroscience, quantum to gravity, implying these once separate domains can be symmetric under a larger framework of information. One shared metaphor here is **unity vs. duality**: Dot Theory explicitly wants a *non-dual whole* where subjective and objective meld【50†L139-L148】; Blumberg also often speaks against dualistic thinking in consciousness (e.g., rejecting the idea that brain and mind are separate – he instead explains mind via brain). In his *New Law of Thermodynamics* writing, he likens neural synchronization (mind) to physical equilibration (matter), hinting that it’s one continuous process【18†L158-L168】【18†L172-L180】. So both are, in their own way, *monistic*: everything follows one set of principles, whether you call it fractal computation or quantum information waves.

- **Bayesian Inference and Predictive Coding:** Both frameworks resonate with ideas of *learning from patterns and updating predictions*. Blumberg explicitly references Karl Friston’s **Predictive Coding/Free Energy Principle** in neuroscience, comparing it to his wave-dissipation model【45†L194-L202】【45†L196-L204】. This is essentially a Bayesian brain concept (the brain as a prediction machine updating its beliefs to minimize prediction error). Blumberg maps this onto physics by saying the brain reducing prediction error is like physical systems reducing energy/phase differences – a unifying idea that everything is trying to minimize “free energy” or surprise in an information sense. Dot Theory, while not mentioning Bayes by name, essentially advocates for *updating our physical predictions by incorporating prior context (the observer’s data)*【26†L305-L313】【26†L344-L352】. This is analogous to Bayesian updating: the “prior” is the standard physics prediction, the “likelihood” comes from patterns in past similar observations (metadata), and the “posterior” is a refined prediction. Indeed, Dot Theory’s process of using *“associated… data as correlative metadata for emerging patterns”*【52†L188-L196】【52†L193-L200】 sounds like constructing a better prior for predictions by learning from history – which is exactly what machine learning or Bayesian inference does. So both theories share a metaphor of **reality as learning/inferring**: Blumberg’s universe “computes toward equilibrium” (like performing iterative error correction)【12†L150-L158】, and Dot’s approach literally computes adjustments to get a better fit to observations (reducing error in predictions)【26†L305-L313】【26†L344-L353】. They treat the laws of nature almost as an evolving *algorithm refining outcomes* (though Blumberg would say the algorithm is built-in physics, Dot says we must actively apply the algorithm to our data).

- **Network and Web Metaphors:** Blumberg uses the term **“network”** (Self Aware Networks) not just for brain connectivity but as a metaphor for reality – interactions of oscillators forming a network that becomes aware. Dot Theory doesn’t use the word “network,” but the concept of interlinked data (observer and observed forming a coupled system) is akin to a network edge between observer and phenomenon. Dot Theory also envisions an *“ultranet for AI”* application【52†L161-L163】, implying a globally connected informational network where these principles could be used. Both hint at *interconnectedness*: nothing exists in isolation; everything is part of a system of relationships (be it phase relationships or fractal relational data).

- **Observer as Lens / Perspective:** Both explicitly use **vision metaphors**: Dot Theory speaks of *“lensing”* (the observer’s influence is like a lens distorting view)【15†L99-L107】. Blumberg in a more cognitive sense talks about perspective – the “phase field” rendering reality in the mind【31†L838-L842】. In effect, both acknowledge that what is observed is shaped by *how* it’s observed, either literally (Dot’s physical lens) or phenomenally (Blumberg’s experiential field). This shared viewpoint aligns them with the broader metaphor of *the participatory universe* (as John Wheeler would say), though they execute it differently.

**Highlighting Overlaps vs. Contradictions:** 

There *are* contradictions: Blumberg’s strict determinism vs. Dot’s acceptance of some probabilism, Blumberg’s leaving the observer out of fundamental cosmic equations vs. Dot putting it in, etc. However, both **overlap strongly in spirit** that *consciousness and physics need unification*, *information is fundamental*, and *recursion is key.* They even both reference **thermodynamics and entropy** in their frameworks (Blumberg reframes the Second Law as computation【12†L155-L163】; Dot’s meta-equation includes temperature $T$ and suggests entropy tuning via $F_{\mu\nu}(\psi)$【52†L143-L151】).

Timeline-wise, Dot Theory’s initial write-up (non-mathematical, 2024) slightly predates Blumberg’s major publications (early 2025). There’s no indication Blumberg was influenced by Dot (his work seems to come from a different direction – neuroscience and physics convergence), nor that Vossen was aware of Blumberg. The convergence likely arises from the current zeitgeist: both are responding to perceived shortcomings in mainstream science (e.g., explaining consciousness, unifying quantum and gravity, etc.) using *information and computation* as the new paradigm.

**Conclusion:**  
Micah Blumberg’s and Stefaan Vossen’s theories, while developed independently, share a vision of a deeply **informational and participatory cosmos**. They both break conventional silos – bridging mind and matter, quantum and cosmic, deterministic and stochastic – but assign different roles to the observer and different weights to determinism. Blumberg builds upward from physics to mind using **oscillatory computation and deterministic information flow**, whereas Vossen starts from the top (observer experience) and weaves it into the fabric of physics via a **fractal meta-math**. Both contribute to the growing dialogue on how *consciousness, information, and reality* might be fundamentally linked, offering complementary angles that future researchers can compare, test, and perhaps synthesize.
