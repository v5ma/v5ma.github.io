- The biblical writers adapted these familiar myths but significantly altered their meaning and context.
- Instead of directly borrowing gods like Marduk or Baal, biblical authors intentionally positioned Yahweh as superior to these deities:
  - Where Baal is seen as struggling against Yam, Yahweh effortlessly overcomes chaos.
  - Unlike Marduk, Yahweh does not struggle; he simply commands or effortlessly subdues Leviathan/Rahab to demonstrate absolute divine sovereignty.

- Yahweh’s identity evolved distinctly in Hebrew theology:
  - **Monotheistic Identity**: Unlike Baal and Marduk, Yahweh is depicted as the singular, unrivaled God, not merely one god among many.

- Hebrew Bible authors often repurposed existing myths polemically to assert Yahweh's uniqueness and supremacy.
- By assigning roles commonly attributed to Baal or Marduk (such as conqueror of chaos) to Yahweh, biblical authors emphasized their deity’s absolute power, implicitly demoting competing regional gods.

### In short:  
- The Bible didn't simply "rename" Marduk or Baal as Yahweh.
