# River Prism: Duck Armada and Mothership Channel

The main index.html now launches the owner's rhythmic-action toybox, not another exploration detour. The previous Undertow rhythm implementation, all five tracks, First Steps and Practice Lab remain at rhythm.html with unchanged JavaScript and save readers. Floodgate Recovery remains at water-mission/index.html. The homepage Prism link is unchanged and reaches the new main entry.

Duck Armada is an approximately 89-second battle synchronized to the existing original 132 BPM Undertow soundtrack. The low river sends duck catapults and directional fruit. Destroying a catapult before launch prevents its volley. The middle tide brings toy-soldier boats and red bombs. High tide brings propeller planes, more fruit and incoming fire. Admiral Quack appears as a large health-bar flagship and exposes its core in the final musical phrase. Water height and flow change; the player's camera and dry standing position are never automatically lifted or moved.

Mothership Channel is a separate immediately selectable battle using the same original score. A large ship looms behind fighter formations. Escorts send bolts while fruit targets preserve the slicing rhythm. Its shield opens in the final phrase. Beat the core and finish the track to clear the chapter; ignoring the boss produces an escape result, not a false victory.

Swing to cut eight-direction, left/right-marked fruit. Trigger presses fire aimed saber lasers at enemies and bombs. Holding a grip turns that hand's saber into a shield, disabling its sword and gun until released. An incoming projectile must actually intersect the oriented shield. Raising it shortly before impact yields a stronger return shot toward its launcher. A moving body can dodge a lane; hazards that miss do not inflict damage. Slashing a bomb is a mistake. Slices, shots, shield blocks, perfect reflections, dodges, health, boss damage and combos contribute to readable results.

On a standard Xbox-style controller, right stick aims and left stick sidesteps. A/X swings left/right. D-pad selects cut direction (diagonal combinations work). LT/RT fires; LB/RB shields; B or left-stick down crouches; Menu pauses. The menus use D-pad, A/B and direct chapter buttons. Mouse users drag with left button, swap hand with F, fire with right button, and hold Q/E for shields. A/D sidesteps and S/Space crouches. P/Escape pauses. Arrow keys plus J/K provide explicit directional slash controls. Coarse-pointer users also have held Fire/Shield controls.

Quest controller grips are shields, not pause buttons. B/Y always provides a gameplay menu. Trigger pointing operates its large spatial controls; hand-joint pinch detection also operates that paused menu. Combat requires tracked controllers, not hand-gesture substitutes. Tracking/visibility loss pauses; recovery does not auto-resume. Reference-space reset requires recentering. AR requests actual immersive-ar and keeps its compositor transparent; it does not claim furniture occlusion or scanned water. Sidesteps and crouches remain inside a bounded play position; no forced head turns, artificial swimming or moving camera is used.

The new toy models, merged colored meshes and river shader are original procedural assets. The approaching water uses analytic flow, foam and caustic-like patterns, not traced fluid optics or sampled camera images. Actor count is capped at 80; effects at 32; laser streaks at 12; event history at 100. Geometry and materials are shared and reused. These budgets do not establish real-device frame-rate or comfort certification.

New completed battle records are isolated under prism-current.river.records.v1 by chapter, input mode and Arcade/Cruise setting. Cruise prevents hull failure but does not grant a boss kill. Failed or escaped runs are not stored as clears. Existing rhythm, lesson, practice and water-expedition records are not read or rewritten by River's score writer. Music volume uses the existing compatible audio preference format; River's other preferences have their own key.

Automated acceptance must include the untouched main entry, both complete real-time chapters through ordinary pointer/key input, actual slice/laser/block contributions, gameplay pause, independent record reload, legacy save preservation, gamepad mapping/disconnect and tracked XR slice/fire/shield/menu paths. Model fixtures alone do not certify those paths. Existing test suites now explicitly select rhythm.html when testing the retained game; they are not substituted for the new entry test. The old test windows and hit requirements are preserved. Native testing runs on GitHub Actions because this local environment blocks HTTP browser navigation and lacks WebGL. A separate full-resolution capture is a visual inspection, not a performance benchmark.

This is a playable first version for owner playtesting, not a claim of AAA completion, flawless music, infinite procedural campaigns or physical Quest/Xbox validation. Human fun, choreography, visibility, controller comfort and sustained device performance remain open. The previous production work and open save-export/accessibility tasks remain in the checklist.
