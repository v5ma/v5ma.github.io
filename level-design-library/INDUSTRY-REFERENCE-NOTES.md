# Industry Reference Notes for SVGN Level Design

These references are used for transferable principles, not for copying maps, art, characters, story, or franchise-specific mechanics.

## The Last of Us Part II

Useful lens: authored density, wide-linear alternatives, embodied traversal, environmental causality, pacing contrast, and spaces designed around believable bodies and animation.

Transfer to SVGN: start from an experience spine, graybox the complete arc, make alternate approaches differ in information or risk, connect interiors and exteriors, design sightlines and recovery, and use environmental damage or functional architecture to justify gameplay geometry.

Do not reduce the lesson to visual fidelity or large asset counts.

## BioShock series

Useful lens: district identity, environmental storytelling, readable systems, spaces with strong thematic purpose, and combat that interacts with the environment.

Transfer to SVGN: make each district or neighborhood function differently, let mechanisms and surfaces create tactical reasoning, and connect environmental story to useful spatial relationships.

Aether Reach benefits most directly, but the principle of a location having a specific ordinary purpose applies portfolio-wide.

## Half-Life series

Useful lens: teach through action, show cause and effect, introduce a relationship safely, then test it in increasingly demanding situations. Physical systems should be dependable enough for players to reason about them.

Transfer to SVGN: demonstrate a rule in the world rather than relying only on text, then reuse it under pressure. Build compact playable slices and iterate from observation.

## Dark Souls

Useful lens: interconnected geography, meaningful shortcuts, repeated landmarks, and powerful recognition when distant-seeming places reconnect.

Transfer to SVGN: use shortcuts as both practical rewards and mental-map revelations. Establish both ends before the reveal whenever possible. Do not copy punitive repetition or maze complexity where it does not serve the game.

## Dishonored 2

Useful lens: verticality, multiple approaches, functional architecture, consistent systemic constraints, and levels whose state changes alter decisions.

Transfer to SVGN: alternate entrances should change information, position, risk, or resource use. Vertical routes should provide real tactical or exploratory value. When the environment changes, preserve enough anchors that the player understands what changed.

## Hitman World of Assassination

Useful lens: dense social spaces, routines, conditional access, observation as gameplay, and opportunities created by changing circumstances.

Transfer to SVGN: use a small number of purposeful routines for residents, wildlife, or traffic. Give players ways to influence timing rather than merely wait. Adapt the social-space concept to peaceful cooperation in Leonardo's Guild and community circulation in Neighborhood Missions rather than copying assassination mechanics.

## Super Mario World

Useful lens: small action vocabulary, clear teaching, variation, secrets that affect route structure, and challenges that develop an idea instead of simply increasing obstacle count.

Transfer to SVGN: introduce one relationship, vary it, combine it with known systems, and give optional mastery without making the main route dependent on it.

## Metroid and Super Metroid

Useful lens: the world accumulates meaning as abilities and knowledge change what earlier spaces afford.

Transfer to SVGN: create memorable questions before providing answers. Distinguish ability barriers, skill barriers, and knowledge barriers. Revisit areas only when the return changes the experience.

Sky Cycle should emphasize knowledge and environmental change before adding more ability locks.

## Castlevania traditions

Useful lens: enemies as part of level geometry, deliberate encounter composition, and the way movement range, attack range, projectiles, and terrain combine into decisions.

Transfer to SVGN: place an enemy because it changes a route, landing, timing, or position. Do not simply fill empty space with enemies.

## Sonic series

Useful lens: movement composition, overlapping routes, speed as both immediate pleasure and learned mastery, and mistakes that can change the route rather than always end the run.

Transfer to SVGN: especially important for Sky Cycle. Design approach, launch, receiver, continuation, and recovery as one movement phrase. Speed should not be maximum at all times.

## Celeste

Useful lens: precision supported by generous interpretation of understandable player intent.

Transfer to SVGN: widen reasonable timing or collision tolerances where a player's intention is clear, but do not hide weak geometry behind invisible steering. Use recovery and buffered intent carefully and transparently.

## Outer Wilds

Useful lens: question-driven exploration and progression through knowledge rather than conventional upgrades.

Transfer to SVGN: environmental clues should create specific questions the player can pursue. Knowledge can be progression when understanding changes what the player chooses to do.

## Ghost of Tsushima

Useful lens: environmental motion and atmosphere used as navigation communication.

Transfer to SVGN: align wind, foliage, sound, lighting, moving machinery, water flow, or other environmental cues with real navigable routes. Atmosphere should reinforce direction rather than pretend to be pathfinding.

## Rhythm-game mapping practice

Useful lens: musical phrasing, note readability, occlusion, physical preparation, comfortable movement transitions, and the relationship between one action's exit state and the next action's demand.

Transfer to SVGN: Prism Current should treat each phrase as a level and evaluate hand state, reach, directional readability, recovery, and musical structure rather than importing spatial-path quotas from other games.

## Cross-reference rule

Use commercial games as analytical lenses. When applying a lesson, translate it into the receiving game's own verbs and identity. If a reference suggests a mechanic that would create menu friction, violate existing save or control contracts, or weaken the game's established promise, preserve the game and discard the imitation.
