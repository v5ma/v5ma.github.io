Aether Reach is moving from prototype layouts to authored worlds.

This production direction was set on September 15, 2026. It supersedes the previous next-task priority of another isolated animation or equipment refinement. The immediate target is a replacement Bellwether Blackout chapter, designed for first-person play and third-person AR/VR diorama play from the beginning. The successful mechanics, licensed assets, saved achievements and controller profiles remain valuable. The prototype geometry does not get automatic approval merely because it already exists.

This document is a production specification, not a claim that the replacement level or diorama renderer has shipped. The accompanying diorama-policy.mjs implements pure presentation rules and coordinate transforms. bellwether-authored-contract.mjs is an abstract route-and-milestone graph. Neither is wired into the current game. Their tests do not establish real geometric reachability, headset support, combat fairness or player enjoyment. The current live game is not changed by these unintegrated modules.

The project remains the existing public Aether Reach game, not the separate living-city experiment. No unpublished story material, private manuscript, new account service or remote analytics is introduced. Bellwether's proposed narrative details are public design proposals, not assertions about private canon.

The design unit is an unfolding chapter, not an island with independent features.

The player should see a problem, investigate its behavior, choose a route, use that understanding under pressure, discover a connection, and return to a visibly changed place. Architecture, machinery, opposition and movement must participate in that sequence. A functional switch beside a functional fight beside a functional rail is not sufficient unless the switch changes a useful decision in the fight or journey.

Aether's proposed distinguishing fantasy is becoming capable by understanding and repairing a connected floating city. Place identity must determine layout. A clockmaker's district needs public streets, repair access, galleries, apparatus and transport serving recognizable purposes. A foundry should later have different decisions and rhythms, not the same relay loop under another material palette. Increasing travel distance or adding more buildings is not a substitute for improving those relationships.

Bellwether Blackout will be the first replacement showcase.

The new arrival should frame the dark Theatre receiver above the market. A nearby motor attempts to run and fails. A visible connection leads toward the Arcade. The dispatch position belongs in this composition, rather than presenting an objective before the player has seen why it matters. The main destination should recur in later views: from the street, through a workshop opening, at an ascent landing, and finally beside the receiver looking back at the beginning.

A safe demonstration pocket should let a player test a small local circuit with an immediate visible result. Its purpose is understanding, not a mandatory text interruption or repeated button drill. A clearly marked manual test action supplies a basic-equipment route. Current can offer an alternative where the game already supports the relevant interaction; possession of Current must not become a hidden prerequisite for entering the chapter. The player can leave and revisit this pocket.

The first street encounter should support a direct public approach, a sheltered service approach and an exposed upper approach. The public approach gives ordinary cover and straightforward combat. The service passage provides a longer route, broken sightlines and access to an isolation control. The balcony reveals positions and an advantageous opening but offers less protection. These are not three decorated corridors with equivalent outcomes. Their differences must survive playtesting. The routes reconnect so that players can change their plan after the encounter begins.

The logical graph does not yet simulate the proposed isolation control's tactical effect. In the real blockout, operating it must visibly change machinery, a supported hazard or an enemy opportunity. A flag and a notification alone are not acceptance. Similarly, multiple cover pockets and lateral escape routes must be built inside the abstract market and rooftop encounter nodes; a single graph edge is not a finished encounter layout.

The optional workshop should explain something useful about the place while providing a clue or alternate entrance. One proposed scene is a repair bench diverted to public equipment. It should be understandable from the arrangement of objects, with readable text available as an aid rather than the only storytelling channel. This detour must not quietly become a required key hunt. Opening every door is not the goal; opened doors must justify the interruption.

The Clockmaker's Arcade should make supply, return and balance legible through distinct gauges, visible connections and a receiver sightline. Preserve an accessible maintenance-card solution. The existing 2, 1, 3 target can remain during migration, but a redesigned player should understand what a successful test changes rather than only copying digits. A failed test should show where the circuit stops. The scene renderer and mission logic must derive their apparatus states from the same source.

The service ascent must be a complete foot route. Landings should turn the player back toward earlier streets and expose their relationships. The optional rail approach should offer reconnaissance or a different arrival position, not merely reduce travel time. A missed transfer needs a readable recovery destination and a reliable route back. A safe lower route does not justify hiding the intended landing or adding arbitrary lethal edges.

The rooftop entrance should provide shelter and orientation before hostile pressure. The encounter should combine readable enemy roles, cover that can be left, lateral movement and machinery the player already understands. The receiver must be part of that encounter's spatial logic. Do not deposit rail arrivals into unavoidable crossfire or use extra health and repeated waves as the principal difficulty increase.

The final synchronization should show the district reconnecting. Keep any hold bounded by meaningful feedback rather than increasing a timer to manufacture duration. The return landing should unlock a shorter connection from its far side. Returning to the original dispatch through repaired lamps, running public machinery and a recognized sightline is the chapter's payoff. The reward must remain one-time and its completion flag must survive subsequent view changes and reloads.

The route contract contains arrival, demonstration, market, service passage, balcony, workshop, Arcade, service stairs, overlook, roof entry, receiver and return landing. These names specify purposes and relationships, not a mandate to build twelve rectangular rooms. Merge or reshape spaces when actual play improves. The graph allows early rooftop reconnaissance while preventing receiver activation before the Arcade milestone. It tests logical completion with walk, climb, interact and basic combat only. It does not prove navigation meshes, jump distances, ladder clearances, enemy behavior or correct floors.

The diorama is an alternate way to inhabit the same game.

Keep first-person XR as the inside-the-world view. Add a third-person VR diorama in a virtual surrounding space and a third-person AR diorama blended into the physical room. The observer can stand or sit above and beside the character and inspect the miniature world. The same mission state, enemies, projectiles, rewards and saved progress must drive every presentation. Do not fork the campaign or scale its physics to obtain a miniature appearance.

The outer enclosure has exactly three valid presets. Open top means the top is absent and the front is present. Open front means the front is absent and the top is present. Both open means both faces are absent. Both closed is not a valid state. Closing the sole open face must open the other in the same state transition. Corrupt preferences, mode switches, restart and controller or hand UI must all preserve this rule.

Top and front refer to the display enclosure. Building roofs and foreground walls need a separate authored cutaway policy. A closed outer top does not authorize hiding every building roof; an open top does not make secret rooms transparent. The table's front is a stable local direction that rotates with the table, not a face that silently changes whenever the observer moves their head.

The observer's tracked head, physical controllers and spatial interface stay at real scale. The miniature environment and its actors live under a visual world transform. The game retains its canonical metre coordinates. Pointer hits are transformed back into those coordinates before querying interaction or targeting. Third-person shots originate from the character's muzzle and use the game's actual line-of-sight and collision rules, never from the giant observer's hand outside the building. The coordinate helpers are implemented; renderer integration and these collision checks are not.

A player-controlled avatar must remain distinct from the observer. Left-stick movement should use a stable presentation-relative heading, not tiny head motions. Right-stick aiming and existing direct gameplay actions should retain familiar meanings. Repositioning, rotating or scaling the table belongs to an explicit inspection/placement state with the character paused and inputs re-armed on return. Do not steal a trigger, bumper or face button from an existing frequent action during combat. The main mode and enclosure controls should be reachable in the controller menu and the existing tracked-hand spatial UI.

Existing hand tracking operates menus only. This plan does not relabel it as hand-only combat or locomotion. Pointing and pinching can select view and enclosure settings; future character-control gestures require their own complete input specification, safe tracking-loss behavior and tests. Hands should not accidentally move the character while the user tries to reposition the display.

A bounded tabletop needs authored viewing sections.

Do not shrink the entire current city until the character and clues become illegible. Start with a chapter-scale composition with adjustable table scale, height and distance. Keep the display anchored while the character moves through a section. Walking around or leaning toward the display must not drag it along with the headset. Entry placement and deliberate recentering may move the table; routine avatar movement should not.

The campaign must support moving between authored sections without discontinuities in the simulated world. Stage a transition at a legible threshold, preserve the physical anchor and character state, and use a comfort-reviewed fade or explicit reframe rather than whipping the display after the player. A fast rail needs a designed view of its receiving route. It must not fire the character out of the visible box and demand an emergency camera move. Initial implementation must solve one complete Bellwether section before expanding this presentation across the city.

Each interior should identify its roof, foreground shell, gameplay boundaries, doors, discoverable contents and critical clue surfaces. Only authored visual shells may be cut away. Collision, enemy visibility, sound obstruction and projectile obstruction remain governed by the canonical game geometry. An unrevealed room can retain an opaque or schematic enclosure rather than exposing its contents. A transparent mesh alone is not a concealment policy.

Third-person overview inevitably gives different information from a first-person view. The fairness target is that both modes are understandable, complete and balanced, not that they reveal identical pixels. Tune ambushes, cover and clue presentation for those different viewpoints. Do not make a mandatory solution depend on a first-person camera angle, and do not let the diorama reveal every future room or bypass a character reach test. Track mode-switch behavior as part of puzzle and combat review.

The AR path must explicitly detect immersive-ar support and request it through a user action. A transparent background in an ordinary VR session is not sufficient evidence of passthrough AR. Inspect the granted session's environment blending, keep irrelevant opaque sky/background objects out of AR, and provide a clear unsupported-mode response without silently substituting VR. Automatic tabletop surface placement should use hit testing when granted; offer an explicit manual height/placement path when that optional feature is unavailable. No room camera recording is required by this plan.

The current runtime has a monolithic world scene and a player-following XR rig. Implement a deliberate separation between canonical game state, district/chapter render groups, physical XR rig, spatial UI and presentation transform. Do not indiscriminately reparent the entire scene or scale the tracked camera. Lights, effects, world labels, character meshes and pooled objects need a consistent owner. First-person view must restore the existing camera and weapon behavior on exit from diorama mode.

The blockout must pass behavioral gates before it replaces the live layout.

Start by building the arrival, street choice, Arcade, ascent, rooftop and changed return as one continuous route using the existing simulation and input actions. Generate visible floors and collision from a shared authored layout. Distinguish walking, stairs, ladders, one-way drops, doors and rails rather than connecting disconnected rooms with invisible teleports. Show representative lighting and feedback early when they communicate gameplay. Decorative density waits for route approval.

Run automated checks for actual foot-route reachability, collision agreement, prerequisite enforcement, basic-equipment completion, safe recovery, single rewards and every saved mission phase. Run a normal-input browser journey that traverses the replacement, not a test that writes player coordinates or grants mission flags. The abstract graph audit is an earlier, narrower check and must not be reported as that browser journey.

Play the same blockout in desktop first person, first-person XR, third-person VR and third-person AR with the three outer opening presets. Test seated and standing heights, indoors and on roofs, including a character behind a foreground wall. Verify that choosing a mode never resets the mission, that loss of a pose clears unsafe input, and that declining or ending an XR session leaves a usable game. Physical Quest and Xbox sessions are still required for comfort, tracking, input quality and sustained performance claims.

Observe unfamiliar players without explaining the intended route. Ask whether they notice the receiver, understand the failed machine, recognize alternatives, change their approach, and recognize the shortcut and restored neighborhood. Record confusion, repeated deaths, missed cues, unnecessary menu use and apparent unfairness. A low completion time is not automatically a better experience. Store optional development observations locally; no remote analytics service is implied.

The standard is not an arbitrary number of rooms, loops or features. A substantial region must justify its role, routes must differ in a way players actually use, an interaction must visibly matter, and the chapter must have an understandable ending. Automated tests can reject broken progression. Human play and physical-device review determine whether the composition and action are compelling.

Replacement must not erase existing progress.

Keep an immutable reference to the accepted prototype release and its test fixtures while building the replacement. Preserve the version-1 save reader, completed achievements, credited rewards, inventory and controller remaps. Never treat an unknown new layout revision as permission to clear storage. Before activating a new default layout, implement an explicit revision-aware migration and test every old Bellwether mission phase. Preserve the original payload before conversion. Map a checkpoint to a tested safe authored spawn; never reuse stale geometry coordinates blindly.

Existing completion flags should continue to suppress duplicate rewards. Any changed puzzle or mission semantics need a documented mapping, not a convenient assumption based on matching names. Keep a readable legacy recovery path until migration has been validated. This is not a recommendation to maintain two unrelated engines or retain the prototype as the normal starting campaign forever. Once the replacement passes its gates, new expeditions should start in the authored experience.

The next implementation task is the shared Bellwether layout and its continuous foot-route blockout. The following integration task is the distinct physical XR rig and miniature render root, using diorama-policy.mjs without modifying simulation coordinates. Then connect first-person/VR/AR selection and enclosure settings to both controller and hand menus. Only after route, migration, input, presentation and device evidence should the replacement become the default level and receive a new playable-release version.

Evidence at this planning checkpoint is limited to thirty local Node tests for presentation policies, coordinate transforms and the abstract chapter graph. The starter-equipment graph has 168 reachable abstract states across twelve spaces and no states from which logical completion becomes impossible. This is not a geometric or physical-device acceptance result. The existing live release and its archive status remain separate from this planning checkpoint.
