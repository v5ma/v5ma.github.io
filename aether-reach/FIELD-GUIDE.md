# Field Guide 0.15.1

This repair responds to the owner's negative physical playtest of Field Rotunda: no readable interaction text, no visible live map, a shallow world window, undiscoverable sizing controls, and uncertainty about what to do. Previous synthetic menu tests do not override that observation.

## What changed

A live north-up local map now occupies a separate room-floor stage inside the XR canvas. It shows the real character position and heading, nearby streets/bridges/rails and blocking solids at the player's elevation, and the selected objective as a gold diamond. A distant objective is clamped to the map edge. The dashed gold line is a bearing, not collision-aware pathfinding. A bottom strip also shows health, shield and ammunition unless the status display is deliberately hidden, so floor-dock overlap cannot hide those readings. The map refreshes at five updates per second; it is not the paused city atlas and never catches pointing rays.

The floor guide also shows a persistent next step and the current reachable object's interaction label. The existing Xbox interaction binding or Touch right grip is named; no gameplay bindings change. First arrival names Iona's noticeboard just ahead and right and explicitly says that buying a weapon is unnecessary. After the real noticeboard interaction, the guide follows the dispatch to Bellwether Market and explains the west stair. Bellwether states and traversal modes use their existing progression sources. Guidance never grants progress, picks a different task, moves the character, or pretends a straight bearing is a clear route.

Actual game messages enter an event feed instead of being sampled from hidden HTML. They appear near the floor for exactly 2,000 milliseconds of wall-clock time from their first presentation frame (not from an earlier event awaiting a slow frame): full opacity for 1,500 milliseconds, then a 500-millisecond fade. Pause does not make them immortal. Repainting or rereading a notice never restarts its timer. The latest 40 messages remain readable in the session's paged history. History is not an additional expedition save and is not retained across a page reload; task progress and the current next step are retained through the original save mechanism.

Touch Y or Xbox Menu opens the existing rotunda. Its first page now puts What do I do?, menu/map sizing, diorama views and the atlas beside Resume. Help provides the current next step, older/newer messages and text pages, and direct links to the two sizing panels. The noticeboard's auto-opened journal now foregrounds its actual message rather than only completion counts. In desktop mode the existing accessible dialogs and two-second messages remain; this is not a wholesale desktop HTML-to-canvas migration.

The box height defaults to 2.5 times the previous height. At the retained default scale its footprint is 1.8 by 1.44 meters and its aperture is 2.625 meters tall rather than 1.05 meters. Height multiplier is independently adjustable from 1 to 4. Overall diorama scale is adjustable from 0.015 to 0.08, and the prior table placement and legal top/front opening rules remain. The thin frame, fragment aperture, pointer interval and first-person window camera all use the same height. Full depth beyond the box remains; no filled glass wall or head-following plane was reintroduced.

Menu panel scale now ranges from 0.45 to 1.6. Floor-guide size ranges from 0.6 to 1.8 and its height can rise from 0.035 to 0.65 meters for seated reading. Old workspace and diorama preference records retain their existing values and receive defaults only for new fields. Menu size, map size, aperture height and world scale are separate controls. Explicit Recall repositions the floor stage; ordinary head motion never drags it through the game. Looking down does not itself open a menu or pause.

An additional bug was found in sound settings: enabling sound called an undefined legacy sound function. It now uses the existing soundscape event API. No new audio source or overlapping soundtrack is introduced.

## Verification and boundaries

Tests cover real-time notice expiry and preserved text, goal and interaction resolution without mutation, actual map geometry/position, bounded preference migration, and first-person eye/aim alignment for short and tall apertures. The rendering fixture retains nine earlier cases and adds three tall-window cases, including both scaled stereo viewports. The new ordinary-input browser journey walks to the real noticeboard, uses it, observes its message and expiry, rereads history, resizes panels through hand interaction, and Continues in first-person AR. No browser actor/progression/health/inventory/clock assignment is permitted.

Use the existing aether-rotunda and public portal workflows, not a new staging process. The seventh independent native field-guide report is required by release evidence collection for version 0.15.1 and later. A fresh local Chromium navigation was blocked with ERR_BLOCKED_BY_ADMINISTRATOR; it is not a browser pass. Native, public-byte, public-gameplay, downloaded-backup and physical-device results must be recorded separately in the release receipt.

All version-1 gameplay saves, remaps, rewards, collision/combat rules, current Bellwether, portal modes, licenses, historical reference material and private hub boundaries remain. The new helper is an X12 repair, not a new district or task system. Physical Quest readability, seated ergonomics, absence of the reported obstruction, controller reliability, performance and unfamiliar-player comprehension remain unverified until actually observed.

The first full guide journey passed its map, approach, prompt, dispatch and journal checks, then failed its notice observer. The observer had an eight-second pre-interaction cutoff despite slow software rendering. Its failure is retained. The observer now runs until it actually sees the notice and its expiry, within a bounded timeout; it never writes the game clock or progression. The presentation timer was separately hardened to begin once on first paint, and an actual post-render notice capture is required. Model fixtures verify that repeated reads do not renew it and that event/history timestamps are not mutated.
