This pass addresses the owner's failed golden-arrow escapes, two-second floor messages, and missing story interactions. Teleportation now resolves at the physical arrow's first collision. Floors retain a 0.28 m body footprint rather than the former extra 0.42 m landing margin. Slightly misplaced floor impacts can find nearby clear footing within 0.95 m at essentially the same elevation. Wall impacts search only the face actually hit and require supported ground and clear space along the adjustment. Exterior walls with no footing, ceilings, occupied destinations and blocked passages remain denied. The solver does not find a route through the wall, invent a floor, ignore enemies, or teleport to a distant safe checkpoint.

Real balustrade, rail, parapet and low-cover tops are valid narrow perches. Support is centred on the structural top; an avatar's footprint may overhang a rail. Ordinary enclosing walls, roofs, movable shutters and gates do not become climbable roofs. Head clearance includes the current tracked eye height. Support is queried from the existing solids rather than added to saved geometry. Movement and checkpoint validation use that same query, so a successful rail arrival is not rejected by the next frame or by Continue.

Blink flight no longer expires after four seconds. A physically airborne golden arrow continues until it hits or falls below the world's lowest supporting geometry. Prediction uses the same collision resolver and a geometry-derived flight horizon. There is no distance cutoff. Existing save fields stay bounded and unchanged: the stored life value remains at most eight seconds, while golden-arrow termination depends on geometry rather than that display/lifetime estimate. Other arrows and the deliberately short-range thrown disk retain their previous rules. Successful Blink moves the simulation's damage target together with the actor immediately, avoiding one update aimed at the abandoned location.

They fade during the last 0.5 seconds and disappear at two seconds, using elapsed wall time rather than a paused game clock. The old toast remains text-accessible but visually hidden.
