Leonardo's Guild: first-person and diorama presentation contract.

Status recorded September 15, 2026: requirements and architecture, not an implemented XR release. Master release.json still describes a seated immersive-VR theatre. A flat game texture in an XR scene does not satisfy the newly requested miniature world or first-person mode.

The owner requests first-person XR plus third-person AR or VR in which the user stands over a diorama containing the character's world. The diorama's top can be open or closed and its front can be open or closed, but never both closed. Preserve standing observation and offer seated height/reach adjustment without requiring the user to stand or bend over a real table.

Represent the enclosure with one three-value state, not two unchecked toggles. Overhead means top open and front closed. Front means top closed and front open. Corner means both open. Corner is the initial proposal. A request to close the last open face first opens the other face and only then closes the requested one. Transitions, saved settings, malformed preferences, input cancellation and tracking recovery must preserve the invariant topOpen || frontOpen. Animate between legal states through Corner when necessary. Neither an invalid saved value nor a reset may create a closed box.

The modes share one simulation. Ordinary third-person desktop remains available. First-person VR places the tracked view at human scale within the level. VR diorama renders actual stereoscopic miniature geometry on a virtual pedestal or in a neutral virtual room. AR diorama places the miniature in the user's physical surroundings using a supported immersive-ar session. The existing seated theatre remains an explicitly labeled fallback. No mode starts a new campaign or bypasses physical interaction requirements. First-person AR is not part of the requested first implementation; AR here means the third-person miniature.

Separate simulation coordinates from presentation transforms. World-space positions, collision, ranges, projectiles, saves and movement remain in the game's existing units. A single presentation transform maps a selected district into an XR-space enclosure with a position, yaw and uniform scale. Convert pointer rays through the inverse transform before querying authored objects, then validate the apprentice's physical reach, floor, line of sight and prerequisites. The observer's hand can request an interaction or designate a destination; it cannot retrieve distant parcels, operate remote valves, teleport the actor or collect rewards through a wall.

Render the real world geometry with a headset view for each eye. Do not label the current single-camera render-to-texture screen as a diorama. Keep one simulation update, avoid a second independent world, and preserve the current desktop presentation while implementing the new paths. The first-person camera must not inherit third-person boom collision or the procedural head bob. Its tracked pose and locomotion origin must be separate from cosmetic rig animation.

The enclosure is a presentation window, not the edge of the explorable world. Frame one meaningful place at an exploration scale; allow a broader overview separately. Showing the whole city at once would make actors too small and reveal too much undifferentiated detail. Author district framing bounds, interior framing and transitions at doors, stairs, gates or other readable pauses. Follow the apprentice only through a deliberate, comfortable framing policy. Do not slide or spin the whole miniature with every step, and do not silently transport the apprentice when changing which portion is displayed.

The front is a property of the placed exhibit, not whichever direction the headset happens to face this frame. Looking around does not repeatedly hide different walls. Rotating the exhibit is an explicit view action and carries its frame and cutaway orientation consistently. Recenter is separate from game movement. Repositioning and scale changes occur deliberately, preferably paused or out of an active challenge, and maintain the avatar's logical world position.

The exhibit shell and the architecture inside it are different layers. Opening the top removes the exhibit's top closure; it does not automatically make every room ceiling disappear or change a real roof's walkability. Interior roofs, front-wall panels and upper-floor occluders need author-tagged cutaway groups. Hide only the presentation geometry needed to read the active space, preserve collision and interaction blockers, and restore intact surfaces in first-person. Structure that is essential to understanding a staircase, bridge, passage or roof must remain readable.

Use explicit rules for active interior floors. A roof and front facade can be cut away to expose the active room without also exposing every cellar and attic. Keep a small floor marker and visual connection to its stairs. Do not freely stack opaque floors over the controlled character or erase the floor the character walks on. First-person may look through doors and windows; the diorama may inspect from outside; both must understand where valid entrances and exits are.

Bound the visible world cleanly with a base and authored back/side treatment. Excluded exterior world geometry must not leak outside the miniature or through the user's room. Geometric cuts need suitable caps or authored surfaces, including appropriate shadows and transparent-water handling. This rendering work has to be tested with actual materials; an abstract plane predicate or a screenshot of a box is not adequate evidence. The prototype's global terrain and lighting assumptions are not automatically suitable for a self-contained miniature.

Accept the observer's improved overview as part of the diorama fantasy. Do not base the central puzzle entirely on whether the human has peeked behind a wall. Use sealed containers, evidence interpretation, physical access and mechanism state to retain discovery. If a parcel is visible from above, the player has learned where to go, not completed its retrieval. Presentation-only cutaways do not become legal sightlines for the apprentice's interaction or combat. Preserve discovery state for genuinely concealed contents rather than exposing them by changing cameras.

Keep frequent actions direct in every input profile. For Xbox, retain established move, aim/use, jump, interact, ride/dressing and last-tool controls. Do not seize LB or the other high-frequency controls for opening and closing the display. Provide one explicit view control that cycles Overhead, Front and Corner, plus an accessible settings entry. Full controller remapping and a final XR button chart must be checked against the reconciled v0.13.1 input layer; do not publish a conflicting shortcut from this design document.

Tracked controllers move the apprentice with a stable orientation basis, not the camera's continuously changing facing. The recommended initial miniature policy uses the observer's horizontal facing sampled when stick movement begins and retains it until the stick returns to center. Looking around then does not suddenly bend an ongoing movement. Turning or repositioning the exhibit releases movement input first. Direct tool use continues through the game's normal action path. A pointer ray is useful for selection, not permission to use arbitrary objects remotely.

Hands retain point-and-pinch UI with release-to-arm behavior. A clear frame handle explicitly enters exhibit manipulation, separating the act of moving the miniature from ordinary UI selection or avatar movement. Provide panel equivalents for people who cannot use a two-handed gesture. Reachable movement and action controls, cancel/back, pause, settings and exit cannot depend on a physical keyboard. Test both hands, controller/hand switching, missed tracking, obstruction, unsupported hand tracking and repeated session entry.

First-person locomotion requires its own acceptance pass, not only a camera-height change. Check head clipping and near planes, hiding the avatar's own head, tool and interaction origin, seated/standing eye calibration, physical leaning near walls, safe pause on tracking loss and comfortable rotation. Offer snap turning and a stable horizon; evaluate smooth locomotion and comfort options on hardware. Camera recoil, forced head bob and miniature-scale transforms must not be applied to the tracked head. Riding can retain the seated-theatre fallback until a comfortable first-person vehicle view is deliberately implemented and tested.

Switch views at a safe stationary checkpoint with input released and a brief visual transition. Keep actor position, legal floor, heading, mission state, parcel ownership and equipment unchanged. Preserve the existing simulation pause rules, do not tick a second clock in another view and do not process an action once per eye. Mode selection should never unexpectedly end a session, reset a save or spawn a second player. AR and VR are distinct WebXR session types; moving between them may require ending and re-requesting a session with user activation rather than pretending a scene-background change is passthrough.

AR capability must be detected at runtime. The WebXR Device API provides session support queries and session requests; the AR module describes immersive-ar and environment blending. Use a requested hit-test feature where available for surface placement, and provide a deliberate user-adjusted placement fallback without claiming a real surface was detected. Plane detection, scene understanding, occlusion against real furniture and persistent anchors are separate optional capabilities, not implied by successful passthrough. Unsupported or denied AR falls back to a clearly labeled VR diorama or ordinary desktop mode, never a black box advertised as AR.

Privacy and comfort requirements are explicit. No room capture upload, hidden spatial logging, silent permissions or external account is part of this design. Request only the features used. Keep system exit reachable and stop held actions when visibility or tracking is lost. Do not claim safe physical placement, hardware frame rate or comfort from mock poses alone. Test Quest 3 controllers and hands on the actual browser/device combination after the native render path is available.

Evidence gates include all three enclosures at multiple viewpoints; never-both-closed transitions and invalid preference recovery; same actor state before/after scale and mode changes; both-eye occlusion/cutaway rendering; legal ray conversion with no remote actions; interior/stair/water/vehicle transitions; mode-specific shader compilation; old-save continuation; and physical hardware playtests. Separate CPU math, mocked XR sessions, native WebGL, device performance and human understanding in the release record.
