Leonardo's Guild: place-mastery level-design reboot.

Owner direction recorded September 15, 2026. This is an adopted direction and a proposed first-level specification, not a claim that the replacement world or new XR modes are implemented or published. Runtime release.json was read at master f4185c09d9345e47b76a06172c1b171fd3863b2b and still identifies Grounded Actions v0.13.0. No gameplay files or saves are changed by this checkpoint.

The owner explicitly authorizes replacement of the early prototype levels. Older instructions to preserve the existing layout must not be interpreted as a ban on reauthoring geography. Preserve the game's identity, working mechanics and earned player history, not every prototype coordinate, repeated building footprint or original route. The change does not authorize work on sibling games in this repository. The supplied methodology can transfer to Dino Atlas and Vesperfall, but Sky Cycle remains outside that shared plan.

The requested basis is the attached place-mastery synthesis. Its terminology is retained here: the physical, conditional, behavioral and information descriptions must describe the same environment. Discovery becomes knowledge; knowledge becomes agency. The synthesis's Leo's Guild example connects households, infrastructure, delivery work, roof and cellar routes, peaceful cooperation, and a recognizable return shortcut. The Waterwheel Quarter below is a new authored proposal applying that example, not an existing location described by the source.

The diagnostic is structural, not a graphics complaint. Current model.mjs constructs the town largely from repeated house rows along three road coordinates. It then expands households and related systems. Current readSave also checks delivered-mail identifiers against world.mailboxes. The first fact motivates replacing the uniform topology; the second means a careless replacement can reject an otherwise valid save. The code reading is not a claim that every existing encounter is poor or that no authored content exists. Living Stories, Cinder Hollow and Stillwater are retained foundations, not reasons to keep a weak spatial scaffold indefinitely.

The production decision is to author a coherent first district before expanding the replacement city. This is the first production level of the replacement campaign, not another disposable mechanics demonstrator. Early graybox geometry is a way to measure real routes before expensive art, not the intended player-facing quality. The next content priority is this district, ahead of automatically adding the previously proposed second basin or more template houses. Earlier production task identifiers remain useful and their incomplete acceptance gates remain open.

The proposed first district is the Waterwheel Quarter. Its learning statement is: the player understands how Leonardo's workshop, a dye household and a precision workshop share a water-powered goods route, then uses that understanding to recover a missing commission and reconnect the neighborhood. Its ordinary function comes first. Supplies arrive by street at a loading court; fabric is finished and dried upstairs; small mechanisms are assembled at the precision workshop; a shared hoist moves goods between the service cellar and an upper gallery. The disruption is a stalled consignment and an isolated service connection, not randomly scattered tasks.

The opening is composed around Leonardo's workshop and a distinctive bricked arch with a recognizably repaired bell bracket. The player sees that arch from the accessible side before setting out. Through it, the player can glimpse the far landing and part of the goods mechanism, but cannot take the locked service route. Across the canal court, drying frames identify the dye household, a moving escapement identifies the precision workshop, and the hoist silhouette identifies the objective area. These are proposed spatial landmarks; their final art and exact dimensions require blockout review. Color supports them but is never their only distinguishing property.

The arrival beat asks the player to form a useful plan before following an instruction chain. A short delivery or conversation explains what normally happens here and what failed. The missing parcel is located in the hoist gallery, but learning its location is not the entire puzzle. The player still needs a legitimate physical route. Looking from a diorama overview must not be treated as cheating or invalid play; route knowledge is welcome, and remote observation never substitutes for the apprentice reaching and handling the parcel.

The physical description contains three interwoven height bands rather than three long isolated corridors. Street-level courts and shops carry public life and vehicle access. The loft, drying roof and hoist gallery form an upper working route with observation and usable descent points. A dry landing, controllable channel and maintenance cellar form the lower service route. Ordinary stairs and ladders must support necessary vertical travel without requiring a new unsupported climbing system. A broken dramatic jump must never be the only route out of the opening.

The social approach runs from the workshop through the canal court, precision workshop and loading court to the hoist gallery. Cooperation with a worker opens legitimate access to the goods stairs. Its advantages are clear instructions, accessible walking and information about the delivery's purpose. Its cost is an authored piece of work or investigation rather than a bribe, a hostile checkpoint or a long mandatory wait. The worker can be approached at either end of their visible route. A recurring activity provides context; an explicit request supplies a deterministic alternative when the player does not want to wait.

The upper approach runs through the dye household, working loft and drying roof to the hoist gallery. It provides an early overview of the goods route and a clue in the stalled carriage's visible path. It trades extra elevation changes and observation for earlier independence from the loading-court routine. It does not become a safe but otherwise identical corridor. Connections back to the loading court allow the player to revise their approach without resetting the case. Railings, parapets and legal step heights are authored using actual movement, not an assumed new parkour system.

The hydraulic approach runs from the canal court to a dry sluice landing, through a lowered service channel and into the maintenance cellar, then up to the gallery. It reuses the calm drain-and-wade principle, not an unimplemented swimming feature. Lowering the water makes a real route usable and reveals the goods system from below. The valve platform visibly overlooks the consequence. Water control is reversible, costs no consumable, and cannot refill an occupied channel into an inescapable state. A dry exit from the cellar remains reachable. The channel also requires a stepped refuge back to the dry controls: depth may block entry, but never the recovery exit. This is an explicit geometry/depth-authoring requirement, not a promise supplied by an abstract graph. No drowning timer, compulsory fight or new music stream belongs in this opening.

These approaches lead to the same recoverable objective through different information and practical opportunities. No global prerequisite requires all three approaches. The case checks the actual authorized retrieval and physical return, not an invisible intermediate trigger on a preferred route. Clues can explain the story and suggest routes without becoming arbitrary permission flags that punish an observant player. The design graph records independent route assumptions; it is not a substitute for their eventual geometry, proximity checks or animation.

The discovery payoff is the service shortcut. From the gallery, a descent reaches the other side of the initially seen arch. Opening the latch reconnects directly to Leonardo's workshop. The bell bracket, workshop frontage and canal alignment make the recognition visible before any notification. The shortcut is usable before reporting the parcel and remains open after reload. It returns the player to repair and supply services and is useful in a subsequent neighborhood task; it is not a one-use loop included only to tick a checklist.

The behavioral description starts small and purposeful. The dye worker alternates between finishing work and the drying loft. The precision worker alternates between a bench and the loading court. A carrier connects the public court and the goods route. Each routine has a readable normal state, response to a direct request and settled state after assistance. The first build must use explicit bounded states and real navigable paths. A resident must not walk through walls, disappear when a camera changes, or carry away a unique required item forever. Resident counts and timing are provisional authoring parameters, not claims of existing simulation.

The information description names where a useful inference becomes possible. At arrival, a sightline establishes the destination and blocked shortcut. At the dye loft, the player reads the goods route from above. At the dry controls, a gauge and visible waterline show the mechanical consequence. In the service cellar, pipe and pulley continuity explains the infrastructure. On the return landing, the opening workshop landmark becomes recognizable from a new direction. Every clue has a destination in the player's subsequent decisions; ambient dressing that does not carry a clue can still provide relief and ordinary life.

Pacing should alternate arrival, protected observation, decision, local commitment, discovery and return. Quiet courtyards and ordinary passages give room to consolidate understanding. The proposed first unfamiliar-player target is a compact opening session, provisionally 20-30 minutes including exploration; this is a design target, not a measured runtime. A familiar return must be materially quicker and permit a different approach. Long waits, repeated introductory exposition and compulsory traversal of the solved approach fail the replay requirement.

Multilevel houses remain a design requirement, but their floors must have different purposes and relationships. A dye loft is not the ground floor copied upward with another collectible. The replacement campaign still needs homes, work, civic services, routes and optional expeditions. The old count of 49 houses is a compatibility/content obligation to account for across the replacement campaign, not a command to reproduce 49 identical buildings inside the first district. Every retained household or mission identity needs an explicit destination, adaptation or supported legacy location. No unported activity is silently represented as already rebuilt.

Vinci remains safe on all ordinary town floors. Social access, infrastructure, missing possessions, investigations and cooperation supply conflict here. Optional Cinder Hollow content retains explicit danger and return routes. Enemy roles, telegraphs, cover and combat pacing are designed for those regions separately; copying hostile-city conventions from the reference games would break an established promise. More empty land, additional enemy counts and a greater number of anonymous entrances are not acceptance criteria.

XR changes what can be observed but must not fragment progression. The first-person VR view, third-person VR diorama, third-person AR diorama and ordinary desktop view use the same level state and actor actions. The existing seated theatre remains a fallback. They are separate presentation requirements, not features already shipped by v0.13.0. Read XR-PRESENTATION-CONTRACT.md for the requested top/front rule, cutaways, discovery, controls and comfort requirements.

Replacement requires a compatibility layer before a production-world swap. Retain svgn.leonardos-guild.v1 and outer save version 2 unless a separately tested additive migration is needed. Separate stable mission, delivery, reward, household and vehicle identities from geometry. In particular, moving or omitting a visible mailbox must not remove its historical identifier from the save validator's compatibility registry. Paid work cannot pay again, completed folio/region/household work cannot reset, and old earned equipment and preferences remain valid.

Store an explicit layout revision and semantic arrival locations rather than trusting coordinates from a removed floor. Use a documented safe arrival at the workshop for incompatible positions, with parked vehicles moved to valid corresponding parking. Preserve progress even where an old mission currently needs a legacy layout. An active authored or generated run can remain on its supported layout revision until a safe transition. Keep a recovery copy before migration; never clear localStorage to make a new map load. This is a proposed migration contract, not migration code.

The production sequence is ordered. First build the stable content-identity and layout-version boundary and preserve a tested continuation path for old progress. Next author the Waterwheel Quarter with real collision, route geometry, normal controls and meaningful state changes. Build first-person and diorama visibility checks against that same level, not against an unrelated demo. Then test alternative-route completion, return/replay, interrupted interactions, every saved consequence and every camera mode. Only then expand the replacement city and migrate the next district. Production artwork, lighting, audio dressing and optimization support accepted spatial relationships rather than conceal missing ones.

Automated evidence must include conditional route connectivity, actual capsule/vehicle clearance, legal floor transitions, world-space interaction reach, no remote ray reward, serialized shortcut persistence, no duplicate payment and recovery from an interrupted water change. Each of the three approaches must complete in its own fresh journey. Head motion, miniature rotation and display scaling must not advance the actor, cadence, quest timers or travel distance. Test the first-person and diorama render paths with actual shaders and screenshots, then physically test Quest controllers/hands, Xbox and target-device performance.

Human acceptance is equally necessary. An unfamiliar player should be able to identify a plausible destination, name an alternative, explain what changing the water or access did, recognize the return arch and find home without continuous coaching. On replay the player should exploit their knowledge instead of perform the same instructions faster. Completion with no understanding is a design failure. Do not label green topology checks, many rooms or an attractive screenshot as evidence of enjoyment.

The adjacent waterwheel-quarter.design.json is a logical authoring contract, not a runtime map. Its nodes are places and its edges express proposed access; no coordinates, colliders or real path costs are implied. validate-design.mjs checks internal consistency and the three legal aperture configurations. Passing it is not proof of a playable route or a completed replacement level. The live game, its release identifier and all sibling projects remain untouched by this checkpoint.
