# Rainward v0.13.0 / Undertow

Northlight Natatorium is the seventh Rainward expedition and the first chapter built around deep-water traversal. It is an original municipal aquatic center inspired by the visual language of aging tiled pools: deep green-blue water, pale ceramic walls, stainless ladders, submerged lane markings, dim fluorescent fixtures, caustic light and empty service rooms. The supplied pool references informed the mood, not copied geometry or branding.

The natatorium contains four water volumes with different behavior. Its original chapter score, Tiles Below the Surface, uses glass-like tones and water ambience that intensify with danger. The warm-up pool is shallow wading water. The competition pool, diving well and flooded filter channel are deep water. Entering deep water automatically switches the character to swimming. In the Survival controller layout, hold B to dive and A to surface. Classic B or prone input also dives; A surfaces. Keyboard Z dives and Space surfaces. Horizontal movement remains left stick / WASD. Swimming reuses the existing horizontal crawl rig so the third-person body lies forward in the water instead of standing upright. Sprint input becomes a faster stroke rather than a land sprint. Diving is a toggle so the player does not have to hold a face button while steering and looking.

A one-hundred-point air meter appears only while swimming. Air decreases underwater over roughly twenty-eight seconds and refills at the surface or on dry ground. Health begins falling only after air reaches zero. Surfacing stops that damage immediately. Entering shallow water does not consume air. Underwater movement does not grant unconditional stealth at point-blank range, but distant patrols cannot see a fully submerged player through the surface. Surface strokes make more noise than submerged movement.

Weapons, melee, reload, healing, smoke and crafting remain unavailable throughout deep-water swimming, including at the surface. The mission's filtration fuse is physically below the competition-pool surface and cannot be collected from above. The pressure spindle, puzzle valves, shelters and required mission consoles remain dry and reachable without underwater combat. This release does not add firearms that fire underwater, underwater enemies, free vertical three-dimensional swimming, drowning cinematics or current physics. Those would require separate combat, animation and accessibility design.

The water shader uses three small procedural surface waves, view-dependent Fresnel brightening, moving pool-light patterns and edge foam. Separate animated caustic projectors illuminate tiled pool bottoms without adding a full-screen render target. Basin walls and floors use self-generated ceramic tile textures; competition lanes and stainless ladders are real scene geometry. The underwater view uses denser blue-green fog, lower exposure and a restrained screen tint. Reduced Graphics keeps the mission playable and preserves the water surface while existing graphics reductions remain in force.

The mission has two primary components: a submerged filtration fuse and a dry pressure spindle. The circulation puzzle asks the player to open the competition intake, keep the diving-well drain closed and bypass the filter return. Two required field tasks then restore the circulation loop and call the north service lift. Four optional tasks provide story, supplies and reasons to explore the locker room, pool deck, diving well and chemical room.

All earlier expeditions and chapter saves remain available. Save-bank storage expands dynamically from six to seven slots; prior six-chapter banks remain valid and do not invent a Natatorium save. Existing v1-v4 shelter checkpoints continue to restore. Water state and air are not persisted because all Natatorium shelters are dry.

The production checklist advances RW-031, Swimming and water encounter design, to Implemented foundation status. It does not mark swimming, water art, controller ergonomics, target-device performance, player comprehension or AAA quality as approved. Physical Xbox testing and unfamiliar-player review remain separate gates.
