# Comparison Wiki infographic donor checklist — 2026-08-28

Status: `ACTIVE-DESIGN-DONOR-BACKLOG`

This checklist preserves Micah's 2026-08-28 visual references as design donors
for the Comparison Wiki. It is a product-presentation backlog, not scientific
evidence and not a release authorization. Each adaptation must use the donor's
explanatory grammar without copying its protected text, artwork, distinctive
composition, or branding.

## Product contract

- [ ] Build every infographic as a compact 16:9 evidence slide, with an
  expandable full-screen view. The default embedded view should occupy roughly
  half the page footprint of the oversized reference Micah flagged.
- [ ] Preserve readable type at ordinary browser zoom: at a 1600 x 900 design
  canvas, body text must be at least 22 px and small labels at least 18 px.
- [ ] Use one main claim, one visual explanation, and one source route per
  slide. Dense newspaper-style systems become drill-down views, not tiny text.
- [ ] Make the same information legible in high-contrast black, high-contrast
  white, and selected color themes. Color may encode categories but may never
  be the only carrier of meaning.
- [ ] Every visual statement must drill down to the exact Micah passage, exact
  target passage, dates, translation, residual difference, and Four Lens
  evidence. Illustration is explanation; it is never evidence by itself.
- [ ] Keep intent-neutral boundaries. Similarity and chronology may be scored;
  access, dependence, copying, intent, misconduct, or legal conclusions require
  their own evidence and may not be inferred from artwork.

## Biological-realism gate

No biology-plus-art adaptation is product-ready until all applicable boxes are
checked:

- [ ] The cortex, white matter, ventricles, deep structures, cerebellum, and
  brainstem have plausible orientation, proportions, and relationships for the
  view being shown.
- [ ] Axons/white-matter tracts, blood vessels, and abstract information paths
  are visually distinguishable; decorative tubes do not masquerade as anatomy.
- [ ] Any tract path is anatomically plausible or clearly labeled as a
  schematic overlay.
- [ ] Excitatory and inhibitory activity are both represented when the concept
  requires them, and the visual identifies the phase-wave differential relative
  to tonic baseline rather than equating the mechanism with excitation alone.
- [ ] The image does not imply a localization, direction of causality, physical
  carrier, or resolution that the cited source does not establish.
- [ ] A human-readable legend separates measured anatomy, inferred mechanism,
  translation layer, and artistic explanation.
- [ ] A neuroscience source review and a presentation review both pass before
  the image is used as more than a clearly marked concept sketch.
