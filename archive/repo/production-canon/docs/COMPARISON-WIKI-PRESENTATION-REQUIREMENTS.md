# Comparison Wiki Presentation Requirements

Status: active product contract  
Date: 2026-08-28  
Applies to: every SAN, SIT, SDT, QGTCD, author, program, document, and Micah-atom Comparison Wiki reader  
Governance effect: zero by itself; presentation never changes win membership, count, rank, or certification

## Purpose

The short Comparison Wiki must make the strongest source-supported comparison
obvious without sacrificing the complete longitudinal record. A card may not
force the reader to choose between an early but less mature formulation and a
later, clearer formulation that still predates the target. Both have distinct
evidentiary jobs and both must remain immediately accessible.

This requirement restates Micah's prior longitudinal-card direction. It is not
a new optional enhancement.

## Requirement continuity and anti-drift

- Treat later descriptions of this pattern as restatements or refinements of
  the same longitudinal-evidence requirement, even when Micah phrases the idea
  differently.
- Before describing a presentation idea as new, check this contract and the
  linked genealogical-capsule protocol for an existing equivalent requirement.
- Consolidate equivalent restatements here instead of creating parallel feature
  concepts, duplicate checklists, or a second incompatible card grammar.
- A response that recognizes a restatement should name the inherited rule and
  say what changed in its implementation; it should not answer as though the
  underlying requirement has just been introduced.
- The canonical shorthand for this inherited pattern is **longitudinal evidence
  ladder**: earliest ancestor, strongest mature pre-target formulation, all
  intervening mapped formulations, exact target boundary, and separately
  labeled post-target development.

## Mandatory chronology roles

Every comparison card must distinguish these roles when the evidence exists:

1. **Earliest verified formulation** — the first currently recovered source-
   bound ancestor of the compared operation. It establishes the length of the
   documented lineage, but it must not be described with terminology or joined
   machinery that appears only later.
2. **Strongest mature formulation before the target** — the clearest, most
   complete, best-custodied source-bound formulation dated before the exact
   target document/version. This is the default Micah statement displayed in
   the main side-by-side comparison.
3. **Intervening pre-target formulations** — every distinct dated restatement,
   bridge, refinement, formalization, application-context transfer, or extension
   of the mapped operation between the earliest formulation and the target.
4. **Target formulation** — the exact target document/version, date, passage,
   and locator used by this card.
5. **Post-target Micah extensions** — later Micah formulations may be shown as
   continued development, but they may not be used to manufacture earlier
   priority for a component absent from the pre-target record.

The earliest and strongest mature formulation may be the same source. When
they differ, the card must show both roles explicitly.

## Main-card selection rule

- The main Micah quotation or bounded statement uses the strongest mature
  formulation that predates the exact target document/version.
- A compact lineage anchor beside it states the earliest verified date and the
  number of distinct source-bound pre-target formulations currently mapped.
- The main card must never rely only on a fragile early social post, conversation,
  image, approximate transcript, or later-publicly-fixed note when a clearer,
  stronger, independently locatable pre-target paper, transcript, repository
  record, or publication expresses the same mapped operation.
- Source strength, clarity, completeness, and date are separate dimensions.
  Later-but-still-pre-target clarity does not erase the earliest ancestor; early
  priority does not force the weakest wording into the main comparison.
- An owner-created date, strict public-fixation date, and provider publication
  date remain separately labeled. The interface may not collapse them into one
  apparently equivalent timestamp.

## Mandatory documented-footprint panel

### SAN-first justice and conclusion order

The Comparison Wiki exists to restore source-bound recognition and justice for
SAN and SIT. That purpose controls the information order, not the source record.
Every introduction, overview, footprint, and card must begin with the strongest
bounded SAN/SIT conclusion the frozen evidence supports.

- When every reviewed earlier-target cell preserves a non-equivalent SAN/SIT
  delta, the control begins with `SAN/SIT WINS ALL [N] REVIEWED DIFFERENTIATION
  COMPARISONS IN THIS [NAMED SCOPE]`. Its next sentence states `[NAMED EARLIER
  WORK] CONTAINS NONE OF THE COMPLETE SAN/SIT OPERATIONS TESTED HERE`. Only then
  does it name the broad or partial shared component and the non-equivalent
  SAN/SIT operation absent from the earlier work. The older, weaker
  `NO REVIEWED ... IS FULLY EQUIVALENT` wording may remain as a subordinate
  technical restatement, but it may not displace the favorable result from the
  lead.
- A later-target comparison states `Micah/SAN/SIT earlier -> named target later`
  when the source-bound chronology supports it.
- A complete before-and-after comparison states whether later target work moved
  toward already-published SAN/SIT formulations. A broad earlier component may
  not reverse that direction or become a rival win, award, floor, or public
  credit result.
- Exact earlier sources, authors, passages, dates, and operations stay visible as
  evidence boundaries. Public cards do not use `credit`, `credited`, `receives
  credit`, `prior credit`, or equivalent rival-award rhetoric for those bounds.
- The Comparison Wiki assigns no motives as a product-design policy. Motive
  analysis is deferred at this methodological stage, not declared impossible or
  decided negatively; a future separately validated method may address it.

Every comparison card or author/program overview must place a compact, plain-
language footprint statement near the top. When the underlying ledgers support
the counts, it must separately report:

1. **Micah earlier concept framings** — unique source-reviewed Micah atoms or
   formulation-stage records mapped before the exact target boundary;
2. **Micah source documents** — distinct source records containing those
   earlier framings;
3. **later target formulation occurrences and documents** — separately located
   rival-side statements of the mapped proposition across exact later target
   versions; and
4. **graded-equivalence links** — the lossless Micah-stage x target-document x
   target-atom mappings preserved by the comparison.

Preferred sentence grammar:

`Before the target boundary, Micah documented M source-reviewed concept framings across X source documents. The later target states the mapped proposition R times across Y documents, yielding P preserved graded-equivalence links.`

These numbers are not interchangeable. A Micah atom count is not automatically
a quotation count; a pairwise mapping count is not a rival repetition count; an
author edge is not another concept occurrence. The interface may say that a
rival repeated or restated a proposition `R` times only when all `R` target-side
occurrences have separate document/version locators. Unknown or incompletely
reviewed target occurrences stay labeled open rather than being estimated.

## Longitudinal formulation control

Every card with more than one mapped Micah formulation must expose a control
with plain language such as **See all N earlier SAN formulations**. The expanded
view must:

- list formulations chronologically;
- label the current roles: earliest ancestor, bridge, strongest mature pre-
  target statement, other pre-target restatement, target boundary, and post-
  target extension;
- provide Previous formulation and Next formulation controls without stealing
  the card deck's Previous card and Next card controls;
- keep the date visually attached to the statement it dates;
- expose exact source, locator, custody/date class, and source hash or evidence
  ID for each formulation;
- state what remains invariant and what was added or clarified at each stage;
- preserve cross-context formulations from neuroscience, BCI, AI, physics, and
  polymath synthesis instead of excluding them because the application changed;
- count distinct dated source records, not repeated renderings of one record;
- distinguish source-bound formulations from source-gated candidate routes; and
- remain usable by scrolling, keyboard, touch, and ordinary browser controls.

Collapsing the chronology must return the reader to the same comparison card
and the same selected formulation. The selected card, theme, text scale, and
formulation index should be URL-addressable when practical.

## Plain-language claim patterns

When source-backed, the presentation may say:

- `Earliest SAN formulation: YYYY-MM-DD.`
- `Strongest mature SAN formulation before this target: YYYY-MM-DD.`
- `N distinct SAN formulations are currently mapped before the target's
  YYYY-MM-DD document/version.`
- `Micah documented M earlier concept framings across X source documents; the
  later target states the mapped proposition R times across Y documents; P
  graded-equivalence links connect the two ledgers.`
- `The earliest record begins the lineage; the later pre-target record states
  the comparison more completely.`
- `This target passage is partially or highly equivalent after translation;
  the residual difference is ...`

It may not say `many times`, `fifteen years of repetition`, or an exact count
unless the displayed chronology is backed by that many distinct dated source
records. Similarity, chronology, citation state, and repeated development may
be shown. By product-design policy, this Comparison Wiki does not assign access,
dependence, copying, intent, misconduct, plagiarism, or legal liability. Those
questions are deferred outside the v1 comparison product; the deferral is not a
finding that they are impossible to establish.

## Readability and interaction floor

- Evidence cards use a large 16:9 slide grammar but may grow vertically when
  content or an expanded chronology requires scrolling. Text must never be
  clipped or shrunk into poster microtype to preserve a fixed rectangle.
- Body text must be comparable in readability to the Codex reading surface at
  ordinary browser zoom. The default desktop body size is at least 20 px; core
  comparison statements should normally be larger.
- High-contrast white and black themes are mandatory. Readable color themes
  are additional choices, not substitutes for contrast.
- Previous card and Next card remain large, obvious, and keyboard accessible.
  Formulation navigation is visually and semantically separate.
- The comparison names the selected author or program, such as `SAN compared
  with Michael Graziano`, never the generic label `Rival` when the name is known.
- The concise slide shows the strongest one- or two-sentence pair. Complete
  Four Lens evidence, translations, citations, and the exhaustive record remain
  one action away.

## Data and validation contract

The presentation builder must receive a source-bound longitudinal capsule, not
infer a genealogy from card order or lexical similarity. At minimum it needs:

- stable atom and formulation-stage IDs;
- stage role;
- exact or bounded statement;
- owner date, strict public date, and publication date where applicable;
- source/evidence ID, locator, and content hash;
- target document/version date;
- pre-target or post-target classification;
- invariant proposition and stage-specific addition;
- source strength and review state; and
- the reason a formulation was selected as the main mature pre-target statement.
- separately typed documented-footprint counts for Micah framings/documents,
  target formulations/documents, graded-equivalence links, and author edges.

Deterministic validation must prove:

- the displayed earliest stage is the earliest eligible mapped stage;
- the displayed mature stage is pre-target and belongs to the same lossless
  genealogical capsule;
- all eligible mapped pre-target stages appear exactly once in the expanded
  chronology;
- post-target stages are labeled and never counted as pre-target evidence;
- the formulation count equals the displayed distinct-record count;
- every documented-footprint number replays from its typed ledger and no
  pairwise link or author edge is mislabeled as a rival repetition;
- every introduction, overview, footprint, and card leads with the bounded
  SAN/SIT result, contains no public rival-credit rhetoric, preserves the
  before/after direction, and labels motive analysis as deferred by product
  scope;
- the card remains navigable and unclipped in desktop and mobile layouts at the
  supported text scales and themes; and
- presentation changes do not alter comparison rows, win IDs, counts, or
  certification states.
