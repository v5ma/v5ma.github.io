The traditional dating of Moses is debated, and there is no single scholarly consensus.

- **12,000 BCE** would be far too early. That period was the late Ice Age, before the rise of agriculture or complex societies. No historians or archaeologists place Moses anywhere near that date.
- **1,200 BCE** is within the timeframe most often proposed by biblical historians for an Exodus-like event, if it occurred. This would be during the Late Bronze Age, roughly contemporaneous with Egypts New Kingdom (Ramesses II, Merneptah).

Some biblical traditions imply an earlier setting around **1,500 BCE** (the 18th Dynasty of Egypt, e.g. Pharaohs like Thutmose III or Amenhotep II). But others favor the **13th century BCE** (around 1,250 BCE), since the Bible associates the Israelites with the city of Pi-Ramesse...
