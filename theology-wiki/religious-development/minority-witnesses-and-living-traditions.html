Minority Witnesses and Living Traditions

Preserving communities and evidence without turning them into interchangeable alternative Bibles.

Source criticism and preservation ethics / September 9, 2026

The opening concern about Samaritans, Yazidis and other vulnerable communities changes the purpose of the research. These are not merely repositories of unusual stories for a larger tradition to borrow. A serious comparative project must make room for people to describe their own texts, practices and disagreements, including their refusal of an outsider's proposed synthesis.

Different kinds of witness require different questions

The existing Samaritan study provides an entry into questions of canon, sacred place and authority. For a particular variant, the next step is to identify the reading and its witnesses rather than call every difference a deliberate alteration. Disagreement with a later familiar text does not automatically establish either corruption or greater antiquity.

Yazidi sacred transmission cannot be reduced to the printed books attributed to the community. Allison's historical account emphasizes oral religious tradition and explains why the commonly circulated versions of the Book of Revelation and Black Book have disputed authenticity. Some contents may echo genuine traditions without making the published work an authoritative ancient scripture. Equating the Peacock Angel with an outsider's Devil is not an acceptable starting assumption.

Lupieri's discussion of Mandaean history likewise records disputes about origins and transmission. Veneration of John the Baptist and narratives involving biblical figures make the tradition highly relevant, but a narrative's ancient setting does not independently date its surviving wording. A text can be religiously authoritative to a living community without serving as an unmediated eyewitness record of the first century.

What preservation should preserve

A useful source record would distinguish the community's own account, the language and genre of the work, the physical or recorded witness, the edition or translator, and the specific proposition under comparison. Where a hymn is recorded from a named performer, the circumstances of that performance matter. Where a manuscript is cited, its date is not automatically the date of the tradition it preserves.

This is a proposal for the wiki's research method, not a claim that such a catalogue is already complete. Community review, careful translation and appropriate permission would be especially important before publishing recordings or restricted ritual material. Nothing in this addition supplies permission to copy a sacred text merely because it is discussed here.

Repair must not require assimilation

The theological invitation to listen across traditions becomes more credible when it protects disagreement. A Samaritan, a Yazidi or a Mandaean should not have to accept the wiki's interpretive framework before their community's survival matters. Preservation serves people as well as hypotheses.

This gives Apocalyptic Repair Theology a concrete test. Does a proposed universal synthesis retain the ability of a smaller tradition to correct its portrayal? Does it preserve inconvenient evidence, or only material that confirms the preferred story? A synthesis that erases its sources reproduces the problem it claims to repair.

The present page is not a current demographic or security assessment. It does not repeat the pasted discussion's outdated political description of Syria, supply population estimates, or pretend that all Christian or minority canons are identical. Its narrower contribution is a standard for handling witnesses fairly while developing the larger inquiry.
