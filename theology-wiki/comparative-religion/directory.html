Ancient textual communities / Textual comparison

Qumran texts and Essene comparisons

The Dead Sea Scrolls contain multiple works and forms of community organization. Particular sectarian texts can be compared with reports about Essenes without making every manuscript the work of one monastic group.

Next research task: Compare individual Community Rule witnesses, War Scroll passages and external reports separately; retain disagreement about communities and dates.

Sources: John J. Collins and James Nati, The Rule of the Association and Related Texts (2024) / Israel Museum, Digital Dead Sea Scrolls project / Israel's angels and Qumran religious identity, Bible Interp, University of Arizona.

Ancient textual communities / Textual comparison

Sethian writings

A scholarly grouping includes writings with distinctive divine genealogies and a lower ruler. A common classification does not establish one fixed institution or two independent primordial creators in every tractate.

Next research task: Develop an Apocryphon of John recension comparison with exact passage locators, rather than a generic definition of all Gnosticism.

Sources: Gnosticism, Internet Encyclopedia of Philosophy.

Ancient textual communities / Textual comparison

Valentinian traditions

Ptolemy's intermediate just maker and Philip's ritual mysteries complicate the assumption that all esoteric Christians condemned the same evil creator and rejected all ritual.

Next research task: Distinguish named teachers, texts associated by scholars with the tradition, and testimony transmitted by opponents.

Sources: David Brons, The Demiurge in Valentinianism / Gospel of Philip, translated by Willis Barnstone.

Related developed study

Ancient textual communities / Source recovery prospect

Basilides and Basilidian testimony

Irenaeus I.24 reports a system associated with Basilides, but it remains an opponent's account. It cannot serve as an unquestioned transcript of all Basilidian belief and conduct.

Next research task: Compare Irenaeus, Hippolytus and surviving fragments before building a complete doctrinal profile.

Sources: Irenaeus, Against Heresies I.24, Roberts and Rambaut translation.

Related developed study

Ancient textual communities / Textual comparison

Hermetic texts

Hermetic works in Nag Hammadi Codex VI include a prayer joining knowledge, embodied transformation and a shared meal. Physical collection with other writings does not imply identical doctrine.

Next research task: Compare the Prayer of Thanksgiving's witnesses and the Discourse on the Eighth and Ninth in critical editions.

Sources: Prayer of Thanksgiving, Brashler, Dirkse and Parrott translation / Hermetic Texts in the Nag Hammadi Collection, Gnostic Society Library.

Ancient textual communities / Introductory reference

Marcion and Marcionite traditions

The tradition belongs in questions about scripture, law and the relation between creator and redeemer. It should not be equated automatically with every Sethian or Valentinian position.

Next research task: Add a dedicated primary-source dossier distinguishing hostile testimony, canon reconstruction and later Marcionite communities.

Sources: Gnosticism, Internet Encyclopedia of Philosophy.

Ancient and transregional movements / Comparative study

Manichaeism

The two principles, rescue of light and distinct community obligations need to be studied together. Invoking Jesus, Buddha and Zoroaster does not make their teachings interchangeable.

Next research task: Add named Syriac, Coptic, Iranian and Chinese witnesses, preserving which formulation belongs to which source.

Sources: Manicheism i. General Survey, Encyclopaedia Iranica / Werner Sundermann, Cosmogony and Cosmology iii. In Manicheism, Encyclopaedia Iranica.

Related developed study

Ancient and living traditions / Comparative study

Zoroastrian traditions

The relationships among Ahura Mazda and opposing spirits vary across historical texts and interpretations. One equal-two-gods formula is not an adequate description of every period.

Next research task: Compare defined Gathic, later Avestan and Middle Persian passages and add contemporary community perspectives.

Sources: Gherardo Gnoli, Dualism, Encyclopaedia Iranica / Jacques Duchesne-Guillemin, Ahriman, Encyclopaedia Iranica.

Ancient and living traditions / Selected textual comparison

Buddhist traditions

The nonself discourse supplies a precise comparison with a substantial liberated light-soul. Shared ascetic vocabulary does not establish an identical account of personhood.

Next research task: Expand beyond the selected early discourse without treating all Buddhist schools as one metaphysical system.

Sources: Samyutta Nikaya 22.59, translation by Bhikkhu Bodhi.

Related developed study

Ancient and living traditions / Comparative study

Mandaeans

A living tradition with distinctive scriptures, rites and community self-understanding. Its Book of John is not a canonical Christian Gospel, and Miriai's narrative requires its own textual treatment.

Next research task: Recover the critical Book of John and a clearly identified Ginza translation; compare actual sections rather than a composite end-times story.

Sources: Charles G. Haeberl and James F. McGrath, The Mandaean Book of John: Text and Translation / Edmondo Lupieri, Mandaeans i. History, Encyclopaedia Iranica / Mandaean Associations Union, Mandaean beliefs.

Jewish traditions / Selected textual comparison

Kabbalah and Hasidic traditions

Tanya supplies one account of divine vitality, the other side and repentance. It is not evidence that all Jewish mystics share one cosmology or now accept Christian doctrines.

Next research task: Add distinct Zoharic, Lurianic and Hasidic witnesses, preserving the difference between schools, texts and living communities.

Sources: Tanya, Iggeret HaTeshuvah, chapter 6.

Jewish traditions / Community-source introduction

Karaite Judaism

Rejection of rabbinic Oral Law as binding does not entail the absence of commentary, legal interpretation or inherited practice. The community's own reading-room catalogue demonstrates those genres.

Next research task: Develop passages on scriptural interpretation and practice using named Karaite authors rather than the formula only literal text.

Sources: Karaite Jews of America, Reading Room.

Israelite traditions / Community-source introduction

Samaritans

The Samaritan museum presents a distinctive five-book Torah and priestly heritage. The community is not adequately described merely as an unusual modern Jewish denomination.

Next research task: Expand its own account of Gerizim, textual witnesses and rites; distinguish traditional ancestry from independently documented chronology.

Sources: Samaritan Museum, About the Museum.

Beta Israel

ANU Museum's Orit exhibit documents a named sacred scroll, eight-book contents and custodial history. It supplies a concrete starting point rather than a story of an unchanging community completely isolated for a fixed millennium.

Next research task: Add Ethiopian-language texts, priestly perspectives and histories of community change and migration.

Sources: ANU Museum, The Orit Scroll of Kes Abba Yitzhak Aysu.

Jewish histories / Research prospect

Anusim, conversos and crypto-Jewish histories

Forced conversion, covert observance and later descendants concern historical circumstances and diverse identities, not one uniformly constituted denomination. Present identity must not be assigned from ancestry alone.

Next research task: Develop geographically and chronologically specified cases using archives and community testimony; do not assume every family transmitted identical practices.

Sources: Crypto-Jewish Education Project, About.

Eastern Christian traditions / Doctrinal comparison

Coptic Orthodox and Coptic Catholic churches

Distinguish the Coptic Orthodox Church from the Coptic Catholic Church. The 1973 Orthodox-Catholic declaration affirms Trinity and real humanity and divinity, not an anti-incarnational Gnostic creed.

Next research task: Add church-specific histories and rites without substituting ancient Egyptian manuscript geography for doctrinal identity.

Sources: Common Declaration of Pope Paul VI and Pope Shenouda III, May 10, 1973 / CNEWA, The Catholic Eastern Churches.

Eastern Christian traditions / Directory introduction

Maronite Catholic Church

An Eastern Catholic church with its own tradition; communion with Rome does not mean every Catholic uses the Latin rite. An Eastern liturgy is not evidence of Gnostic cosmology.

Next research task: Add primary liturgical and synodal sources, with a dedicated historical chronology.

Sources: CNEWA, The Catholic Eastern Churches.

Assyrian Church of the East and Chaldean Catholic Church

These require distinct church entries despite related East Syriac heritage. Neither the name Assyrian nor a historical label such as Nestorian constitutes a claim that the church is Gnostic.

Eastern Christian traditions / Historical introduction

Saint Thomas Christians

Kerala's Thomas Christian heritage now spans distinct church affiliations. Apostolic origins should be identified as a tradition rather than treated as a securely witnessed arrival date for every later community.

Next research task: Give the Syriac traditions and later Catholic, Orthodox and other institutional histories their own sources.

Sources: CNEWA, The Thomas Christians.

Latter-day Saints

The church's own introduction affirms additional scripture and a Godhead account distinct from Nicene consubstantiality. Those claims should be represented in their own terms rather than called a surviving ancient Gnostic sect.

Next research task: Add primary doctrinal and historical entries while distinguishing religious claims from independently established events.

Sources: Latter-day Saints 101, official church introduction.

Muslim traditions / Orientation

Sunni and Shia traditions

Shared commitments coexist with differing accounts of authority, law and interpretation. These broad labels contain internal schools and communities rather than two completely uniform sets of practice.

Next research task: Build separate histories of jurisprudence, theology and imamship using sources specific to each school.

Sources: The Pluralism Project, One Ummah with Many Views.

Muslim traditions / Orientation

Sufi traditions and orders

Sufism describes devotional and interpretive streams and orders, not a mutually exclusive third denomination beside Sunni and Shia. Remembrance is one important practice, not the full identity of every order.

Next research task: Compare named teachers, texts and institutions; keep practice, doctrine and communal affiliation separate.

Sources: The Pluralism Project, Sufism / The Pluralism Project, dhikr.

Related developed study

Muslim traditions / Community-source introduction

Ahmadiyya Muslim traditions

The community traces its foundation to Mirza Ghulam Ahmad in Qadian in 1889, in British India. Its prophetic and messianic claims and its Muslim self-identification need to be distinguished from other authorities' assessments.

Next research task: Add internal branches and disagreements through their own documents, rather than treating Ahmadiyya as a type of Sufi order.

Sources: Ahmadiyya Muslim Community, official introduction / Ahmadiyya Muslim Community Greece, community and founder account.

Next research task: Compare specific doctrines of authority and law across time rather than universalizing a modern shorthand.

Distinct living traditions / Community-source introduction

Baha'i Faith

The community names Buddha and Zoroaster within progressive revelation, alongside other divine educators. This is its theology of religious relationship, not evidence that all earlier figures taught one identical system.

Next research task: Develop the meaning of Manifestation and the tradition's historical emergence through primary and independent sources.

Sources: Baha'i international community, introductory teachings.
