This is the September 18, 2026 comparative-religion expansion of the Theology Wiki. [Open the study series](index.html), [the tradition directory](directory.html), [the source register](sources.html), or [the claim and quotation audit](claim-audit.html). The complete plain-text version of the developed studies is [READING.md](READING.md).

The series contains nine developed studies and an introductory directory of 36 ancient textual schools or living traditions. Twenty claim reviews distinguish consequential conflations from supported comparisons. Six quotations attributed in the supplied conversation to modern scholars remain explicitly unverified. Their original wording is not silently adopted as authenticated scholarship, and the original conversations are not modified.

The argument supports a precise historical conclusion: several Jewish witnesses before Christianity describe hostile spiritual powers under divine sovereignty. It distinguishes that claim from doctrinal acceptance by later Jewish authorities and from metaphysical proof. Maimonides is examined through identifiable primary passages rather than a general claim that he eradicated all apocalyptic thought. Iranian and Greek connections are considered as distinct historical hypotheses rather than a single automatic synthesis.

The author's proposed common thread is developed as a shared revelatory-transformative repertoire. The comparison retains creator, covenant, body, ritual, authority and restoration as variables rather than subtracting the very differences that would distinguish a historical genealogy. Qumran, Sethian, Valentinian, Mandaean and Hermetic witnesses are not assigned one voice. A proposed transmission requires evidence beyond shared vocabulary.

The Mandaean study separates Miriai from the supplied composite biography and records the missing passage-level verification for the detailed Ginza end-time sequence. The Nag Hammadi study preserves ritual in Philip, the affirmation of resurrection in the Treatise on the Resurrection, and the separate Codex Tchacos provenance of the Gospel of Judas. The interreligious study distinguishes historical appreciation of Jesus, cooperation and assent to Christian doctrine. The directory does not treat Eastern churches as Gnostic merely because of geography or age.

Sources.json and sources-more.json contain bibliographic witnesses and explicit access scopes: primary texts, translations, scholarly arguments, community self-descriptions, indexed passages and abstract-only records. These are not all full-book readings. There is no claim of fresh original-language manuscript collation or a survey establishing a new Hasidic trend. Claims of a definite unbroken lineage and the six long scholarly attributions are not treated as proven.
