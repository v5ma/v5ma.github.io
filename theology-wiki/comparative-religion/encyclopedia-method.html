Building an encyclopedia without flattening living traditions

Breadth should come from traceable entries and explicit coverage, not confident generalizations about whole populations.

The editorial ambition and its actual present scope

The Wiki can pursue encyclopedic depth while retaining its original research questions. A substantial encyclopedia explains why texts are related, what a community claims about itself, how outsiders have described it, and where the evidence remains disputed. It does not gain depth by giving every small tradition a compressed label such as secretive, progressive, primitive or purely mystical.

This edition is a comparative foundation, not a completed rival to a multivolume encyclopedia. Its long studies support shorter directory entries. Entries whose primary evidence has not yet been examined say so. A useful index can name unfinished work without presenting a placeholder as a completed history.

Three kinds of entry

A textual entry identifies a work, manuscript witnesses, translations, dating proposals and the passage under discussion. A tradition entry identifies how a living community describes itself, with internal differences and clearly dated external research. A research-hypothesis entry states the proposed relationship and evidence that would distinguish it from alternatives. None substitutes for the others.

For example, a Book of John edition is not a demographic survey of Mandaeans, and a museum's Orit scroll is not the entirety of Beta Israel's history. An artifact can be unusually valuable precisely because its owner, contents and custody are specific. Generalization needs additional witnesses, not the removal of that specificity.

Sources: Charles G. Haeberl and James F. McGrath, The Mandaean Book of John: Text and Translation / ANU Museum, The Orit Scroll of Kes Abba Yitzhak Aysu.

Self-description and independent study both matter

A community source is indispensable for understanding what its members or institutions publicly affirm. It is not an independent verification of every historical origin story. A scholar's reconstruction may explain a transmission history, but it should not silently replace a living community's own self-name. The encyclopedia makes both kinds of source available and distinguishes the questions each can answer.

Karaites provide a useful example. A community bibliography of commentaries, law and liturgy shows why rejection of rabbinic Oral Law as binding authority is not the same as absence of interpretation. For Samaritans, their own museum makes their Torah and priestly tradition visible without requiring the encyclopedia to decide that they are merely a branch of someone else's modern denomination.

The same principle applies to hostile accusations. A polemical report about Basilides is a witness to Irenaeus's account, not an unfiltered record of everything Basilidians believed or did. A contested printed Yazidi sacred book cannot be substituted without qualification for the community's oral sacred tradition.

Sources: Karaite Jews of America, Reading Room / Samaritan Museum, About the Museum / Irenaeus, Against Heresies I.24, Roberts and Rambaut translation / Christine Allison, Yazidis i. General, Encyclopaedia Iranica.

An evidence-bearing wikilink

A useful link states its relationship: interprets, quotes, contrasts with, preserves a related manuscript, reports an accusation, or proposes a line of transmission. Two pages sharing a word should not automatically be connected as proof of dependence. The main repair article links to this series because it develops the historical distinction between cosmic opposition and an autonomous evil creator, and because it tests the proposed common repertoire.

The module's sources record whether a text was read directly, available only through an indexed passage, represented by an abstract, or not recovered. The six long attributed scholarly quotations supplied in the conversation remain unverified rather than being promoted into direct evidence. A verified paraphrase may replace an unverified quotation in the argument; the two are not the same artifact.

Textual similarity is not a quantity that proves a lineage by accumulation alone. Independent witnesses, repeated uses of the same source, and a modern paraphrase quoting an older paraphrase must be distinguished. The reader should be able to follow the proposed relation back to a source and see what would change its status.

The next level of depth

The most productive additions are critical-edition comparisons of the Community Rule, the relevant Mandaean passages, and selected Nag Hammadi tractates; a named-speaker chronology of Jewish reception of Jesus; and histories of individual churches and communities using both their own documents and independent scholarship. The broad claim about an unbroken underground synthesis should remain a question until those smaller bridges are demonstrated.

None of that requires postponing the constructive theology. The author can explicitly choose an ethical synthesis in which suffering calls for repair, authority remains correctable and spiritual transformation is tested through conduct. What must not be implied is that every ancient author or every present community has already signed that synthesis.
