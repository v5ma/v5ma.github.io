Explicit textual appeal

Luke's afterlife narrative directs its hearers to Moses

From: Luke 16:19-31

To: Moses and the prophets as an already available obligation

Source record: The story's conclusion explicitly directs the living to the inherited texts rather than requiring a new messenger from the dead.

Proposed inference: The author reads future judgment as exposing failure to meet an existing obligation, not replacing concern for present suffering.

Limit or alternative: This is a literary and theological reading; it cannot recover what a historical Moses would have thought of Jesus.

Textual counterexample

Sheol and embalming occur within the Torah

From: Genesis 37:35 and Genesis 50

To: The supplied claim of complete Torah silence about death

Source record: Jacob refers to Sheol, and the narrative depicts Jacob's and Joseph's embalming without explicit condemnation of those acts.

Proposed inference: A practical emphasis on life and land is compatible with some language about the dead.

Limit or alternative: Neither example proves a complete later heaven-and-hell system, or every belief held by a historical Moses.
