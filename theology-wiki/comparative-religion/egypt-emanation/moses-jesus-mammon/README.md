The central positive bridge is Luke 16:19-31's appeal to Moses and the prophets, interpreted as an existing obligation made consequential in an afterlife narrative. The proposed ethical distinction is between protection of life and an attempt to obtain exemption from responsibilities to other people. This is a constructive reading, not a recovered interview with Moses or a proven uninterrupted Egypt-Persia-Jesus genealogy.

The Egyptian Seed and Hyksos/Exodus hypotheses remain identified and available for research. New comparisons use the NGV inscription of Iti and the Met monument of Neferiu: care for others appears within Egyptian funerary self-presentation itself. The study therefore does not define all Egyptian religion as an absence of moral responsibility. It also preserves the differences among Aaron's calf, Moses's response, divine-name narratives, Sheol, ritual impurity, seventh-year release and Jubilee. Sources describing Pharisees and Sadducees are not transformed into a verdict on all Jews or the private motives of every ancient member.

Twenty-nine sources include translated scripture, an ancient account, museum object records, first-party archaeology, a lexical study and an explicitly abstract-only journal reading. The new modern application is a proposed test of access and externalized harms, not a researched allegation about unnamed wealthy individuals. Medical improvement, safety and future planning are not classified as intrinsically exploitative.
