This is the plain-text index of the 60 dedicated group pages in the Abrahamic inheritance atlas. Inclusion is not an endorsement of any chosenness claim.

African Hebrew Israelites of Jerusalem
Claim type: Recovered / reidentified Israel
Family: Israelite-identification movements

The African Hebrew Israelites of Jerusalem developed a specific Israelite identity, migration and communal-restoration narrative within the wider Black Hebrew Israelite field.

Their claims should be studied as the claims of this particular movement rather than generalized to Black Jews or all Hebrew Israelites.

Ahmadiyya Muslim traditions
Claim type: Islamic Abrahamic restoration with later messianic claim
Family: Muslim traditions

Ahmadiyya identifies as Muslim and therefore inherits Islam's Abrahamic restoration claim while adding distinctive claims concerning Mirza Ghulam Ahmad.

Its Muslim self-identification and other Muslim authorities' disagreements should both be represented without the Wiki deciding a reader's faith.

Existing Wiki context: ../manichaeism-and-prophetic-lineages.html

Alawite / Nusayri traditions
Claim type: Abrahamic genealogy but not a simple chosen-people claim
Family: Distinct living traditions

Alawite or Nusayri traditions developed in an Islamic setting and remain part of the broader Abrahamic religious history while preserving distinctive teachings and communal identity.

Restricted or disputed teachings should not be invented from outsider analogies.

Existing Wiki context: ../encyclopedia-method.html

Anusim, conversos and crypto-Jewish histories
Claim type: Recovered Jewish continuity / historical identity
Family: Jewish histories

Anusim, converso and crypto-Jewish histories concern people forced into other public identities while Jewish ancestry or practices could persist.

This is a history of coercion, continuity and descendants, not one denomination with a single present-day doctrine.

Existing Wiki context: ../encyclopedia-method.html

Assyrian Church of the East and Chaldean Catholic Church
Claim type: Christian heirs of Abraham
Family: Eastern Christian traditions

The Assyrian Church of the East and Chaldean Catholic Church both inherit the biblical Abraham-Israel-Christ narrative through East Syriac Christianity.

They require separate ecclesial histories even where language, liturgy and regional heritage overlap.

Existing Wiki context: ../jewish-and-christian-boundaries.html

Baha'i Faith
Claim type: Progressive / universal revelation
Family: Distinct living traditions

The Baha'i Faith places Abraham within a progressive history of divine revelation that continues through later Manifestations of God.

This universalizes inheritance rather than claiming that one ethnic community alone replaces Israel.

Existing Wiki context: ../manichaeism-and-prophetic-lineages.html

Basilides and Basilidian testimony
Claim type: Comparative / Christian esoteric inheritance
Family: Ancient textual communities

Basilidian traditions belong to the contested early-Christian field and are relevant as rival claims about authentic revelation.

Most surviving descriptions come through opponents, so a confident reconstruction of their Abrahamic self-understanding requires source recovery.

Existing Wiki context: ../encyclopedia-method.html

Beta Israel
Claim type: Jewish / Israelite covenant continuity
Family: Jewish traditions

Beta Israel belongs to the history of Jewish and Israelite identity in Ethiopia and later Israel.

Its history should be studied through its own textual and communal witnesses rather than reduced to one lost-tribe legend.

Existing Wiki context: ../encyclopedia-method.html

Black Hebrew Israelite movements
Claim type: Recovered / reidentified Israel
Family: Israelite-identification movements

A diverse field of Black Hebrew Israelite movements identifies particular African-descended peoples with biblical Israel or the lost tribes.

This is not one denomination, and ancestry claims, racial teachings and relations with Judaism differ sharply among groups.

Existing Wiki context: ../encyclopedia-method.html

British Israelism / Anglo-Israelism
Claim type: Recovered lost-tribe identity
Family: Israelite-identification movements

British-Israelite movements claim that British or related peoples descend from the lost tribes of Israel and therefore inherit biblical promises.

Genealogical assertion, theological use and historical evidence are separate questions; the page is an orientation stub pending dedicated source work.

Existing Wiki context: ../encyclopedia-method.html

---

Buddhist traditions
Claim type: Comparative / outside Abrahamic inheritance
Family: Ancient and living traditions

Buddhist traditions enter the Wiki through Manichaean, Baha'i, Ahmadiyya and comparative-esotericism discussions rather than through an Abrahamic covenant claim.

They are a control case showing that universal prophetic systems can incorporate non-Abrahamic teachers.

Existing Wiki context: ../manichaeism-and-prophetic-lineages.html

Catholic Christianity
Claim type: Christian heirs of Abraham
Family: Christian traditions

Catholic Christianity places the Church within Abrahamic and Israelite salvation history through Christ while contemporary Catholic teaching also rejects portraying Jews as simply rejected or cursed by God.

This page should distinguish participation in Abraham's call from crude replacement formulas.

Existing Wiki context: ../jewish-and-christian-boundaries.html

---

Christian Identity
Claim type: Racialized Israelite replacement claim
Family: Israelite-identification movements

Christian Identity developed from British-Israelite ideas into explicitly racialized interpretations of biblical Israel and chosenness.

Its racist and antisemitic doctrines must be described as features of that movement rather than attributed to ordinary Christianity, Judaism or British-Israelism as a whole.

Existing Wiki context: ../encyclopedia-method.html

---

Christianity generally
Claim type: Christian heirs of Abraham
Family: Christian traditions

Christianity broadly reads the Abrahamic promise through Christ; Galatians 3 is a classic formulation in which those who belong to Christ are called Abraham's offspring and heirs according to promise.

Christian traditions disagree sharply about Israel, the Church, supersession, covenant and the continuing role of the Jewish people.

Existing Wiki context: ../jewish-and-christian-boundaries.html

Conservative Judaism
Claim type: Jewish covenant continuity
Family: Jewish traditions

Conservative Judaism understands Jewish covenantal peoplehood as continuous while combining commitment to halakhic tradition with historical development and institutional interpretation.

This page is an orientation stub pending dedicated movement sources.

Existing Wiki context: ../maimonides-and-mystical-continuities.html

Coptic Orthodox and Coptic Catholic churches
Claim type: Christian heirs of Abraham
Family: Eastern Christian traditions

Coptic Orthodox and Coptic Catholic Christians participate in the Christian claim that the Abrahamic promises reach their fulfillment through Christ.

The two churches are distinct institutions and should not be conflated simply because both are Egyptian.

Existing Wiki context: ../jewish-and-christian-boundaries.html

Druze tradition
Claim type: Abrahamic genealogy but not a simple chosen-people claim
Family: Distinct living traditions

The Druze tradition developed historically from an Ismaili environment and therefore belongs in the wider Abrahamic genealogy while developing a distinct religious identity.

It should not be forced into the formula 'we are the true Israel' unless a specific source makes that claim.

Existing Wiki context: ../manichaeism-and-prophetic-lineages.html

Early Jewish Jesus movement
Claim type: Internal Jewish fulfillment claim
Family: Ancient Jewish-Christian movements

The earliest Jesus movement emerged within Jewish life and initially presented claims about Messiah, Israel and covenant before Christianity became a predominantly non-Jewish religion.

This page distinguishes an internal Jewish messianic claim from later Christian theories of covenantal inheritance.

Eastern Orthodox Christianity
Claim type: Christian heirs of Abraham
Family: Christian traditions

Eastern Orthodox Christianity understands the Church within the biblical history of Abraham, Israel, covenant and fulfillment in Christ.

The many autocephalous churches share communion and tradition without becoming one centralized administrative institution.

Existing Wiki context: ../jewish-and-christian-boundaries.html

Ebionites and related Jewish-Christian traditions
Claim type: Torah-observant Jewish-Christian continuity
Family: Ancient Jewish-Christian movements

Where reconstructable, Ebionite traditions represent attempts to combine Jesus as Messiah with continued Torah-observant Jewish identity.

Most evidence is late and often comes through opponents, so this page remains a source-recovery prospect.

Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo Christianity
Claim type: Christian heirs of Abraham with strong Israelite symbolism
Family: Oriental Orthodox Christianity

Ethiopian Orthodox Christianity participates in Christian Abrahamic inheritance while giving unusually prominent roles to Zion, the Ark, Solomon and the Queen of Sheba traditions.

Traditional sacred history, liturgy and independently established chronology should remain distinct.

Greek Orthodox and Melkite Greek Catholic traditions
Claim type: Christian heirs of Abraham
Family: Eastern Christian traditions

Greek Orthodox and Melkite Greek Catholic traditions both inhabit the Christian Abrahamic narrative while belonging to different ecclesial communions.

Their shared Byzantine heritage does not erase their institutional differences.

Hasidic Judaism
Claim type: Jewish covenant continuity
Family: Jewish traditions

Hasidic Judaism operates within Orthodox Jewish peoplehood and covenant while emphasizing distinctive devotional, mystical and communal forms around Torah, prayer and the tzaddik or rebbe.

Hasidic groups are not one centralized denomination and should not be assigned one attitude toward Christianity.

Existing Wiki context: ../maimonides-and-mystical-continuities.html

Family: Restorationist Christian movements

Hebrew Roots movements urge Christians to recover Torah, Hebrew names, biblical feasts or other perceived Israelite foundations of faith.

Claims range from cultural or theological restoration to stronger assertions about Israelite identity, so individual organizations require separate sourcing.

Ibadism
Claim type: Islamic Abrahamic restoration

Ibadism is an early and distinct Muslim tradition and therefore participates in Islam's claim to Abrahamic continuity.

Islam generally
Claim type: Islamic Abrahamic restoration
Family: Muslim traditions

Islam presents Abraham as a primordial submitter to God and situates Muhammad's message as restoration and continuation of that monotheistic path.

This broad claim should not erase Sunni, Shia, Ibadi, Ahmadiyya and other differences.

Ismaili traditions
Claim type: Islamic Abrahamic restoration / living sacred authority
Family: Muslim traditions

Ismaili traditions participate in Islam's Abrahamic continuity while giving the Imam a distinctive role in authoritative interpretation.

Different Ismaili branches require their own historical treatment.

Jehovah's Witnesses
Claim type: Spiritual Israel / Christian inheritance
Family: Restorationist Christian movements

Jehovah's Witnesses interpret Abraham's promised seed through Christ and a distinct understanding of spiritual Israel.

Their claim is theological rather than an assertion that all members are literal descendants of ancient Israel.

Kabbalah
Claim type: Jewish covenant continuity / mystical interpretation
Family: Jewish traditions

Kabbalistic traditions interpret Torah, creation, divine life and repair from within Jewish covenantal identity rather than constituting a new ethnic Israel.

Different Kabbalistic schools and periods should be separated rather than turned into one timeless cosmology.

Kabbalah and Hasidic traditions
Claim type: Jewish covenant continuity
Family: Jewish traditions

Kabbalistic and Hasidic traditions operate within Jewish covenantal identity while offering distinctive mystical interpretations of divine life, Torah and the role of the righteous.

They do not constitute a separate ethnic Israel from Judaism; their continuity claim is nested inside Jewish peoplehood and covenant.

Existing Wiki context: ../maimonides-and-mystical-continuities.html

Karaite Judaism
Claim type: Jewish / Israelite covenant continuity
Family: Jewish traditions

Karaite Judaism claims Jewish and Israelite continuity while rejecting rabbinic Oral Law as binding authority.

Its disagreement is over the authoritative form of interpretation, not a rejection of Abrahamic-Israelite identity.

Existing Wiki context: ../encyclopedia-method.html

Latter-day Saints
Claim type: Restored Israel / Abrahamic covenant
Family: Restorationist Christian movements

Latter-day Saint teaching gives the Abrahamic covenant and the gathering of Israel a prominent role, with participation understood through descent, adoption and covenant.

This is a particularly explicit restorationist form of Israel language within Christianity.

Existing Wiki context: ../jewish-and-christian-boundaries.html

---

Mandaeans
Claim type: Abrahamic genealogy but not an Abrahamic inheritance claim
Family: Ancient and living traditions

Mandaeans share a scriptural and historical environment with Jewish, Christian and Baptist figures while preserving a strongly distinct religious identity.

Their tradition is not accurately summarized as 'we are the true heirs of Abraham'; Abraham and several other Abrahamic figures can be treated critically.

Existing Wiki context: ../mandaean-comparisons.html

Manichaeism
Claim type: Progressive / universal prophetic lineage
Family: Ancient and transregional movements

Manichaeism places Jesus within a wider prophetic history that also incorporates figures such as Buddha and Zoroaster, with Mani presenting a culminating revelation.

This is a successor-revelation claim, not an ethnic claim to be Israel.

Existing Wiki context: ../manichaeism-and-prophetic-lineages.html

Marcion and Marcionite traditions
Claim type: Rival Christian inheritance
Family: Ancient textual communities

Marcionite Christianity claimed the authentic gospel of Christ while sharply separating that revelation from the creator and law associated with Israel.

It is therefore a major boundary case: historically Christian and biblical, but not a simple claim to continue Israel's covenant.

Maronite Catholic Church
Claim type: Christian heirs of Abraham
Family: Eastern Christian traditions

The Maronite Catholic Church participates in the Christian and Catholic reading of Abrahamic salvation history while preserving an Eastern Syriac heritage.

Communion with Rome does not make the Maronite tradition identical to the Latin rite.

Messianic Judaism
Claim type: Jewish covenant continuity plus Christian messianic fulfillment
Family: Jewish-Christian movements

Messianic Jewish movements seek to retain Jewish or Israelite covenantal identity while confessing Jesus or Yeshua as Messiah.

The field contains disputes over Jewish status, Torah practice, church relations and institutional definition; one movement should not stand for all.

Existing Wiki context: ../jewish-and-christian-boundaries.html

Nation of Islam
Claim type: Islamic-derived distinct movement
Family: Religious movements in the Americas

The Nation of Islam emerged in an Islamic and biblical vocabulary but has distinctive teachings that separate it from mainstream Sunni and Shia Islam.

Its Abrahamic relationship is genealogical and interpretive rather than a simple continuation claim identical to mainstream Islam.

Old Calendarist and True Orthodox jurisdictions
Claim type: Christian heirs of Abraham
Family: Eastern Christian traditions

Old Calendarist and True Orthodox jurisdictions understand themselves within Orthodox Christian continuity and therefore within Christian Abrahamic salvation history.

Calendar disputes and jurisdictional claims do not, by themselves, define an independent Abrahamic people.

Oriental Orthodox Christianity
Claim type: Christian heirs of Abraham
Family: Christian traditions

Oriental Orthodox churches understand themselves within the Abraham-Israel-Christ story while preserving distinct ancient church traditions.

Coptic, Armenian, Syriac, Ethiopian, Eritrean and Malankara histories should not be collapsed into one ethnic or liturgical identity.

Orthodox Judaism
Claim type: Jewish covenant continuity
Family: Jewish traditions

Orthodox Judaism understands Jews as continuing the covenantal people of Israel and gives binding authority to Torah as interpreted through rabbinic tradition.

Orthodox Judaism itself contains substantial internal diversity and should not be represented by one rabbi or one philosophical school.

Existing Wiki context: ../maimonides-and-mystical-continuities.html

Protestant Christianity
Claim type: Christian heirs of Abraham
Family: Christian traditions

Protestant churches generally inherit the Christian claim that faith in Christ brings participation in Abraham's promise.

Views of Israel, covenant, law, baptism, election and church identity differ widely across Protestant traditions.

Existing Wiki context: ../jewish-and-christian-boundaries.html

Qumran texts and Essene comparisons
Claim type: Historical covenant / remnant claim
Family: Ancient textual communities

The sectarian Qumran texts portray a faithful covenant community within Israel rather than a later non-Israelite heir to Abraham.

This is a historical remnant claim reconstructed from particular texts, not a living denomination or proof that every Dead Sea Scroll was produced by one group.

Rabbinic Judaism: Orthodox, Conservative and Reform
Claim type: Jewish covenant continuity
Family: Jewish traditions

Rabbinic Judaism understands the Jewish people as continuing Israel's covenantal history through Abraham, Isaac and Jacob, Torah and communal obligation.

Orthodox, Conservative and Reform Judaism require separate pages because they interpret covenant, law and election differently.

Rastafari
Claim type: Israel / Zion reinterpretation
Family: Religious movements in the Americas

Rastafari reworks biblical Israel, Babylon, Zion, Ethiopia and exile within African and diasporic history.

Different houses and teachers vary, so the page should not treat one Israelite identification as the entire tradition.

Existing Wiki context: ../encyclopedia-method.html

Reform Judaism
Claim type: Jewish covenant continuity
Family: Jewish traditions

Reform Judaism retains Jewish covenantal identity while commonly interpreting chosenness in terms of mission, responsibility and ethical witness rather than superiority.

Specific platforms and congregations should be dated because Reform formulations have changed historically.

Existing Wiki context: ../maimonides-and-mystical-continuities.html

Sacred Name movements
Claim type: Restoration of original worship
Family: Restorationist Christian movements

Sacred Name movements emphasize recovering the divine name and often other features understood as original Hebrew or Israelite worship.

The movement family is diverse; recovering a name is not by itself proof of ethnic descent or sole covenantal legitimacy.

Saint Thomas Christians
Claim type: Christian heirs of Abraham
Family: Eastern Christian traditions

Saint Thomas Christian communities in India participate in Christian Abrahamic inheritance through several modern church communions.

The traditional apostolic origin and the later institutional histories need to be distinguished.

Samaritans
Claim type: Original Israel continuity claim
Family: Israelite traditions

Israelite Samaritans understand themselves as preserving ancient Israelite identity, Torah, priesthood and the sacred center at Mount Gerizim.

This is one of the clearest living claims to direct continuity with ancient Israel and competes with Jewish accounts on important points.

Existing Wiki context: ../encyclopedia-method.html

Sethian writings
Claim type: Comparative / Abrahamic textual genealogy
Family: Ancient textual communities

Sethian writings reuse biblical figures and Jewish-Christian materials but do not straightforwardly claim to be the ethnic or covenantal Israel of Abraham.

Their importance here is genealogical and interpretive, not a simple chosen-people claim.

Seventh-day Adventists
Claim type: Remnant / Christian inheritance
Family: Restorationist Christian movements

Seventh-day Adventism understands itself within Christian Abrahamic inheritance and gives special theological importance to an end-time remnant and mission.

A remnant claim is not identical to a claim of biological descent from ancient Israel.

Existing Wiki context: ../jewish-and-christian-boundaries.html

Shia Islam
Claim type: Islamic Abrahamic restoration plus sacred lineage
Family: Muslim traditions

Shia traditions share Islam's Abrahamic claim while emphasizing authority through the Prophet's family and the Imams in distinctive ways.

Twelver, Ismaili, Zaydi and other Shia traditions should not be collapsed into one doctrine of leadership.

Existing Wiki context: ../manichaeism-and-prophetic-lineages.html

Sufi traditions and orders
Claim type: Inner lineage within Islamic Abrahamic continuity
Family: Muslim traditions

Sufi orders and traditions generally operate within Muslim Abrahamic identity while emphasizing spiritual transmission, remembrance and disciplined paths of transformation.

Sufism is not normally a mutually exclusive third denomination beside Sunni and Shia.

Existing Wiki context: ../shared-repertoire.html

Sunni and Shia traditions
Claim type: Islamic Abrahamic restoration
Family: Muslim traditions

Sunni and Shia traditions both understand Islam as the continuation and restoration of Abraham's submission to the one God, while differing over authority and leadership after Muhammad.

The aggregate page points to separate Sunni and Shia pages because the traditions are not one institutional body.

Sunni Islam

Family: Muslim traditions

Sunni Islam participates in the Quranic claim to Abrahamic continuity through Muhammad and the Muslim community.

Sunni schools differ in law, theology, devotional practice and political history; this page is an orientation entry, not one doctrinal profile.

Valentinian traditions claim Christian revelation while reinterpreting creation, salvation, scripture and spiritual identity through an esoteric cosmology.
