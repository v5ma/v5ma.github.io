This September 21, 2026 atlas turns the comparative list into linked Wiki pages. It contains 60 dedicated group pages. Thirty-six entries are carried forward from the existing comparative-religion directory, and twenty-four split or add Abrahamic, restorationist and Israelite-identification traditions already discussed in the Theology Wiki.

The atlas does not assume that every included group claims to be the chosen people. Each page has a relationship type: direct Israelite continuity, Jewish covenant continuity, Christian inheritance, restoration/remnant Israel, Islamic Abrahamic restoration, progressive or universal revelation, Israel/Zion reinterpretation, Abrahamic genealogy without a simple chosen-people claim, or comparative material outside the Abrahamic inheritance claim.

[Open the atlas](index.html). Every name on the index links to a dedicated page under groups/. Pages reuse the source IDs and developed studies already present in the comparative-religion module when available. Entries without a dedicated source set are explicitly labeled as source-expansion prospects rather than receiving invented references.

The atlas is an orientation layer, not a truth ranking. It records what kind of continuity claim the Wiki is examining and what the claim does not establish. A group's own belief about ancestry, covenant or restoration is different from independent historical verification of genealogy. An ancient textual school, a modern denomination, an ethnic people and a research category are also different kinds of objects.

Canonical data are groups.json. build.cjs generates the index, 60 group pages, index.json, READING.md and build-report.json without network access. reader.js performs local filtering only, and every page remains readable without JavaScript. atlas.css is additive to the existing comparative-religion stylesheet.

The parent comparative-religion build receives a durable link to this atlas through an idempotent integration step. The generated parent index and directory pages are rebuilt from their real source generator so future comparative-religion rebuilds retain the atlas link. Existing study prose, source registries, original chats, forecasts and unrelated projects are not rewritten.

A later research pass can deepen the thinner pages one by one. Creating a dedicated page now does not mean the page is a complete denominational history.
