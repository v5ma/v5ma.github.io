John is important, but not the whole history

The Gospel of John is central to arguments over Jesus's relation to the Father, but it is not the only early source using exalted language. Philippians 2:5-11 combines an elevated portrayal with self-emptying, obedience and exaltation by God. The relation between divine status and obedient agency cannot therefore be described simply as a late switch introduced by John's Gospel.

The Gospel of John and the Johannine letters are distinct works. The term antichrist occurs in the letters, not as a universal definition supplied by the Gospel. First John 2:18-23 connects the category with denial of Jesus as Christ and with Father and Son. First John 4:1-3 and 2 John 7 make coming in flesh central. They do not say simply that any person doubting any reported miracle is thereby an Antichrist.

A contemporary theology can extend a textual category into a moral pattern, but it must identify that extension. It should not place a newly invented definition in the mouth of an ancient writer. The question whether a text's polemic should govern present relations between living communities is also distinct from the historical question of what the text meant.

Sources: Philippians 2, World English Bible / 1 John 2 and 4, World English Bible / 2 John, World English Bible.

An ethical teacher and a divine object of worship are different propositions

A person can recognize a teacher's Jewish setting, appreciate a saying or acknowledge historical significance without accepting incarnation, a resurrection claim or Christian worship. The idea of Jesus as a Jewish teacher or non-deified messianic claimant may remove one doctrinal obstacle for an interlocutor, but it does not automatically satisfy that interlocutor's criteria for Messiah or fidelity to Torah.

Maimonides' criteria provide an example of why a disagreement can remain after the divinity question is set aside. The issue includes restoration and the outcome of the claimant's mission. The inquiry should ask which objection is actually being answered rather than assume that all disagreement depends on one metaphor or title.

Sources: Maimonides, Laws of Kings and Wars 11, translated by Eliyahu Touger / To Do the Will of Our Father in Heaven, Orthodox rabbinic statement (2015).

What named Jewish writers establish

Buber's appreciation of Jesus, as discussed by Mendes-Flohr, is a specific intellectual relationship. Boyarin's publisher describes a thesis about Jewish contexts for early Christological ideas. Knohl's publisher describes a proposed earlier messianic figure and relevant textual reconstruction. None of those records is a survey showing that contemporary Hasidic communities are collectively adopting Christian claims.

A claim of an expanding movement requires named teachers, dated publications, institutions, sustained practices and evidence of change over time. A scholar of Kabbalah is not automatically speaking as a representative of all Kabbalists; an academic interpretation is not automatically an authorized communal doctrine. This edition has not established the broad growth claim supplied in the discussion.

The Orthodox declaration of 2015 offers a narrower, verifiable example of cooperation. It preserves differences while encouraging a shared practical relationship. The encyclopedia can document that without assigning the signatories a belief they did not declare.

Sources: Paul Mendes-Flohr on Martin Buber, ABC Philosophers Zone interview / Daniel Boyarin, The Jewish Gospels, publisher description / Israel Knohl, The Messiah Before Jesus, publisher description / To Do the Will of Our Father in Heaven, Orthodox rabbinic statement (2015).

Islamic recognition likewise preserves a boundary

Quran 4:157-171 rejects the claim that Jesus's opponents killed or crucified him and warns against saying Three. Its honor for Jesus does not amount to acceptance of Nicene incarnation. Conversely, the later explanations of the crucifixion passage are not all simply identical to the passage's few sentences.

The useful comparison keeps the object of affirmation explicit: prophetic authority, messianic title, exemplary teaching, divine sonship, incarnation and saving death are different claims. Communities may overlap on some while disagreeing on others. Their relationship need not be described either as total identity or as total absence of shared ground.

Sources: Quran 4:157-171, translated text.

Eastern Christianity is not a survival category for Gnosticism

The 1973 joint declaration of Pope Paul VI and Pope Shenouda III affirms the Triune God and the real divinity and humanity of Christ. It is direct evidence against treating Coptic Christianity as a disguised rejection of incarnation or a simple survival of the Nag Hammadi tractates. The declaration did not merge the churches or erase every difference.

Geography, communion, liturgical rite and doctrine are independent dimensions. Maronite, Melkite and Chaldean Catholic traditions belong to Eastern Catholic churches; Coptic Orthodox and the Assyrian Church of the East have different institutional relationships. A mystical liturgy, an ancient language or the practice of deification does not by itself make a church Gnostic in a demiurgical or anti-incarnational sense. The directory below uses those distinctions rather than an East-versus-West scale of esotericism.

Sources: Common Declaration of Pope Paul VI and Pope Shenouda III, May 10, 1973 / CNEWA, The Catholic Eastern Churches / CNEWA, The Assyrian Church of the East.
