This is the Theology Wiki's cross-platform corpus review, added on September 17, 2026. [Open the review desk](index.html). It connects a broad searchable metadata register to a smaller contextual statement log, without mistaking automated retrieval for individual reading.

The completed one-shot collection screened 92,883 unique archive records: 56,570 Twitter records dated May 4, 2009 through January 8, 2021, and 36,313 Truth Social records dated February 14, 2022 through September 17, 2026. The Twitter input contained 56,571 rows; one repeated ID had conflicting text and was retained as a review issue, not counted twice. The [machine-generated coverage receipt](results/summary.json) records exact input hashes, row counts, dates and limitations. [The collection run](https://github.com/v5ma/v5ma.github.io/actions/runs/35269662454) records the execution. The datasets are independent archives rather than certified complete native exports.

The source documents are the [historical Twitter mirror](https://huggingface.co/datasets/fschlatt/trump-tweets/blob/main/README.md) and the [Truth Social collection documentation](https://github.com/stiles/trump-truth-social-archive/blob/main/README.md). The latter identifies the continuing CNN feed separately from the older GitHub scraper. A public URL does not establish that a feed includes every deleted post, repost, edit, attachment or later X statement.

All retrieved valid records were text-screened, including nonmatches. There were 9,737 distinct keyword-hit records across the two archives; topic totals overlap and must not be added together as independent events. There were 7,269 records with no extracted text. Those empty-text records are not evidence of silence: an image, clip or linked document may carry the message. Current X activity after January 2021 and a comprehensive public-speech corpus are not covered by this bulk pass. These are concrete acquisition gaps, not completed reviews.

The contextual log adds [28 new records](reviewed.json) to the [earlier 22-record Truth Social selection](../truth-social/posts.json). Its combined reader therefore displays 50 statement records. Some records are separate passages from the same speech, and some are linked statements in one episode. Fifty records is not fifty independent demonstrations or fifty distinct speeches. The new set contains Twitter text, Truth Social text, archived video transcripts, public speeches and a televised interview clip. The date and full-text scope of each witness are retained.

The organizing questions follow Micah Blumberg's existing [First Beast article](../../san-reader.html?page=trump-first-beast-of-revelation) and [Antichrist-as-conduct article](../../san-reader.html?page=antichrist-as-a-pattern-of-conduct). The relevant subjects are the speaker's claimed authority, requests for allegiance, descriptions of opponents, promised protection or redress, threatened punishment, religious self-presentation or third-party praise, and conditions attached to material access. The comparison concerns observable language and relationships. It does not infer an unseen mental state from punctuation, posting frequency or an isolated adjective.

The passage subjects remain distinct. Revelation 13:3-8 concerns the first beast and public allegiance. Revelation 13:11-17 introduces the second beast's promotion, image and enforcement. References to a narrator, an image or buying and selling therefore do not silently become first-beast actions. Revelation 6:3-6 supplies war and subsistence subjects; Revelation 16:13-16 supplies the gathering-for-conflict subject; Revelation 18:9-19 supplies rulers, merchants and maritime commerce. [Read Revelation 6](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Revelation+6&version=KJV), [13](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Revelation+13&version=KJV), [16](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Revelation+16&version=KJV) and [18](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Revelation+18&version=KJV).

Research prioritization is implemented as work queues: original-source recovery, attribution and media review, paired-context review, and implementation follow-up. Each identifies the next piece of evidence needed. Neither the queue nor the number of keyword matches is a scale of Antichrist identity, a psychological diagnosis or a political rating. Default sorting is by date; readers can select a topic, year, source platform or outstanding research task.

Every contextual record has a short quotation, a factual description, context, a source and a question. The exact passage is not promoted into a complete-post backup. The phrase about royal and divine status in the August 2019 Twitter thread remains attributed to Wayne Allyn Root as quoted by Trump. The January 2024 video words remain the narrator's. Declared limits, peace appeals and later revisions stay attached to the related demands or threats. An accusation remains attributed; an announcement remains distinct from an implemented policy or observed consequence.

The [cross-check of the earlier selection](results/legacy-crosscheck.json) found the excerpt in the feed for 19 records. One excerpt requires inspection, and two earlier records lack original platform IDs. A failed substring comparison can arise from punctuation, encoding, edits or source differences; it is not itself proof of fabrication or deletion. The earlier files are preserved, not silently repaired from an unmatched automated result.

The full metadata indexes are deliberately loaded only on request: approximately 28 MB for Twitter and 18 MB for Truth Social before transport compression. They contain identifiers, dates, links, attribution flags, normalized-text hashes and matched search terms, not bulk republications of post text. The reader paginates results. Metadata search is not full-text post search; read the linked source before creating a contextual entry. No data are sent to an AI service, no personal data or browser storage are collected, and no recurring monitoring task is created.

The canonical files are reviewed.json, collect.py, this README and the reader. The results directory is an immutable snapshot of the one-shot run; later snapshots should preserve their own collection dates and input hashes rather than overwrite this receipt. The original event register, author forecasts, report catalogue and 22-post selection remain unchanged. The full scarcity report is still a separate missing-export task; this corpus review does not claim to recover it.
