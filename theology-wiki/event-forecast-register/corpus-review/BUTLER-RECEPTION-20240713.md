This note records the public reception of the July 13, 2024 Butler assassination attempt and the subsequent religious language about survival. It was first logged on September 17, 2026. The event date is not the date of this interpretation update. This is a documentary companion to the existing Theology Wiki narrative, not a new medical account, political rating, or finding of prophetic identity.

Micah Blumberg supplied the following research emphasis in the current conversation: "So that's the point to focus on, not the failed assassination details themselves, but the political after effect, the marveling, and the comparisons to Trump as God's warrior, and all the religious stuff that happened in the aftermath of that assassination attempt." This quotation records the author's direction. The dated observations below are separately sourced; the phrase God's warrior is not attributed to a particular historical speaker without a matching source.

The textual question concerns the sequence in Revelation 13 rather than an isolated injury. Verse 3 describes one of the beast's heads as appearing fatally wounded, its healing, and the world's astonishment and following. Verse 4 adds worship and language about the beast's perceived unassailability. Verse 12 returns to the healed wound in the second beast's promotion of the first. Verse 14 again refers to the wounded beast living and to the making of its image. Injury, recovery, public reaction, promotion and representation can therefore be recorded as separate textual subjects without detaching them from their connected passage.

The King James wording includes appearance language in the wound description, and the ESV also preserves that qualification. The qualification should be retained rather than replacing it with an unqualified assertion that the verse explicitly narrates death followed by resurrection. It likewise should not be treated as a complete resolution of how the vision's wound is to be interpreted. The present note isolates the reception question: what did people say and do after survival became apparent, and how did later speakers represent that survival?

The dated record begins on July 14, 2024. AFP reported Trump's Truth Social message attributing his survival to God. The same post expressed concern for the other victims and called for unity. This is an immediate statement of the subject's religious explanation, not evidence of what every observer believed.

On July 15, 2024, the Associated Press reported cheering delegates when Trump appeared at the Republican National Convention with a bandaged ear. This observation concerns the convention audience and its response to his appearance. It is separate from later retrospective descriptions of the Butler crowd.

Also on July 15, Senator Tim Scott described the survival using miracle language and an image of an American lion standing again. The South Carolina Daily Gazette's July 16 account records the speech and crowd responses. Scott is the speaker of that interpretation; the account does not turn it into Trump's own original wording.

On July 18, Franklin Graham's convention remarks and prayer attributed Trump's survival to God. His association's July 19 report preserves those remarks, his acknowledgment that he could not explain why another attendee died, and prayers for the other victims. The prayer's addressee was God. A study of religious language should preserve that distinction instead of relabeling every prayer for a political figure as worship of that figure.

On July 18, Trump's acceptance speech itself narrated an apparent-death-to-reassurance sequence. He said spectators thought he had died, then described raising his fist and their reaction when they realized he was alive. He attributed his presence to divine grace. The transcript separately records the convention audience answering him. His account of the Butler audience is a retrospective claim about other people's perceptions, not a contemporaneous survey of those spectators.

ITV's July 18 reporting documented delegates wearing ear bandages and explaining them as solidarity, respect and support. This supplies a concrete instance of supporters adopting a symbol associated with the injury. The report is dated July 18; it does not establish that every individual photographed began the practice that day.

On July 26, at the Believers Summit in West Palm Beach, Trump said he had recovered and removed the last bandage, while thanking the audience for prayers and attributing his presence to prayer and divine grace. The Factbase transcript places this passage at approximately 00:01:04-00:02:00. Its page title and URL say July 27; contemporaneous July 26 reporting provides the event date used here. The reason for the archive's date difference is not established. Fox News reported applause after his gesture toward the ear.

On January 20, 2025, the inaugural address connected survival to a political mission: "I was saved by God to make America great again." The official transcript records applause following the statement. This provides a later use of the survival narrative in an official ceremony, not merely an immediate reaction in July.

At the same inauguration, Franklin Graham's invocation thanked God for saving Trump's life and referred to God having raised him with strength and power. The association's account supplies the prayer and its setting. These are Graham's religious claims and ceremonial language, not independently established supernatural events.

These records separate several observable things: a speaker describing survival as extraordinary, a crowd expressing approval, supporters adopting a visual symbol, religious figures attributing protection to God, and a later statement tying survival to an intended political mission. A change in public opinion or loyalty across a population is a further question that needs evidence beyond selected speeches and applause. No such causal estimate or population-wide finding is made here.

For the proposed verse-by-verse project, the unit of documentation should include a verse's distinct clauses and agents. Revelation 13:3 can have separate injury, healing and reception entries, all linked to the same episode. Each entry should preserve its textual wording, the relevant dated source, the actual speaker or publisher, the observed response, the source's scope, the author's proposed connection, and unresolved questions. These are cross-references, not an exclusive allocation in which every verse must be assigned to a political event.

A single event can inform several textual questions, and several dated records can document the development of one public narrative. Repetitions and later commemorations should link to the original episode rather than become independent counts of fulfillment. A verse or clause with no documented connection can remain unassigned. The framework does not require numerical scores or rankings.

The prior corpus already contains a January 2024 religiously framed video, the July 14 survival statement, and the January 2025 mission statement. The January material supplies a pre-event comparison: religious imagery present before Butler should not automatically be described as originating in its aftermath. The present companion adds context and sources without changing the earlier record IDs, forecasts, metadata snapshot or review counts. It is not yet a generated route in the principal reader and does not claim a complete survey of worldwide reactions or every social-media reuse.
