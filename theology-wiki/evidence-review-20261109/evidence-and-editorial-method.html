What changes in this edition

The main argument should stand on identifiable texts, artifacts and scholarship. A conversational reply is not evidence that a historical claim is true. This edition therefore supplies revised studies before readers enter selected archived discussions, while retaining the original record for attribution and correction.

The transition is editorial, not cosmetic. Removing a speaker label does not verify a claim. Adding words such as “possibly” does not repair an invalid inference. New prose must state what the source supports, where an interpretation begins and what evidence could change the conclusion. No claim of exclusively human authorship is made for these editorial studies.

Four distinct evidence labels

Primary evidence: an identifiable artifact or textual passage. Supply a shelf mark or edition and a passage locator. A text's statement is evidence of what that witness says; it is not automatically a reliable account of the event it narrates.

Scholarly interpretation: an argument attributed to a named scholar and linked to the relevant publication. Identify disagreements and the actual scope of access. A publisher's description or abstract must not be represented as a full-text assessment.

Working hypothesis: a proposed explanation that goes beyond the surviving evidence. State the missing connection. A resemblance between stories may justify comparison without establishing direct dependence or identical historical actors.

Unverified archival claim: a statement preserved in an earlier conversation that this edition does not independently establish. A file hash verifies unchanged bytes, not historical accuracy, speaker expertise or the origin of quoted material inside a turn.

How a claim is checked

  1. Identify the precise proposition: a manuscript date, a linguistic feature, an event, a literary relationship or a theological judgment.
  2. Link the proposition to a source that actually supports it. Use the specific passage, section or artifact identifier instead of a search-result URL.
  3. Separate observation from inference. Record significant alternatives and differences, not only attractive similarities.
  4. Describe uncertainty specifically: damaged letters, disputed dating, sample size, text transmission, author identity or access limited to an abstract.
  5. Give a fair counterargument and identify what further evidence would discriminate between explanations. Do not manufacture unanimity or treat all views as equally supported.
  6. Retain a correction trail. Do not quietly change the historic words of the author or a recorded conversational partner.

Reading both sides without false balance

The paired “Evidence” and “Counterarguments and limits” sections are an invitation to inspect the reasoning. They are not a vote between two equally probable options. Where a position is a disputed proposal, it is described as such; where specific evidence is stronger, the article explains why.

The linguistic and epigraphic matrix demonstrates why earlier Israelite identity, written blessings, a book's compilation and later translation must be examined separately. The Dead Sea Scrolls study separates radiocarbon evidence from an AI handwriting prediction and both from a historical identification. The Revelation study pairs intertextual comparison with the ethical difficulty of violent imagery.

What is preserved

Original conversations, speaker labels, turn links and source integrity checks remain available. New editorial summaries are linked alongside them; they do not silently replace the archived words. A historical transcript is useful for reconstructing how a question developed, not as a substitute for the sources required to answer it.

This expanded evidence edition covers seven guides and studies, not a completed claim-by-claim review of all 354 archived conversations. Other developed articles retain their own stated evidence and review status. The general archive warning applies to every conversation, while the new claim-specific companion links cover Revelation, the Dead Sea Scrolls, the Gmirkin discussion, and selected Paul/James comparisons.

Review status and corrections

Status: source-checked editorial working edition, November 9, 2026. Not independently peer-reviewed. Technical tests check navigation, rendering and preservation; they do not validate historical conclusions.

A useful correction identifies the page, disputed sentence, proposed revision and a source with a precise locator. Readers can inspect the repository and its existing discussion channels and the release log. Every revision should preserve both the original record and the reason for the correction.
