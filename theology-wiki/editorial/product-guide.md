The Theology project is a connected research collection with several products, not a book with promotional material attached. The book offers a deliberate narrative. The wiki preserves the full branching investigations. A listening edition makes those investigations available away from a screen. Short audio and video episodes introduce specific problems. Interactive applications let a reader change stated assumptions. The separate AR and VR museum can make a route through the evidence spatial without changing what the research argues.

## Begin with the full reconstruction

The [[listening-room|listening room]] contains every developed article, not only a synopsis. A listener can choose an article or one of the proposed chapter routes, move by paragraph or section, and return to the original written version. Source and visual notes are identified separately. The saved position refers to a particular text hash; a changed article must not silently resume in an unrelated paragraph.

Browser voices depend on the listener's device. Local voices are preferred. Remote voices require an explicit choice and can send the spoken text to the device's voice provider. The site does not call a paid speech API or collect microphone recordings. Browser speech is not itself an MP3 export. Where a prerecorded study is available, its actual audio file, transcript and source version are separately listed.

## Episodes should create curiosity without changing the claim

The [[production-studio|audio and video studio]] begins with three editorial scripts: competing chronologies, the earlier Teacher and historical carriers, and what an objection should preserve. Each script gives the positive proposal room before examining the relevant comparison. An introductory episode is not a complete version of a long argument, and it is not a newly recovered quotation by Micah.

The earlier-Teacher episode retains the proposed earlier founder, successor community and relocated biography. The chronology episode retains political reversal and disaster as an event sequence rather than treating the work as a preferred date. The challenge episode restores the already stated distinction between famine and a later firstborn narrative. Those are production requirements, not decorative disclaimers. Removing them would alter the project.

The short version must always lead to the longer one. A screen caption, spoken scene or museum label connects to stable source records and complete articles. Sources belong in the production package as well as on the video page. A viewer should not have to reconstruct the bibliography from a flashing credit frame.

## Keep the research configuration across formats

The source atlas links works, witnesses, translations and proposals with different relationship types. Those distinctions carry into audio, video and exhibits. A dramatic image cannot transform a conjectured route into an excavated fact. A skeptical caption cannot replace institutional continuity with thematic similarity. The full claim and the evidence bearing on each step remain reachable together.

An episode script is a distinct editorial work based on particular public article versions. Its production record pins those article hashes. A recording pins its narration text and lists its voice engine. A museum consumer pins the atlas it reads. When the research changes, the affected products can be identified rather than left circulating an unmarked older version.

## A coherent release path

The shared [[research-roadmap|research plan]] still owns the sources, historical comparisons, chapter development and author-review requirements. The product register below adds delivery work: playback, pronunciation review, narration choice, captions, media production, device testing, XR consumption and distribution. A functioning player is not the same milestone as an approved audiobook. A video on this website is not automatically a YouTube publication or podcast launch.

No narrator identity is assumed. The current recordings, when present, are explicitly synthetic production drafts. Micah can approve a voice and presentation style before the final book and series adopt them. The current implementation does not clone his voice or publish private SAN manuscript content.

The next useful growth is not to invent a different story for every channel. It is to improve the common research, develop episodes that preserve its most interesting mechanisms, and make the complete evidence easier to reach from whatever format brings a person into the inquiry.
