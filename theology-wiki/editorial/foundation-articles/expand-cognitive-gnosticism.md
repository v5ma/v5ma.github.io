## Interpretive reconstruction across several kinds of evidence

The Teacher of Righteousness vs Gnostic Jesus conversation makes the scope substantially broader than an analogy between a religious word and a brain process. In author turn 9, Micah proposes reconstructing the semantic nuances of Jesus's messages through a wide comparison of religious, philosophical and scientific texts. Turn 11 explicitly connects that program to Neo Mind Cycle, biological and machine interpretability, Self Aware Networks and religious education for AI. These are parts of the proposed architecture, not unrelated biographical details.

The tomography metaphor is useful when its limits are explicit. Different sources supply different views of a proposed structure. One text may clarify a teaching's vocabulary, another its social conflict, another a possible inward interpretation. Combining them could reveal a relation that no isolated source makes obvious. But incompatible views cannot simply be averaged into a preferred answer. Their dates, genres, speakers and disagreements remain information about what the reconstruction can support.

The [[tor-thomas-and-gnostic-transmission|transmission investigation]] therefore asks more than whether religious communities share uplifting themes. It asks which interpretive functions travel, what changes around them and how an inherited message can acquire a new role. A text can change a modern reader without thereby proving the reader's account of the text's origin. Preserving both questions is essential to the proposal's breadth.

## Understanding is different from repeating an answer

Consider a reader who can repeat a saying about mercy but responds to disagreement by humiliation. In the proposed model, the missing step is not simply another copy of the saying. The teaching must become available within the situation in which an alternative response is needed. The reader notices what the impulse would do, compares it with the meaning they intend to enact, and changes the response. Later reflection may reveal that the interpretation itself was incomplete.

The point of the example is not to reduce gnosis to politeness. It is to show the distinction between possessing a sentence and understanding it in a way that reorganizes judgment. A person may need to resist rather than accommodate, speak forcefully rather than withdraw, or admit that an apparently compassionate act served their reputation more than another person's need. A transformed orientation changes how a problem is perceived, not only which scripted answer is chosen.

[[god-and-our-models-of-god|God and our models of God]] prevents a further compression: the account of an inward mechanism is not the claim that its divine referent is unreal. It also prevents the converse error, in which naming the referent divine exempts the interpretation from criticism. Cognitive, historical and metaphysical questions belong together without being treated as one already completed proof.

## An inquiry that can test its own readings

The source conversation repeatedly asks for broader coverage rather than selective examples. The new [[book-contents|chapter map]] and [[research-board|research board]] turn that request into a visible development task. A worked case should retain the relevant text, the interpretation being proposed, the competing interpretation it addresses, and the consequence of choosing one over the other. A diagram can show those relations, but the source must remain accessible behind it.

This is also where the relation to interpretability becomes intellectually productive. A fluent explanation of a decision is not automatically an account of the process that produced it. Studying how a person or machine describes its interpretation and studying the causal process of its decisions are different tasks. The proposal aims to bring them closer; it should not claim they have become identical because both use the word understanding.
