## The proposal: an earlier founder in a later biography

The Earlier-Teacher and Displaced-Biography Hypothesis asks whether the Teacher of Righteousness and the person remembered as Jesus were, at their historical origin, the same founder. It proposes that a continuing movement transmitted his teaching and memory across generations, while later Gospel writers placed that remembered life within a first-century CE setting. The proposed displacement concerns the biography, not the physical scrolls.

I accept the relevant pre-Christian manuscript evidence as a constraint on this version of the inquiry. I am not moving second-century-BCE witnesses forward to make them contemporary accounts of Pilate's Judea. Nor does accepting an early manuscript date establish the exact lifetime of its Teacher. The [[jesus-teacher-of-righteousness-hypothesis|full identity investigation]] retains the stronger same-founder claim, succession mechanism, earlier chronology variants, and the Pauline and external-witness constraints it must explain.

### Five dates that must remain distinguishable

| Layer | What the date concerns | What it cannot establish alone |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Material manuscript | The production of a particular surviving copy | The birth or death of its remembered teacher |
| Composition and revision | When a work or identifiable textual layer took form | That every passage had one author |
| Remembered founder | A reconstructed person's activity and death | Identity with a person in another tradition |
| Proposed identification | The claim that two portraits preserve one historical founder | Its own chronology without independent evidence |
| Gospel setting | The time and people within a narrated biography | Whether that setting is inherited history or a later relocation |

“Pre-Christian” does not mean that all Dead Sea Scrolls date to the second century BCE. The [Library of Congress's 1993 exhibition](https://www.loc.gov/exhibits/scrolls/intr.html) describes a broad third-century-BCE-to-68-CE horizon and labels the displayed 11Q Psalms copy ca. 30–50 CE. Its [Enoch exhibit](https://www.loc.gov/exhibits/scrolls/scr3.html) labels 4Q201 ca. 200–150 BCE. These are dated curatorial examples, not a newly updated inventory or a calendar for the Teacher's life.

### How the proposed relocation would work

The hypothesis joins transmission and reinterpretation: sayings, remembered authority, communal practices, and a founder's story could survive in texts and oral teaching. A later narrator could adapt that inheritance into Greek expression and a different political biography. This is a proposed mechanism, not an identified chain of manuscripts proving that such a transfer occurred. It also does not assign every sectarian scroll, Johannine discourse, or Thomas saying personally to the Teacher.

Daniel 9 supplies a possible fulfillment framework to investigate. The [[daniel-jubilee-and-the-teachers-time|Daniel study]] explains the seventy weeks, possible reanchoring, and why the claimed roughly 150-year shift is not a demonstrated exact offset. No consulted source establishes that particular Gospel scribes moved a decree to manufacture a Pilate-era date. The existing 147/88-BCE proposal and the separate 390 + 20 + inferred 40 + 40 reconstruction remain different models.

### The attachment comparisons become passage-level questions

The [[sacred-inheritance-and-rival-continuations|inheritance study]] now works through covenant in Damascus, Isaiah's wilderness way, 4Q521's restoration motifs, Johannine light and water, and the calendar and Sabbath comparisons. Each can support a question about continuity without settling personal identity. The [[tor-thomas-and-gnostic-transmission|Teacher–Thomas study]] explains why the survival of teaching and the composition of a narrative biography need not share one date.

Four explanations remain visible: common Jewish scriptural resources; circulation or literary influence between distinct groups; institutional continuity through successor communities; and the stronger displacement of one founder's biography. These are different hypotheses. Evidence for one should not be presented as proof of all four. Differences in law or literary form deserve examination as possible changes or rival practices, rather than being erased or made into an automatic disproof of identity.
