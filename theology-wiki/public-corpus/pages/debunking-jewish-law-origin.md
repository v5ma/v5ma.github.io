The claim that “Jewish law practices are not found until the 2nd century BC in Palestine” is **incorrect and misleading**. Here's a concise debunking:

### 1. **Jewish Law (Torah) Predates the 2nd Century BCE**
- The **Torah**, especially the **Pentateuch** (Genesis–Deuteronomy), contains the core of Jewish law and is traditionally attributed to **Moses (13th–15th century BCE)**. While scholarly consensus sees it as compiled from earlier sources, most scholars date major components of the Torah to between the **10th and 6th centuries BCE**.
- **Deuteronomic reform** under **King Josiah (ca. 640–609 BCE)**, described in 2 Kings 22–23, shows clearly that written legal texts (e.g., Deuteronomy) were already in use and influencing state-level religious reform.

### 2. **Archaeological Evidence**
- **Elephantine Papyri** from Egypt (5th century BCE) contain references to Jewish practices and legal traditions, including **Sabbath observance**, **Passover**, and **marriage law**, consistent with Torah law.
- The **Dead Sea Scrolls** (2nd century BCE to 1st century CE) contain extensive legal discussions (Halakha), but also older copies of biblical books, showing that law was established and interpreted even earlier.

### 3. **Historical Continuity**
- Jewish exiles in **Babylon (6th century BCE)** were already practicing elements of Torah law, as seen in **Ezra-Nehemiah**, where the returnees reestablish Torah-based law in Jerusalem around **450 BCE**.

### 4. **Greek Sources**
- Greek writers such as **Hecataeus of Abdera** (late 4th century BCE) refer to Jewish customs and laws.
- **Josephus** (1st century CE) also references the antiquity of Jewish laws, citing sources that suggest a well-established legal tradition prior to Hellenistic influence.

### Conclusion:
Jewish law **predates the 2nd century BCE by centuries**. What emerged in the 2nd century BCE is **sectarian interpretation** and **codification of debates** around the law (e.g., Pharisees, Sadducees, Essenes), **not the origin** of the law itself.
