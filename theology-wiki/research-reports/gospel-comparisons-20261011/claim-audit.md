# Gospel comparison claim audit

Review date: 11 October 2026

Scope: claims considered in developing **The Living Jesus in Thomas, John and the Synoptic Gospels**. This audit records corrections and the strength of constructive reconciliations. It does not authenticate an entire gospel or settle canonical authority. Primary passages are linked in the article's numbered source notes.

## Textual and historical corrections

| Claim | Finding | Revised treatment |
|---|---|---|
| Jesus deliberately synchronized every action and location with Moses and Jonah | Overstates what literary parallels establish | Distinguish a narrated saying, an evangelist's presentation and independently established historical intention |
| Moses and Jonah were the only major patterns | Incorrect | Include David, Solomon, Elijah and Elisha; distinguish Pauline Adam and inferred Isaac typology |
| All Synoptics give the same Jonah timetable | Incorrect | Matthew 12:40 gives the fish-duration comparison; Luke 11 emphasizes sign and response; Mark 8 refuses a demanded sign |
| The Transfiguration took place on Hermon | Not stated in the narratives | Leave the mountain unnamed; describe Hermon only as a proposal |
| Thomas entirely erases Hebrew history | Incorrect | James 12, Adam/John 46, vineyard/stone 65–66 and Caesar 100 are concrete counterexamples |
| Thomas is certainly a second-century Gnostic work with no early material | Scholarly questions collapsed into one assertion | Distinguish manuscript date, composition, layered development and individual sayings; present competing research positions |
| Absence of a passion narrative means Thomas denies death or resurrection | Invalid inference | Genre observation alone does not establish denial |
| Thomas' kingdom is exclusively inward | Incomplete | Thomas 3 includes inward and outward;113 places the kingdom across the earth |
| Thomas 52 explicitly rejects the whole Hebrew Bible or calls its God evil | Not explicitly stated | Present the sharper anti-prophetic reading and a coherent present-teacher reading without pretending either is explicitly explained |
| The twenty-four prophets certainly mean twenty-four books | Interpretive proposal | Compare the book-count tradition without treating the identification as Thomas' own definition |
| All translations say the prophets spoke “about” Jesus | Inaccurate | Other translations use “in” or “through”; do not depend on a single English preposition |
| The canonical Jesus could never describe ancient figures as dead | Incorrect | John 6:49 acknowledges dead ancestors; John 11 distinguishes Lazarus' death from life despite dying |
| Thomas 52 and Matthew 22 refer to exactly the same persons | Incorrect | Thomas names prophets collectively; the resurrection argument names Abraham, Isaac and Jacob |
| Jesus was challenged by Pharisees about dead prophets in front of Romans | Conflation | Separate tax and Sadducean resurrection episodes; Luke states an intended prosecution, not Roman attendance |
| Jesus' proof depends entirely on English “I am” tense | Too narrow | Include continuing divine relationship, resurrection context and Hebrew nominal wording |
| John 5 teaches that scripture contains no life and has no value | Misleading | Read5:39–47 together: scripture and Moses testify to Jesus, and the failure is not coming to him |
| The greatest-commandment exchange is always a hostile final trap | Incorrect | Matthew depicts testing then a further David question; Mark portrays a sympathetic scribe; Luke locates the related exchange elsewhere |
| A fixed 613-commandment count is a documented detail of this encounter | Unsupported anachronistic certainty | The standard explicit rabbinic attribution is to the later Rabbi Simlai; do not invent the trap's exact strategy |
| Sadducees were “nihilists” who accepted only the Pentateuch | Misleading/overconfident | Their denial of resurrection is stated; Josephus' written-law/tradition contrast is not an explicit five-book canon list |
| Q was certainly copied by Matthew and Luke and Thomas proves it | Incorrect certainty | Q is a source hypothesis; a sayings collection proves the genre existed, not that particular dependency model |
| Shorter sayings are automatically earlier and more authentic | Invalid rule | Abbreviation is possible; assess wording and transmission case by case |
| More attestations always mean independent attestations | Invalid inference | Assess literary dependence before using multiple attestation |
| Thomas 101 explicitly says love parents because each carries a divine spark | Not in the saying | A modern theological application must be identified as such; include the damaged true-mother clause |
| Thomas 14 explicitly condemns only hypocritical observance | Not stated | This is a harmonizing qualification; compare 104 and Matthew 6 but preserve the remaining tension |
| Mark 9:29 certainly reads “prayer and fasting” | Textual variation | Flag fasting as a manuscript variant rather than silently making it the undisputed reading |
| Matthew 28 narrates Jesus leaving earth | Incorrect | It promises continuing presence; the ascension narrative belongs elsewhere |
| Thomas 77 unambiguously teaches pantheism | Interpretive overstatement | Distinguish cosmic/light imagery from a settled metaphysical classification |
| The wood-and-stone material always occupied its current place in 77 | Incorrect | GreekP.Oxy. 1 groups it with material corresponding to 30; Coptic arrangement differs |
| Desert Fathers demonstrably used 77 as a basket-making mindfulness manual | No supporting evidence established | Cite Antony/Cassian for actual work-prayer-scripture practice; mark the Thomas connection as unproven |
| Monastic possession proves the exact reason Nag Hammadi texts were buried | Invalid inference | Ownership/readership and burial circumstances remain separate research questions |
| Marcion is demonstrably downstream of Thomas | Not established | A theological resemblance cannot substitute for evidence of dependence |
| Thomas was excluded specifically because later groups weaponized 52 | Not established | Do not offer an invented reception history |

## Reconciliation assessment

| Comparison | Positive lens | Strength and remaining question |
|---|---|---|
| Thomas 52 and Synoptic God-of-living argument | Biological death/absence versus life before God | Strong logical compatibility; Thomas does not thereby explicitly affirm every canonical resurrection teaching |
| Thomas 52 and John 5 | Valid testimony versus failure to encounter its living referent | Strong conceptual analogy; John affirms Moses more explicitly |
| Thomas 51/113 and present/future kingdom | Present participation and future fulfillment | Plausible with canonical precedent; Thomas' complete eschatology is not thereby reconstructed |
| Thomas 101 and familial renunciation | Priority of discipleship and distinct kinds of belonging | Plausible; damaged text and true-Mother language require care |
| Thomas 14/104 and ritual practice | Situation and purpose can change the value of a practice | More difficult: hypocrisy qualification is not explicit in 14 |
| Thomas 22 and children | Undivided receptivity | Shared motif; Thomas' inner/outer and gender symbolism goes further |
| Thomas 77 and John/Matthew presence | Light and encounter extending beyond one place | Constructive theological comparison; not proof of identical metaphysics or direct dependence |
| Thomas 16 and peace teachings | Intended practice versus conflict provoked by commitment | Coherent contextual distinction; does not authorize violence |

## Evidence boundaries

The article treats compatibility as an interpretation supported by juxtaposed primary passages. Historical authenticity is a separate, saying-specific inquiry. Scholarly positions are attributed to the scholars' own public explanations. Reference pages containing ancient translations alongside anonymous comments are used for identified translations and named scholarship, not anonymous assertions.

Original conversations and private attachments are not reproduced in this public review. The prose is a new synthesis; it is not represented as recovered verbatim speech by Jesus or by the wiki's author. No prior public source transcript, unrelated article or earlier research chronology is rewritten by this new comparison.

## Additional sources for audit-only claims

- Josephus, [Antiquities 13.297](https://cojs.org/josephus-_antiquities_xiii-_297-_the_pharisees_and_sadducees_on_the_traditions_of_the_fathers/): written observances contrasted with ancestral traditions. This passage does not supply a five-book list of accepted scripture.
- Josephus, [Antiquities 18.16](https://lexundria.com/j_aj/18.16/wst): an ancient outside account of the Sadducees' position on souls and bodies. Report the attribution rather than redescribing them as nihilists.
- [Makkot 23b](https://www.sefaria.org/Makkot.23b?lang2=en&with=all): explicit attribution of the 613-commandment enumeration to Rabbi Simlai.
- Skinner, [Gospel of Thomas](https://www.nasscal.com/e-clavis-christian-apocrypha/gospel-of-thomas/): scholarly research overview and manuscript inventory. The lack of scholarly agreement prevents converting a broad label into a settled compositional history.
