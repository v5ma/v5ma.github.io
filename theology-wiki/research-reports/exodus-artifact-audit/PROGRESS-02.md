# Evidence checkpoint: primary passages and a conditional chronological test

The full Kitchen article has now been recovered: https://www.tyndalebulletin.org/article/32324.pdf . It explicitly reports a Dynasty XIII household list of 79 servants, 45 Asiatics, and nearly forty paired Semitic/Egyptian names. The older recto register separately concerns 76 people who had absconded from state service. Those are distinct document sections and populations, not numbers to combine. The author's exact BCE dates reflect his 1956 chronology. Hayes's complete 1955 edition and the original museum record still require collation to resolve differing published totals. The Egyptian/Semitic name comparison does not identify the people as Israelites or as named biblical individuals.

A real rabbinic connection behind the uploaded narrative was verified. Sanhedrin 92b attributes to Rav the identification of Ezekiel's revived dead with Ephraimites who calculated the departure incorrectly, while also preserving competing interpretations: https://steinsaltz-center.org/portal/library/Talmud/Sanhedrin/chapter/92.b . Shemot Rabbah 20:11 explicitly gives thirty years and relates the avoided route to the scattered bones; the Hebrew and English primary passage is accessible within https://voices.sefaria.org/sheets/535446 . Only that embedded passage, not the compiler's unrelated political commentary, is used. Neither witness names Ahmose or Avaris.

A new internal comparison follows. If the preferred variant uses an illustrative 1555 BCE Moses departure, Exodus 7:7's age eighty gives a birth at 1635 BCE. Acts 7's forty-year life-stage scheme gives flight at 1595 BCE. Importing Shemot Rabbah's thirty-year earlier Ephraim departure would place it at 1585 BCE, ten years after that flight and within the hypothetical Midian interval. This is an internally consistent combination of literary intervals, not an independently dated event. It does not support identifying that early departure with Ahmose's later conquest. The video's hundred-year claim is a different interval again.

The Ahmose son of Ebana inscription in Lichtheim's reproduced translation describes repeated combat at Avaris and captives given as slaves, not a bloodless entry into a capital emptied by Ephraimite officers: https://ancientegyptonline.co.uk/ahmoseebana/ . Its military and captivity evidence is valuable without the added tribal identification.

The Seqenenre CT study establishes severe perimortem head injuries. Capture, restraint and a battlefield reconstruction are the authors' interpretation; the paper explicitly says the exact place of death is unknown. The original report's stronger formulations should be qualified, not used as proof of a Sea-of-Reeds location: https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/medicine/articles/10.3389/fmed.2021.637527/full .
