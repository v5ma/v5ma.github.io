# Exodus artifacts, Ephraim and the competing Moses chronologies

This audit evaluates the uploaded Dynasty of Slaves narrative and the user-supplied Ryan Henry artifact transcript. It distinguishes the supplied claims, the objects or texts actually consulted, the scholars' interpretations and new conditional inferences. It does not rewrite the earlier Theology Wiki report or treat a plausible background as an identification of Moses. The source identifiers below resolve in [SOURCES.md](SOURCES.md).

## What becomes stronger

The strongest new contribution to the preferred pre-Ahmose model is evidence that people of West Asian background were already present in Egyptian servitude before Ahmose's conquest. An argument for early coercion therefore need not begin by borrowing a post-conquest enslavement story that places Moses's birth after the event in which the preferred model already requires him to be elderly.

Papyrus Brooklyn 35.1446 is real. Kitchen's 1956-57 discussion of Hayes's edition distinguishes an older state-service register from a reused Dynasty XIII household list. He reports 79 household servants, 45 Asiatics, and nearly forty paired Semitic and Egyptian names. The older register separately records people pursued for leaving state service. These are different documentary layers, not one list of runaway Hebrews. Kitchen's precise BCE assignments belong to his historical scheme; a modern chronological audit should not simply adopt them as fixed measurements. [brooklyn-kitchen]

The video's 95/30/9 formula is not verified by that source. The primary museum page was not retrievable, and Hayes's complete edition and Albright's full name study remain to be collated. A count may refer to a different subset of a damaged document, but that possibility cannot be declared the explanation without checking. Shared Northwest Semitic names, including forms later found in the Bible, do not uniquely identify an ethnic Israelite group or a particular biblical bearer. [brooklyn-museum; albright-names]

The useful synthesis is therefore a social possibility, not a solved genealogy. A Levantine-derived population can contain dependents, servants, merchants and elite families; the origin of a ruling dynasty does not confer one uniform status on everyone with a similar cultural background. Avaris's isotope study supplies separate evidence of mobility, including many nonlocal individuals in sampled cemeteries. That sample is not a census and does not identify Israelites. Combining these sources suggests a better question about differentiated social positions, not proof that the Brooklyn household belonged to the Avaris palace. [avaris-isotopes]

## Brickmaking: real evidence in a later period

The name rendered as Reay in the transcript most likely refers to Rekhmire. The Met documents a modern facsimile of the ancient brickmaking scene; its ancient setting is Dynasty XVIII, during Thutmose III and early Amenhotep II. Flora Brooke Anthony's analysis identifies foreign captives at work and relates the caption to building for Amun at Thebes. The labor comparison is substantive. The scene does not name Moses or an Israelite workforce. [rekhmire-met; rekhmire-anthony]

For a departure before Ahmose, its chronological role must be marked as later comparative evidence. Similar production methods and coercive labor can illuminate a proposed earlier setting without turning a later tomb image into a contemporary record of that event. The date of the modern facsimile must also remain distinct from the original scene's date.

## Ipuwer: copy date, composition and remembered catastrophe

The Admonitions of Ipuwer survives on Papyrus Leiden I 344 recto. The scholarly TLA record assigns the surviving copy to probably late Dynasty XIX and identifies a literary dialogue in Middle Egyptian. Rosell's research note distinguishes the New Kingdom manuscript from an earlier, proposed Middle Kingdom composition. The river/blood clause is present in the digital transliteration, so the comparison does not originate solely in the video's paraphrase. [ipuwer-tla; ipuwer-rosell; ipuwer-oraec]

However, a physical manuscript date, a proposed date of composition and a date for the events represented are not interchangeable. This is not an independently dated eyewitness diary of the ten Exodus plagues. No recovered evidence supports the video's clinical comparison with COVID-19. That assertion requires evidence quite different from a poetic description of calamity.

The productive use for the Wiki is to investigate a repertoire of Egyptian catastrophe language and its possible later reception. One text's image of social and cosmic disorder can be compared with another without asserting the same catastrophe. Direct borrowing, shared conventions and historical memory are different explanations to test. This audit has not established one of them as the full explanation.

## Amenhotep II: different inscriptions and a different pharaoh hypothesis

The Elephantine stela of Amenhotep II is an actual monument, with a related text at Amada. It should not be combined indiscriminately with the Memphis and Karnak campaign records. The CEFB reconstruction distinguishes campaigns in regnal years 3, 7 and 9. The video's claim of only two campaigns is therefore not an uncontested factual premise. Co-regency and numbering proposals require their own source comparison. [amenhotep-khm; amenhotep-ucl; amenhotep-cefb]

Foreign raids and captive-taking are real historical subjects. But a smaller campaign count than a predecessor's does not establish a destroyed pursuit army. Nor does a captive list identify the purpose as replacing escaped Hebrews. To establish that explanation one would need an independently supported prior loss, relative timing and a connection between that loss and the policy, rather than reasoning backward from the hypothesis to its proposed confirmation.

Amenhotep II belongs after Ahmose in Dynasty XVIII, whereas Seqenenre belongs to the earlier Theban-Hyksos conflict. Exact reign years vary across modern chronologies, but those differences do not make the two men one person. The Amenhotep-II Exodus theory must be compared as a rival chronology, not recruited as direct confirmation of the preferred Seqenenre identification.

## Shasu-yhw: an important name without a forty-year calendar

Kennedy's peer-reviewed 2019 study documents two Soleb witnesses and a later parallel at Amara West. Soleb belongs to Amenhotep III in the fourteenth century BCE; the Amara West parallel belongs to a later, early thirteenth-century context and may reuse earlier list material. A pillar and a wall at one temple are not two independent countries observing the same wandering group. [soleb-kennedy]

Kennedy argues that the yhw-associated Shasu should be connected with Yahweh-worshipping Israelites. Adrom and Mueller's chapter questions the certainty of several linguistic, geographical and ethnic steps in such identifications. Their disagreement concerns what the inscriptions permit us to conclude; it does not erase the inscriptions themselves. The signs are not a statement naming Moses, an escape from Egypt or forty years in the wilderness. [soleb-adrom]

The phrase tent dwellers, even when securely attached to a Shasu source, is not uniquely diagnostic of one biblical group. In the Wiki's Kenite-Midianite inquiry, it would also be circular to assume that no non-Israelite group could have known Yahweh before evaluating possible transmission to Israel. The valuable lead is early yhw-associated geography or religious history. A specific migration and its duration need additional evidence.

## A genuine Ephraim tradition, but not the video's military chronology

The uploaded account has a real textual connection beneath its much larger reconstruction. Sanhedrin 92b attributes to Rav an identification of Ezekiel's revived dead with Ephraimites who miscalculated the time to leave Egypt; it quotes the Chronicles episode. The surrounding discussion preserves other identifications and an interpretation of the vision as a parable. Shemot Rabbah 20:11 relates the avoided route to the scattered bones and gives an interval of thirty years. These are genuine reception witnesses, not a new modern invention. [sanhedrin; shemot]

They do not name Ahmose, Avaris, a Hyksos chariot corps, a security briefing or a policy to enslave the relatives of deserters. The century asserted in the video also differs from the thirty-year interval in the consulted midrash. The narrative's reference to 1 Corinthians 7:22 should be corrected to the relevant 1 Chronicles 7 passage; the verse does not supply the entire military history attached to it.

Ezekiel 37 itself identifies the bones with the whole house of Israel and connects Joseph/Ephraim with Judah in its restoration vision. It does not identify Gath as an excavated battlefield or describe a modern archaeological inspection. The Wiki should link the biblical passage to its later interpretations, then clearly label the Ahmose-Ephraim connection as a further modern proposal. [ezekiel37]

There is also Egyptian evidence against presenting the taking of Avaris as an unopposed walk-in. Ahmose son of Ebana's autobiography, in the consulted Lichtheim translation, describes repeated fighting at and around the city's waters, followed by people taken as captives and granted as slaves. It then describes the siege of Sharuhen. This is powerful evidence for warfare and captivity, but it neither names Ephraim nor explains captivity as a response to his tribe's alleged betrayal. [ahmose-ebana]

The hippopotamus complaint likewise has an identifiable witness: the Tale of Apophis and Seqenenra in Papyrus Sallier I. The British Museum classifies the surviving manuscript as a New Kingdom literary text, Dynasty XIX(?). That is evidence for later representation of the rivalry, not a contemporary diplomatic document proving the war's immediate trigger. [sallier]

## A timeline that keeps the alternatives separate

In the preferred variant, choose 1555 BCE only as an illustrative confrontation/departure anchor. Exodus 7:7's age eighty yields a birth around 1635 BCE. Adding Acts 7's forty-year life-stage scheme yields flight around 1595 BCE. Those numbers are calculated from selected assumptions, not dates read from an Egyptian inscription. [exodus7; acts7]

An additional conditional comparison is possible without the video's Ahmose theory. If the thirty-year Ephraim interval is applied to that same 1555 anchor, it gives 1585 BCE: ten years after the hypothetical flight of Moses and within his proposed Midian interval. This is internally consistent arithmetic. It does not establish that an Ephraimite departure actually occurred in 1585 or that the different textual traditions preserve one original calendar.

In the uploaded Dynasty of Slaves variant, Ahmose first takes Avaris, then enslaves the remaining population, and Moses is born afterward. That cannot also be the story in which Moses is already eighty before the conquest. Both variants can be studied, but combining them requires changing a major premise, not simply describing both as end-Hyksos.

The preferred variant has its own geographical obligation. An Avaris foster household belongs to the northern ruling setting; Seqenenre was a southern Theban king. A reconstruction in which the latter pursues people associated with the former must supply a real political or military connection. It cannot assume that he automatically commanded the northern court and its labor system throughout Moses's life.

## What the mummy actually constrains

The Seqenenre CT study documents severe perimortem craniofacial injuries. Restraint, capture, the sequence of blows and the proposed battlefield scenario are the authors' reconstructions. They explicitly say that the exact death location is unknown. Earlier wording that turns a plausible anti-Hyksos battle reconstruction into a completely established event should be qualified. [seqenenre-ct]

A Sea-of-Reeds hypothesis must explain the violent trauma and recovery of the body, not ignore them. The presence of a mummy does not by itself exclude every conceivable watery battle scenario; equally, it does not supply such a scenario. Adding a marsh, a pursuit or Moses to the reconstruction is an extra hypothesis that needs independent support.

## How the story should change

The emerging model should no longer depend on all West Asiatic people having one collective status. Earlier records of servitude and later evidence of royal access can coexist within a socially differentiated world. This improves contextual plausibility while leaving specific adoption, escape, kinship and leadership claims open.

The strongest pieces now serve different roles. Brooklyn bears on early servitude and bicultural names. Avaris research bears on mobility and elite integration. The battle inscription bears on conquest and captives. Rekhmire illustrates later labor. Soleb bears on an early yhw-associated name. Ipuwer and the Ephraim traditions bear on the history of literary and religious memory. None should be counted as if it independently dates the same departure.

The next useful additions are a collation of the Brooklyn name list and legal terms; object-specific comparison of the Amenhotep inscriptions; precise mapping of political control during the proposed Moses life stages; and a reception dossier separating Chronicles, Ezekiel, the rabbinic interpretations and the modern Ephraim-Ahmos argument. The genuine contextual support should be retained, and the still-missing identifying links should stay visible. The better question is which combination of facts one chronology can actually accommodate, not how many striking descriptions can be placed beside Exodus.
