This edition opens a 148-passage concordance on September 17, 2026. It is integrated through substantial additions to the existing First Beast and Antichrist-as-conduct articles, whose main-reader search and listening transcripts are rebuilt from canonical source. The previously pending Butler reception note and X/shared-imagery additions were merged first, as pull requests 180 and 179 respectively.

[Open the concordance](index.html). [Read all passage rows without the interface](PASSAGES.md). The canonical inputs are build_data.py, build_dossiers.py, first-beast-supplement.md and conduct-supplement.md. The two Python programs export passages.json and dossiers.json deterministically. build.cjs regenerates index.html and PASSAGES.md without network access. reader.js adds local filters; all records are present in the static HTML and remain readable without JavaScript. Nothing is sent to a server by the reader. No political leader is scored, ranked or assigned a total of matching verses.

The selection contains 128 individually identified verses from the 66-book biblical base and 20 individually numbered sections from a wider corpus. These are not 148 asserted matches to Trump, nor all the verses in Revelation. Passages about the Lamb, communities, judgment, discernment and restoration remain distinct from adversary passages. Every row retains the actual textual subject and an unassigned status unless a documentary comparison is linked. Inclusion of an ethical contrast is intentional, not an assertion that every selected verse predicts an adversary.

The wider corpus contains Didache 16:3-8 in Kirsopp Lake's section numbering, ten sections of Ascension of Isaiah 4 in R. H. Charles's translation, two verses in the fourth-Ezra core of 2 Esdras, and two verses of 1 Enoch. Numbering and translation are explicit; no collation of manuscripts in their original languages was undertaken. Translations sharing a motif are not automatically independent predictions.

Canon is relative to a tradition. The Ethiopian Orthodox Church's own account lists Enoch in its canon and discusses other preserved works: https://www.ethiopianorthodox.org/english/canonical/books.html. The term wider corpus here means outside this edition's 66-book indexing baseline. It does not assert that one council removed all the selected works. The base uses the World English Bible; the Ezekiel selection uses the linked King James text because direct access to the selected eBible chapter failed. The 2 Esdras selection uses the World English Bible Classic. These editions are identified rather than silently harmonized.

Thirteen documentary dossiers distinguish contemporary statements and reception, historical accusations, official institutional documents and an Egyptian museum object. A historical source showing that somebody accused Napoleon, Reagan, Clinton or Obama is not automatically evidence of the accusation's truth. The Hitler dossier uses an actual state-official oath rather than adding an unsourced claim about a particular Antichrist accuser. The Russia-policy dossier records two dated statements without a comparative moral judgment or an exhaustive policy history.

The author's recurrence proposal is recorded as a typological hypothesis. The articles distinguish an individual, a public persona, an institution, a recurring form and a proposed final fulfillment. First John and Second Thessalonians provide relevant present-and-future language; the Elijah/John passages give a role-and-identity comparison. These textual observations neither endorse a political identification nor dismiss typology merely because a motif appears more than once. Missed dated forecasts are not silently converted to successful predecessor predictions.

The Butler case distinguishes the wound, account of survival, audience response, religious interpretation and later mission statement. The media case distinguishes creators, upstream publishers, account approval and official amplification. Each comparison retains agent and source scope. A representation of power, a claim of power and an implemented rule require different evidence.

No original public chat, original forecast record, corpus snapshot, source text, recorded audio asset or private repository is overwritten. The one-time integration helper appends the reviewed supplements to two known canonical article bodies after verifying their pre-existing Git blob identities. It records a preservation receipt and updates only their metadata summaries/dates and the reader edition identifier. The main build derives public article files, indexes and listening transcripts. A repeat integration must be byte-idempotent. The original article prefixes remain available in Git history and are tested against their pre-integration hashes.

Run python theology-wiki/scripture-concordance/build_data.py and python theology-wiki/scripture-concordance/build_dossiers.py, then node theology-wiki/scripture-concordance/build.cjs, followed by node --test theology-wiki/scripture-concordance/concordance.test.cjs. INTEGRATED=1 also verifies the actual canonical articles, generated main-reader pages, searchability and preservation. Existing main-wiki and event/corpus suites remain independent gates. Browser tests exercise the new real HTTP page, not merely a mocked data view. Passing interface or build checks do not certify a historical or theological conclusion. Release and hosted-verification evidence is recorded separately in the pull request.
