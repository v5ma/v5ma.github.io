## Current working model in the Theology Wiki

The favored experimental chronology is an end-of-Hyksos / late Second Intermediate Period setting, not the Hatshepsut/Senenmut or Amenhotep II 1446 BCE model. If the initiating confrontation is provisionally placed near Seqenenre Tao's violent death in the mid-16th century BCE, Exodus 7:7 makes Moses about eighty at confrontation and therefore suggests a birth around c. 1635 BCE. Acts 7:23 and 7:30 supply the separate 40 + 40 life-stage scheme; that is a later Christian chronological interpretation and should not be silently presented as an Egyptian datum.

This moves Moses's hypothesized upbringing to Second Intermediate Period Avaris. The foster mother, if historical, must belong to an early/middle Hyksos royal generation rather than Hatshepsut or the early New Kingdom. Senenmut should be retained only as a structural comparison and rejected as an identity candidate in this chronology.

The deep-research report preserved in the conversation/library is titled "Hyksos Avaris and the Moses Prosopography: A Second Intermediate Period Investigation." Its central conclusion is deliberately tiered: Avaris supplies a real archaeological social world in which a Levantine-origin population could rise to elite/ruling status and a Levantine child could plausibly be raised in an Egyptianized elite household, but archaeology does not presently demonstrate Moses or Israelites at Avaris.

## High-confidence historical scaffold

Avaris/Tell el-Dab'a was the center of a large population with Levantine origins that became deeply Egyptianized over generations. The Fifteenth Dynasty Hyksos rulers emerged from this eastern-Delta milieu. The court included identifiable royal people such as Khyan and his eldest king's son Yanassi. Apepi later ruled from Avaris while the Theban dynasty expanded north.

Seqenenre Tao died violently in the anti-Hyksos conflict. CT work documents catastrophic craniofacial weapon trauma and evidence compatible with restraint and death away from the normal royal mortuary setting. This makes his death genuinely relevant to the historical war horizon but contradicts a simple claim that he merely drowned untouched. Any Sea-of-Reeds association would require a battle/disruption in a watery or marsh theatre followed by weapon death, body recovery and embalming. That reconciliation is speculative and is not presently demonstrated.

Kamose's stelae document the actual Theban-Hyksos war and communications involving Apepi and Kush. Ahmose I subsequently completed the reconquest. Ahmose son of Ibana supplies a contemporary/near-contemporary military biography for the conquest horizon. These are historical anchors; adding Moses to them is the hypothesis.

Manetho, as preserved especially through Josephus, contains a late memory with a striking structural sequence: Avaris, foreign/shepherd rulers, war with Theban rulers, mass departure with families and possessions, wilderness and movement toward the Levant. Josephus explicitly identifies the expelled people with Jewish ancestors. This is important evidence for an ancient Hyksos-Israel association, but it is late, mediated and apologetically interested, not a contemporary Hyksos inscription.

## Royal-woman / Moses prosopography

The current target is a Moses candidate born roughly 1660-1630 BCE, raised in an elite or royal Avaris environment, with a court career that ends/disappears roughly 1620-1590 BCE under a strict Acts 40 + 40 model. A looser Exodus-only model requires age about eighty at confrontation but need not force exact forty-year Egyptian and Midian stages.

Yanassi remains useful because he is the right kind of person in the right social world and survives only fragmentarily, but his monument calls him the king's eldest son. There is no evidence allowing us to convert that into an adopted Hebrew child. Treat "Yanassi = Moses" as low confidence.

Royal women to continue screening: Tati ("King's Wife," chronology debated, potentially useful early household context); Tani and Ziwat ("King's Sister," probably late); Herit/Herti ("King's Daughter," strong titular parallel but apparently linked to Apepi and likely too late for a Moses born around 1640). An otherwise unattested royal daughter of the required earlier generation is plausible as a preservation inference, not an identified person.

## Evidence from the recent Exodus-evidence transcript

A second uploaded transcript promoted the 1446 BCE / Amenhotep II / Hatshepsut / Senenmut model. It is valuable mainly as a list of artifacts to audit, not as support for the Wiki's favored chronology.

Brooklyn Papyrus 35.1446 is important because it belongs to the late Middle Kingdom / Second Intermediate Period horizon and documents household dependents/servants with many West Semitic names. It is evidence that people of Levantine linguistic background could be present in Egyptian servile households before the New Kingdom. It does not label those people Hebrews or Israelites, and claims that nine names are specifically Hebrew require name-by-name philological verification.

Tomb scenes showing mudbrick manufacture by foreign captives/workers are genuine Egyptian evidence for brickmaking labor but many famous examples are New Kingdom and therefore demonstrate a labor practice, not the date or identity of the Exodus.

The Ipuwer/Admonitions text is real but should not be treated as a contemporary eyewitness record of the ten plagues. Composition/manuscript dating and literary genre make direct Exodus identification weak.

The Soleb/Amarah-West "Shasu of yhw" material is important evidence for a toponym/divine-name-like yhw associated with Shasu in the southern Transjordan/Seir-Edom sphere during the New Kingdom. It is highly relevant to the Wiki's Kenite/Midian/Yahweh-origin research, but it does not identify those Shasu as the Exodus Israelites or place Moses in Egypt at that date.

Amenhotep II's campaign record and the Senenmut/Hatshepsut reconstruction belong to a competing fifteenth-century Exodus model and cannot be imported into the Wiki's c. 1550 BCE model as though they were mutually compatible.

## Evidence from the recent Ephraim / Avaris transcript

The uploaded transcript "Pasted text(20260924-000029).txt" proposes a much more elaborate reconstruction: Ephraimites as the Hyksos military/administrative elite, a premature armed breakout from Avaris, their destruction near Gath, Ahmose treating remaining Hebrews as a fifth column and reducing them to forced labor, and later links to Exodus 13 and Ezekiel 37.

Separate its real textual components from its historical additions.

1 Chronicles 7:20-27 really contains an Ephraim genealogy in which Ezer and Elead are killed by men of Gath over livestock; Ephraim mourns and a later son is named Beriah in connection with disaster. Later Jewish traditions do contain a motif of Ephraimites leaving Egypt prematurely and being killed. These traditions are worth tracing textually and dating carefully.

Exodus 13:17 really says the Israelites were not led by the road through Philistine territory because encountering war might cause them to return to Egypt.

Ezekiel 37 really contains the valley of dry bones and later in the same chapter the two-stick sign joining Judah with Joseph/Ephraim. The chapter itself explains the dry bones as "the whole house of Israel" in an exile/restoration oracle. Identifying those bones specifically as Ephraimite casualties at Gath is an additional interpretation, not the explicit referent of the text.

No evidence currently recovered establishes that Ephraim was the Hyksos chariot corps, military backbone, intelligence elite or ruling class at Avaris; that Ephraimite troops deserted Avaris during Ahmose's attack; that Ahmose enslaved remaining Hebrews because of that defection; or that the Gath deaths happened in c. 1550 BCE. These are hypotheses supplied by the modern reconstruction and should not be promoted to archaeological facts.

The transcript also mistakenly cites "1 Corinthians 7:22" for Ephraim mourning; the relevant passage is 1 Chronicles 7:22-23. Preserve that correction.

Claims that Gath's attackers were Rephaim/giants or early Sea Peoples, that "taking cattle" is military shorthand for a scorched-earth resupply raid, that the bodies remained unburied along the later Exodus route, and that Ezekiel was viewing the literal Gath battlefield are presently unsupported and require independent evidence.

The transcript's proposed Ahmose military sequence also needs auditing against primary Egyptian records. Ahmose son of Ibana does record fighting at Avaris and later Sharuhen, but the detailed modern narrative about Ephraimite chariot defection, an unguarded eastern escape corridor, Ahmose reacting by seizing Tjaru specifically because of that defection, and systematic conversion of Hebrews into a security labor force is not established by that autobiography.

The archaeology of Avaris does document major political and functional changes across the Hyksos-New Kingdom transition. Do not translate "changed/abandoned/reused settlement sectors" automatically into "elite Hebrews were stripped of status and made slaves." That inference requires direct evidence.

The transcript's Levite argument also needs philological care. Moses is widely recognized as compatible with an Egyptian ms/mose name element. Phinehas has often been discussed as Egyptian in origin. "Merari" should not be casually called an Egyptian high-status title without a source. Egyptian-looking names among Levites can support a possible Egyptian cultural memory but do not establish that the tribe of Levi formed an Egyptian priestly intelligence/administrative class.

## Important chronology tension to preserve

1 Kings 6:1 gives 480 years from the Exodus to Solomon's fourth year. With conventional Solomon chronology this points to a fifteenth-century BCE Exodus and therefore conflicts with a c. 1550 BCE end-Hyksos Exodus. The Wiki's favored model must explicitly explain this cost rather than hiding it: 480 may be schematic, an anchor date may need revision, or another chronological tradition may be preferred.

Tell el-Dab'a chronology itself is not settled to a decade. The deep-research report notes a substantial radiocarbon versus archaeological-synchronism tension, potentially on the order of many decades. This is large enough to affect candidate matching and should be treated as uncertainty, not tuned to force a preferred person into the timeline.

## Continuity rule

Do not revert to Senenmut merely because his biography is richer. The current hypothesis deliberately asks whether a much more poorly documented Avaris court can explain why a Moses-like elite figure leaves almost no Egyptian dossier.

Do not conflate "Semitic," "Asiatic," "Hyksos," "Hebrew," "Israelite" and "Ephraimite." They overlap only when independent evidence establishes the link.

Use four evidence tiers in future Wiki prose: securely attested fact; plausible historical background; suggestive parallel; unsupported/speculative identification.

The goal is not to prove the hypothesis by accumulation of similarities. The goal is to make it increasingly falsifiable.
