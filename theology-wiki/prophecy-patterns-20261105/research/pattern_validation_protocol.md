# Testing recurring prophetic patterns without demanding perfect prediction

Research appendix prepared November 5, 2026. This is a proposed research design, a limited audit of the existing register, and a reproducible synthetic demonstration. It is not a completed empirical validation or falsification of the theological framework.

## The strongest claim worth testing

Micah's correction in the source conversation is sound: a useful historical model need not predict every event. A recurring pattern can contain real predictive information while its timing, expression, and outcome remain uncertain. Human action, including action influenced by a prophecy, can be part of the mechanism predicted. Neither randomness nor self-fulfillment automatically makes a forecast false.

The empirically testable version is:

**A fixed interpretation of specified passages identifies a recurring configuration of institutions, incentives, rhetoric, and vulnerability that predicts specified later outcomes better than relevant comparison models.**

This differs from requiring inevitability, assuming a perfect timetable, or treating every historical accusation as a successful prediction. It also avoids reducing the entire theology to a statistical exercise. A moral interpretation, a historical causal account, a forecast, and an account of divine meaning can coexist, but their evidential requirements differ.

The source is the [shared Gemini conversation](https://gemini.google.com/share/ef34a9aab77b). Gemini's later claims of a flawless historical algorithm were assertions, not results of a fitted model or a validation study.

## Five claims that should not be collapsed

1. **Textual claim:** The proposed structure is defensibly present in the ancient passage. Establish wording, translation, composition history, literary context, and the distinction between features stated by the text and added by the interpreter.
2. **Recurrence claim:** Independently documented historical episodes instantiate the same fixed structure. This needs both positive cases and comparable noncases.
3. **Predictive claim:** Knowing the structure improves forecasts of later outcomes. This requires information available before the forecast cutoff and evaluation on data not used to choose the structure.
4. **Mechanism claim:** The proposed chain actually helps produce the outcome. Temporal prediction alone is insufficient to establish causation.
5. **Theological claim:** The pattern has divine significance, or a person represents its final fulfillment. The preceding findings can bear on such an argument without determining it automatically.

A retrospective test is possible when an authentic old prediction is sufficiently specific and its original scope is recoverable. Preregistration is not a requirement that ancient authors should somehow have used a modern registry. It is a protection against changing today's interpretation after seeing today's evidence. A newly reconstructed historical mapping is exploratory until tested beyond the cases that shaped it. [Nosek and colleagues explain the distinction between discovery and confirmation](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5856500/).

## Operationalize three separate research programs

### A. Conquest, violence, scarcity, and mortality

**Candidate hypothesis:** In a specified population of armed-conflict episodes, an independently coded combination of moralized conquest rhetoric and civilian-system vulnerability predicts later food-access deterioration and noncombat mortality beyond what ordinary conflict-risk indicators predict.

Use separate measurements:

- **Legitimating rhetoric:** A dated statement from an actor with relevant authority, coded for an explicit justification of territorial expansion or coercion as morally necessary. Merely finding the word peace, victory, or justice is not sufficient. Preserve quotations and dates. Coders should not know later outcomes where masking is feasible.
- **Violence:** Use an external conflict definition, not a list chosen because it resembles Revelation. UCDP's state-based armed-conflict threshold is at least 25 battle-related deaths in a calendar year under its specified party/incompatibility rules; its war category begins at 1,000. The [versioned codebook](https://ucdp.uu.se/downloads/ucdpprio/ucdp-prio-acd-261.pdf) also distinguishes annual and cumulative intensity.
- **Scarcity:** Choose one primary endpoint before scoring, such as a defined change in a consistent food-access measure. A price rise, reduced harvest, acute food insecurity, and area-level famine are distinct outcomes. Use the [IPC classification protocols](https://www.ipcinfo.org/ipc-manual-interactive) rather than treating famine as a loose synonym for hardship.
- **Mortality:** Use excess noncombat mortality or another predeclared endpoint. Counting battle deaths once as the red stage and again as the pale stage creates a partly automatic match. A rise in deaths alone does not identify the cause of that rise. An IPC famine classification already incorporates mortality evidence, so it cannot be treated as an independent forecast of that same contemporaneous mortality; distinguish earlier food-access measurements from later deaths.

Prespecify whether the claim concerns a strict order, overlapping causal stages, or simply co-occurrence. Do not let an order test become an unordered resemblance test when a case fails. If timing is uncertain, retain intervals and count order as unresolved where intervals overlap. A multistate event-history model can allow transitions, reversals, skipped stages, and interventions; it need not impose an inevitable four-step procession.

**Denominator and timing:** For forecasting from conflict onset, enroll every qualifying onset, not only wars later known to be devastating. For forecasting after an already observed severe escalation, define that later observation as the forecast origin and use only information then available. Selecting eventual severe wars and then claiming to predict their earlier escalation would leak outcome information.

**Possible disconfirmation:** No added forecast skill; reversed or missing stages more common than the stated claim allows; equivalent performance from ordinary war-and-vulnerability indicators; or systematic failure in a held-out region or period. Aid, imports, repairs, diplomacy, and resilient institutions must be recorded as possible interruptions, not redescribed as hidden confirmation.

### B. Domination, institutional deterioration, and corrective leadership

Replace an unmeasurable progression toward increasingly stupid rulers with named observable quantities: executive constraints, corruption controls, service delivery, leadership turnover, concentration of coercion, or another explicitly defended construct. No one of these is identical to intelligence or moral worth.

**Candidate hypothesis:** Under stated institutional conditions, rising concentration of power and declining corrective feedback increase the risk of later institutional breakdown. A separate hypothesis asks whether severe breakdown increases or decreases the chance of sustained improvement.

Compare four outcomes, rather than retaining only the attractive one: recovery, continuing dysfunction, replacement by another coercive order, and prolonged fragmentation. Include successful reform without catastrophe. A model that can forecast all four with appropriate probabilities is stronger than a narrative that interprets every breakdown as the prelude to renewal.

The claim of permanent enlightened leadership cannot be verified by a finite observation period. A modest empirical counterpart could assess improvement sustained for five or ten years, with subsequent relapse recorded. It must be labeled a bounded proxy, not quietly substituted for the stronger theological claim. [V-Dem](https://www.v-dem.net/data/the-v-dem-dataset/) offers institutional indicators and uncertainty estimates; it does not measure a ruler's spiritual status.

### C. Truthful speech, withdrawal of obedience, and political change

**Candidate hypothesis:** Independently corroborated disclosures or public truth claims, under specified social conditions, contribute to changes in belief and coordination that precede loss of support for a coercive institution.

Do not define a truthful speaker as whoever later wins. Code factual accuracy from evidence independent of political success, then code audience reach, credibility, organizational infrastructure, defections, repression, and institutional outcomes separately. Compare successful disclosures with suppressed or ignored ones. A leader's removal through elections, defections, strikes, negotiated transitions, military defeat, and assassination are different mechanisms.

[NAVCO's campaign data](https://doi.org/10.7910/DVN/MHOXDV) can help frame comparative research on nonviolent and violent resistance. It cannot by itself test words alone, the truth of a message, or the identity of Christ. Nonviolent mobilization involves organization and material action as well as speech. Case-level process tracing would be needed to test the proposed information-to-obedience mechanism.

**Possible disconfirmation:** Accuracy and exposure do not precede defections; changes in coercive or economic conditions account for the transition; accurate warnings frequently fail under the claimed conditions; or information helps a new tyrant replace an old one. Such findings can revise a sociological mechanism without exhausting the theological meaning of the sword-from-the-mouth imagery.

## The comparison must be harder than random coincidence

### A separate test for self-fulfilling effects

Separate three records: when a statement was made, who encountered it, and what actions they took before the outcome. The relevant questions differ: did the forecast occur, did exposure help cause its occurrence, and did the original statement anticipate that feedback? Total control of history is not required for a belief to be a contributing cause. Adversaries can react to one another and jointly produce an outcome no one fully controls.

Comparing exposed and less-exposed settings may help, but exposure is not random. People drawn to a narrative may already have different strategic interests, institutions, and risks. A causal claim therefore needs a defensible identification strategy, such as a credible natural experiment or unusually strong process evidence, not just a correlation between belief and conflict. Do not run interventions that promote conflict to test the theory.

If the same structure appears where transmission of the specific narrative is unlikely, that supports independent recurrence more than repetition in communities consciously enacting it. Conversely, narrative enactment can still show genuine causal power. Neither finding, by itself, establishes or rules out divine meaning. If the evidence cannot separate independent foresight from enactment, report that non-identifiability rather than claiming the alternatives have been disproved.

### A ladder of baselines

Use a ladder of comparison models:

1. **Base rate:** How often does the outcome happen in the stated population and time horizon without using the motif?
2. **Persistence:** Predict continuation of recent trends or the previous state.
3. **Ordinary causal knowledge:** Use conflict intensity, pre-existing food insecurity, dependence on vulnerable imports, state capacity, climatic stress, and other justified predictors available at the cutoff.
4. **Text-derived model:** Add the fixed prophetic features or structure, with the same access to ordinary predictors and comparable tuning opportunities.
5. **Specificity controls:** Compare with preselected alternative narrative templates of similar length and flexibility, and with shuffled or reordered structures using a null that preserves relevant dependence.

Beating the base-rate comparison demonstrates something weaker than beating the ordinary-causal and specificity controls. If the theological schema equals a competent secular account, that may support its descriptive insight. It does not show its historical insight was worthless, but it does not establish uniquely exceptional predictive power either.

The primary inferential question should not be whether war and famine are independent. They are connected by the hypothesis itself and by ordinary mechanisms. A useful null already allows those connections. Randomly permuting every observation would destroy temporal clustering, country differences, and shared shocks, creating an artificially easy opponent. Use matched episodes, appropriate block resampling, or a fitted non-theological baseline instead, and state the assumptions of whichever null is used.

For causal estimates, distinguish pre-existing confounders from mediators. Adjusting away destroyed infrastructure may erase the very war-to-food pathway being studied. For prediction, later destruction cannot be included as if it were known before the forecast.

## A concrete first benchmark

Start with one tractable component, not a universal history model: **Does a fixed escalation-and-vulnerability score improve 12-month food-access forecasts?**

Before looking at test outcomes, freeze:

1. One versioned conflict dataset, a coverage period, and a rule for including all eligible country-periods. Specify border changes and international conflicts.
2. The exact food-access outcome, geographical unit, measurement window, and deterioration threshold. First audit data coverage without inspecting the target relationship. Missing outcomes remain missing; they are not coded as normal conditions.
3. The score's textual basis, feature dictionary, weights or training procedure, and allowable timing tolerance. Label interpretive choices as such.
4. Training years, tuning years, and a later locked test period. Also use a geographically held-out check where the sample allows it. Keep repeated observations from the same conflict episode together.
5. A rolling historical cutoff so each forecast uses only information actually available then. Revised datasets can leak later knowledge; record vintages or explicitly call the result a retrospective hindcast.
6. One primary scoring rule and a minimum useful improvement chosen before outcomes are read. Use the same tuning budget for competitors.
7. An independent coding and outcome-adjudication procedure. Record disagreements, inter-rater agreement, uncertain dates, and source gaps.
8. A fixed evaluation window, transparent exclusions, and a rule for all deviations. Estimate required sample size by simulation under plausible base rates, effect sizes, dependence, and missingness rather than choosing an impressive-looking round number.

The [UCDP download center](https://ucdp.uu.se/downloads/) and [WFP/HDX food-data documentation](https://centre.humdata.org/getting-up-to-speed-wfp-food-data-on-hdx/) offer starting points. Data access does not guarantee compatible geography, timescales, reliable wartime measurement, or enough independent episodes. Those issues are a feasibility gate before fitting the model. The famine endpoint may be too rare for a small first study; choosing food-access deterioration instead would test the weaker endpoint, not validate a famine forecast.

This benchmark is a proposal, not a claim that the necessary joined dataset has already been collected or that a test has passed.

## Mathematical measures that answer the actual question

For a binary outcome y and a forecast probability p, the Brier loss is (p − y)². Average it over every eligible test case. Report the model's loss and the comparison model's loss; Brier skill is 1 − model loss / comparison loss when the denominator is nonzero. Positive skill means improvement on that comparison, not perfection or proof of a divine source. Log loss provides a complementary check and penalizes unjustified certainty. These are [proper scoring rules](https://doi.org/10.1198/016214506000001437), designed to reward honest probabilistic forecasts.

Also report calibration, false alarms, misses, and performance by region and period. Overall accuracy can be misleading when the outcome is rare: forecasting no catastrophe every time may appear highly accurate. Confidence intervals should reflect shared episodes and shocks, rather than pretending every sentence or country-month is independent. If only a few independent cases remain, report the uncertainty rather than manufacture precision.

For recurrence, one can write a fixed feature score S = sum of w_j times f_j, with documented features f_j and frozen weights w_j. Missing evidence needs a separate category or interval; it must not silently reduce the denominator and make every poorly documented case look like a perfect match. An unweighted match count is a description, not automatically an evidential weight.

Bayesian comparison clarifies what accumulating examples can and cannot do: evidence favors a model when the observed data are more probable under it than under a specified competitor. Adding cases that both models predict equally well does not greatly distinguish them. Repeated accusations, copied sources, and the multiple consequences of one war are not independent replications. Models that differ only in their metaphysical interpretation while assigning the same probabilities to observations are not statistically distinguishable by those observations.

Finally, a social tipping point is a proposed property of a system. A p-value threshold is a decision rule about evidence under a null. Calling both a statistical threshold does not demonstrate that accumulating tyranny must generate its own cure. The proposed tipping mechanism needs its own measured variables, functional form, and test.

## Two demonstrations actually run for this appendix

The accompanying Python program uses no historical data and makes no assessment of any person, prophecy, or religion. Its rules and seed are explicit, and it runs with the Python standard library.

**1. Imperfect prediction can be valuable.** In 2,000 synthetic cases, outcomes have probabilities of either 25% or 75% according to an observable signal. The fixed probabilistic model classified 75.75% correctly. Its Brier loss was 0.18375 versus 0.25 for the constant 50% baseline: a 26.5% loss reduction in this simulated sample. The corresponding theoretical reduction is 25%. Almost one quarter of classifications are wrong, yet the model is clearly useful within this constructed system. This illustrates Micah's objection to the perfect-prediction requirement.

**2. Selecting striking matches can manufacture apparent rarity.** Under an intentionally simplified null of 16 independent fair binary features, a fixed candidate has a 3.8406% probability of matching at least 12. If one searches 100 independent candidates and reports the best, the probability that at least one meets that same threshold is 98.0086%. In 20,000 simulations the observed rates were 3.785% for the fixed candidate and 98.185% for the best of 100; a fresh independent holdout returned to 3.745%.

These numbers are not p-values for the Bible. Historical features are neither fair coins nor generally independent, and the actual universe of interpretations is unknown. The demonstration explains why testing the whole selection procedure matters, rather than treating a selected example as if it had been fixed in advance. Where the number of tried interpretations cannot be reconstructed, sensitivity analysis and genuinely new data are more honest than an invented precise probability.

A third small calculation illustrates dependence: in a hypothetical war cohort, suppose food crisis has probability 0.6, mortality has probability 0.7 with food crisis and 0.1 without it. The joint food-crisis-and-mortality probability is 0.42. Multiplying their marginal probabilities as if independent incorrectly gives 0.276. Several related consequences can form a real causal signature without constituting several independent miracles.

## The conclusion a good study could earn

A strong positive result would show that a fixed, defensibly text-derived structure survives blinded coding, predicts beyond credible baselines, generalizes to held-out cases, and retains useful effect sizes after selection and dependence are considered. That would support an empirically informative recurring-pattern interpretation. A weaker but still meaningful result could establish that the text lucidly recognizes an ordinary causal structure without outperforming other competent accounts.

A negative forecast result would challenge the tested operationalization and its claimed predictive advantage. It would not by itself disprove every theological interpretation. Conversely, a successful model would not settle divine authorship, a final Antichrist identity, a unique future culmination, or a permanent cure of political evil.

The best next question is therefore concrete: **What does this fixed interpretation predict that a careful reader using ordinary historical knowledge would be less likely to expect, and what observation would make us revise it?** That question takes the pattern seriously enough to expose it to evidence.
